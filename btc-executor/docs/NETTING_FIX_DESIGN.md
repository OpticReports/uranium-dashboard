### 1.3 Root cause (one mechanism, three phenotypes)

The executor keeps a **per-leg** ledger and **per-leg** reduce-only orders on a venue that holds only the **net**, and nothing re-attributes a venue event that moves the net across zero to the other leg:

- **P1 (INC1):** a dominant-leg stop sized `abs(led.qty)` is resized at trigger to the net and takes the account flat; both absorb doors zero one leg; the other leg is left claiming exposure the venue no longer holds.
- **P2 (INC2):** a maker entry fill that flips the net through zero is invisible to every ledger-sum comparison for up to 4 h; on an opposed book that reads as a sign flip.
- **P3 (latent, pinned RED as gates 3/3b in `test_netting_gates.py`):** the dominant leg's SIGNAL/TIME exit inside an opposite episode → `_close_leg` clamps to the net → venue flat → same orphan as P1; and its mirror, the subordinate exiting first → `close_refused_unbacked` on every poll until something else moves.

Reduce-only did its job in both incidents (nothing naked opened); the divergence guard did its job; the halts were fail-closed and cost taker fees plus a manual resume, not capital.

**Still unverified (no production event log in the repo):** the A/B ordering of the pullback absorb in INC1 (moot for the fix — neither ordering sends an order); the INC2 CANCELLED-door trigger is established by elimination plus the `historicalOrders` row, not by the executor's own log.

## 2. Measurement: what it says, and what it cannot say

`btc-paper-engine/research/netting/results.txt`, chart `leg_overlap.png` (in-sample fixture 2022-02..2026-07, 9,792 4h bars, trade-close basis, cash_apy 0, S4 fee 12 bps conservative).

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Bars opposite-side (both legs held, opposite signs) | **1,210 / 9,792 = 12.36%** (one-leg 76.55%, same-side 5.75%, flat 5.34%) |
| Opposite episodes | **110** in 4.5 y (~25/yr); median 9 bars (36 h), mean 11, max 52 (8.7 d) |
| Per year | 2022 8.4% → 2023 12.7% → 2024 13.9% → 2025 14.8% → 2026 (to Jul) 10.9% |
| Asymmetry | **S3** faces an opposed S4 on **61.5%** of its held bars; 110/190 S3 trades touch an opposite bar. **S4** faces an opposed S3 on 13.3% of its held bars; 65/128 S4 trades |
| Blend log-return share carried by opposite bars (pro-rata) | **−15.6%** at lev 1.0 (−18.9% lev 1.5, −22.5% lev 2.0): opposite bars carried 36.9% of gross log gains and **54.0% of gross log losses** |
| Stricter attributions | any-held-bar-opposite **+18.5%**; last-held-bar-opposite **−44.1%** |

**What it implies for the cost of changing leg coexistence:**

1. Opposite-side periods were net-negative *in-sample as realised*. That is an **attribution of the S3−S4 path, not a counterfactual**: forbidding coexistence changes entries, exits and the equity each leg sizes from, and the three attribution rules disagree by 60 points of sign-flipping share. **The number cannot price any coexistence rule.** Pricing one needs its own replay with charts (CLAUDE.md standing rule) and Casey's sign-off.
2. Any rule touches a large fraction of one leg's life: block S3 and you touch 61.5% of its held bars; block S4 and you displace ~half its trades (65/128) with a late-entry bias.
3. The transition bars (one_leg → opposite), where both incidents actually happened, are not singled out; ~25 episodes/yr is the upper bound on netting events the executor must survive.

Conclusion: **the executor fix must preserve coexistence** (per the strategy constraint), and the strategy question — whether opposite-side coexistence should exist at all — is a separate study that this fix neither answers nor forecloses.

### 3.1 NET-MIRROR (recommended) — why it wins, and its BLOCKING findings verbatim

It is the design the existing venue-record gates already describe: "*a booked opposed book carries exactly one resting reduce-only stop sized to the net … venue net == what the ledger believes the venue holds after every poll*" (`test_netting_gates.py`, RED strict-xfail since task #40). It keeps every reduce-only, adds no third order category (every order stays reduce-only or engine-justified entry-class), books maker fills the moment the venue confirms them, re-attributes netted fills across both legs, and re-opens consumed exposure only toward what the engine reports. Both critiques: `flawed_fixable`; "*What does NOT survive is the design's handling of its own new state … all three are fixable by applying doctrine the file already has*."

The two distinct BLOCKING findings, verbatim (each found by both lenses):

> **[PHANTOM LEDGER, BLOCKING]** _absorb_netted_fill (and the dominant _close_leg path that calls it) CONSUMES the other leg by zeroing qty and CLEARING ITS ENTRY REFS. Under the design's own early booking (_book_maker_fill), a pullback leg can legitimately have qty != 0 while its post-only maker order is still OPEN and partially filled. Clearing entry_cloid/signal_ts on that leg forgets a LIVE resting order: the remainder keeps filling with no ledger handle, branch 2's identity dedupe (mirror.py:2256, `led.entry_cloid and led.signal_ts == pend['signal_ts']`) no longer matches so a SECOND full-size entry is sent, and the resulting surplus is same-signed so _stop_backing's surplus relaxation (mirror.py:2632-2636) reads 'ok' and never halts. Today's code never clears a live maker ref (the FILLED door at 2720-2733 only clears the stopped leg's own ref, which for pullback is already None after _enter_from_fill). This is a design-introduced phantom of the inverse kind: the venue holds what the ledger does not, unstopped.

> **[LIVE RISK, BLOCKING]** Step 4 (_absorb_netted_fill) clears the consumed leg's ENTRY refs unconditionally ("stop_cloid/stop_px/entry refs cleared"). Today that clear exists because the TREND keeps its market entry_cloid for the position's life (mirror.py:2721-2733). For a PULLBACK whose post-only maker order is still OPEN (partially filled), clearing the ref without cancelling the order breaks branch 2's identity dedupe (mirror.py:2256: `if led.entry_cloid and led.signal_ts == pend['signal_ts']: return`) and the next poll re-sends a full-size entry beside the still-resting remainder. The surplus that results passes _stop_backing's same-sign relaxation (mirror.py:2626-2628) so it is never halted, never booked, never stopped. It also contradicts _reestablish's own precondition, which needs that entry_cloid to confirm the venue fill.

> **[PHANTOM LEDGER, BLOCKING]** _reestablish is single-attempt and books `led.qty += sign*filled` from a confirm read, then sets netted_qty = 0 unconditionally. hl.order_status returns {'status': 'UNKNOWN', 'filled_qty': 0.0} on ANY exception (hl.py:498-511) and the codebase treats that as a first-class case everywhere ('a single 429/timeout must never be read as a terminal state', mirror.py:1117-1129). On an UNKNOWN confirm the R order's real fill is booked as 0 and netted_qty is dropped, leaving a REAL unstopped position with no ledger row; the very next poll then runs _enter_from_fill (qty 0, entry_cloid None since consumption cleared it) with filled 0 and CHASES full size at market -> the venue holds 2x the engine's leg, same-signed, so _stop_backing's surplus relaxation says 'ok', a stop for half the size is placed, and the book never halts. Today's chase books its own size by intent (mirror.py:2475), so it does not under-book on a blind read; the design's 'book by fills' rule (I9) is applied without the 'belief is recorded before the confirm' doctrine branch 2 already has (mirror.py:2325-2354).

> **[LIVE RISK, BLOCKING]** _reestablish books `led.qty += sign*filled` from ONE confirm read, is single-attempt, and zeroes netted_qty (remainder dropped as netted_shortfall). An UNKNOWN (hl.order_status returns {'status':'UNKNOWN'} on any exception, hl.py:509-511; _ostat retries once, mirror.py:1117-1129) or transitional OPEN read on a live IOC therefore books 0, drops netted_qty, and leaves qty 0 with the engine still reporting the leg. Next poll branch 1 takes `elif led.qty == 0: _enter_from_fill` (mirror.py:2400-2482) with entry_cloid None -> filled 0 -> missing = full _leg_qty -> market chase -> the venue now holds 2x the leg. The opposite patch (keep netted_qty on UNKNOWN) double-sends within the SAME poll, because _close_leg's cross-leg call and the leg's own branch-1 call both run _reestablish. This is the exact 08-26 'reads down, writes up' shape (hl.py docstring; mirror.py:2340-2350 chose 'belief recorded before the confirm' for entries for this reason).

Both are folded in as **N1** and **N2** (§4.1). One further BLOCKING-class defect that neither NET-MIRROR critique caught but the MINIMAL-PATCH critique found in the identical primitive — **same-sign consumption resurrects a leg after its own stop-out** — applies to NET-MIRROR's `_absorb_netted_fill` verbatim ("*every OTHER leg with qty != 0 is CONSUMED*" when net' == 0), and is folded in as **N8**. The live-lens SERIOUS #6 ("the incident-1 replay narrative is inconsistent … something prevented _close_leg from sending for 3 h") is **void**: §1 shows no 20:00 exit and no blocker existed.

### 3.2 SUB-ACCOUNTS (#2) — the cleanest structure, blocked by inputs we do not have

No BLOCKING findings; both lenses confirm the pinned SDK (v0.24.0) supports `Exchange(vault_address=…)`, `Info.query_sub_accounts`, and per-address reads. It deletes the opposed special-case instead of reasoning about it, and both incidents become ordinary per-leg events. It loses **now** because:

- **Two missing key inputs** (CLAUDE.md: ask, don't analyze around): (a) sub-account creation requires **$100,000 cumulative trading volume** on the master — not knowable from the repo; (b) each sub must be **funded** (spot→perp in the master then perp→sub, or `subAccountSpotTransfer`) — this is precisely "asking Casey to move funds", which the 2026-09-10 standing rule forbids requesting. The design is honest that the executor stays out of transfers, but the option cannot exist without the operator doing one.
- **Cutover as written cannot be executed**: `_resume_locked` refuses to clear VENUE_CHANGED (1691-1701) and `_adopt_venue_locked` refuses under it (1791-1801); the only path is deleting `state.json`, which discards `coverage_live`, the fills/slippage sample and drill history (RAMP_V4 evidence); and with the post-INC2 ledger already zeroed the empty-ledger branch would adopt the new stamp silently, so the promised fence never fires.
- **Master-account blind spot**: aggregate `position()` = sum of subs; a residue in the master (a partial halt IOC — `hl._send` does not detect partial fills — or a UI test order) is invisible to drift, boot reconcile and the halt forever.
- **Read-routing hazard**: any un-routed `order_status` reads `unknownOid` → CANCELLED, an *affirmative* false terminal (`hl.py:509-511`), which `_verify_stop_refs`/`_halt_locked` act on.
- The halt stays **book-wide** (a P-only race liquidates a healthy T at taker and re-chases it on resume); margin no longer nets (per-sub liquidation with "a few hundred dollars" of margin is a real scenario); `vaultAddress` acceptance by the master's agent is unverified live; largest change (~900 lines).

All fixable on paper, but none of it can be started before Casey answers the two questions in §6. The NET-MIRROR r2 work is not wasted if SUB-ACCOUNTS is chosen later: early booking, book-by-fills, corroborate-before-demote and the UNKNOWN doctrine in `_enter_from_fill` are needed on any venue; only the one-stop topology and the R-order path would be deleted.

### 3.3 MINIMAL-PATCH (#3) — same idea, worse core

Its primitives (`_closable`, a virtual stop for the netted leg, a netted-out marker set after a fill of ours, re-entry through the chase) are NET-MIRROR's, but its bookkeeping choices create three holes NET-MIRROR does not have: the unbooked maker fill is kept as a **pseudo-leg** (qty 0) rather than booked, so a reduce-only order of the other leg can consume it with nothing to re-express, and a multi-print maker fill races the two reads into a false `diverged` inside the exact window the design claims reads `ok`; `_absorb_netting` explains away **any** net-0 divergence within 120 s of any reduce-only fill of ours (operator flatten and own-stop race both re-enter); and mechanism (E) sets `netted_out` **from a position read alone**, violating its own I4 and materialising one leg of a cancelling phantom pair. It is not minimal (750 lines). BLOCKING, verbatim:

> **[PHANTOM LEDGER, BLOCKING]** _reconcile_netting (called from the stop-FILLED door at mirror.py:2720-2731, from _absorb_fired_stop at 2992-3021, and from the committed close at 3193) declares EVERY other non-zero leg 'netted out' whenever the fresh net reads exactly 0. That rule is only coherent for a leg whose sign OPPOSES the fill. For a SAME-SIGN leg it is impossible for our fill alone to take the net to 0: the order was sized _closable = min(|leg|,|net|) with net = sum of both legs, so pullback's SELL 0.03 out of net +0.05 leaves +0.02. Net 0 after that fill therefore means the other leg's OWN stop also fired (or an external close). The design instead sets netted_out, clears the other leg's stop and entry refs, deliberately does NOT set stopped_entry_ts, and branch 1 re-enters it at market (non-reduce-only) while the engine still reports the position for up to 4h. That is the 2026-08-11 resurrection trap (LegLedger.stopped_entry_ts docstring, mirror.py:200-207) rebuilt as a feature, and it fires in the COMMON same-direction regime (75/25 blend, both legs long or both short ~82% of bars), not the opposed one the design was written for.

> **[LIVE RISK, BLOCKING]** The single-leg churn-guard fast path never resizes a net-sized stop after the other leg leaves. The design's guard is 'return if trigger within 5bp AND (no other leg live OR stop_qty == _closable)'; the size check is explicitly skipped when no other leg has qty or an entry_cloid. Every path that ends an opposed book by a ledger-only close plus top-up (design's incident-2 step 4, test_gate_subordinate_exit_first_is_ledger_only_then_topup) lands in exactly that state: the dominant leg has been topped up to full size, the other leg is at qty 0 with cleared refs, and the pullback's ATR trigger never moves >5bp (mirror.py:2740-2744, the existing guard; the pullback's fixed trigger is the documented reason _decay_stop_vanish exists, 2515-2542). So stop_qty stays at the old net for the life of the trade, contradicting I7 and the design's own test assertion 'stop resized 0.02481'. When that under-sized stop fires, the FILLED door zeroes the WHOLE leg (2718-2730 today; design keeps this), _reconcile_netting finds net != 0 and does nothing, both legs are at qty 0, and NOTHING calls _stop_backing any more (its only callers are _maintain_stop and _close_leg, both gated on led.qty != 0, 2693-2695 / 3037). _check_drift only pages (3774-3780). Result: a real venue long with no stop, no halt, stopped_entry_ts blocking re-entry, behind a position_drift RED.

(The second finding's fast-path bug is avoided in r2 by making the churn guard compare `stop_qty` against the required size on every poll where `led.qty` changed — no venue read needed on a single-leg book, §4.2.)

### 3.4 BLOCK-OPPOSITE (#4) — a strategy change, and it does not close the door

Honestly labelled `preserves_strategy: false`. It forfeits the −0.15-correlation diversification on 12.36% of bars, displaces ~half of S4's trades with a systematic late-entry bias, adds two S4 taker round trips per episode plus gratuitous round trips on unfilled S3 pendings, and the measurement cannot price any of that (§2). Even judged purely as an executor change it fails: the boot check and runtime rule miss the exact INC2 pre-state (held trend + resting opposite pullback limit looks one-sided on a position read; branch 2's identity return runs before the proposed `_make_room` insertion), so **"INC2 replays byte-for-byte under the new code"** (live-risk SERIOUS #1). BLOCKING, verbatim:

> **[PHANTOM LEDGER, BLOCKING]** Uncapped close/re-enter oscillation when the pullback's entry is DEFERRED after the yield close. _make_room(pullback) closes the trend whenever the LEDGER says trend opposes; _make_room(trend) re-admits the trend whenever the LEDGER shows neither a pullback qty nor a pullback entry_cloid. A deferred pullback entry (I2 'opposed' on a residue, or place_limit raising at mirror.py:2329 before the ref is set) leaves the ledger with no pullback qty and no ref, so the trend chases back in on the same poll via _enter_from_fill (mirror.py:2470, entry_cloid None after I6 so it chases the FULL want), and the next poll's _make_room(pullback) closes it again. Two taker fills per 20s poll, no counter, no cap, for the life of the pullback pending (up to one 4h bar = ~720 cycles). _check_drift (mirror.py:3734) is silent because the residue that drives the 'opposed' branch is below drift_tol_frac x equity, and the RED entry_backing_opposed dedupes for 600s. The design's own 'restore trend.qty from net' remedy does not break the loop: the restored dust is cleared by _close_leg's $10-floor branch (mirror.py:3035-3050) with no order, _entry_backing still reads 'opposed', and the trend leg chases in again because _ledger_opposes looks at the OTHER leg only.

If Casey ever wants the strategy question answered, it is a replay study (equity curves, drawdown, single-side blend vs blend), not an executor ticket.

## 4. The recommended design: NET-MIRROR r2

### 4.1 Critique fixes folded in

| # | Finding (lens, severity) | Fix folded in | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N1** | consumption clears a live maker ref (both lenses, BLOCKING) | Consumption never clears a ref the venue has not confirmed terminal. On consume, read the leg's `entry_cloid`: **OPEN** → `venue.cancel` (risk-reducing, no gate) and keep all refs until a venue-confirmed terminal read (branch 2's C1 rule, 2340-2366); **FILLED maker entry (pullback)** → keep the ref — it is the booking evidence and `_reestablish`'s pending-case precondition, and `_enter_from_fill` is idempotent on it; **FILLED market entry (trend)** → clear, as today's FILLED door does (otherwise the 2026-08-11 resurrection); **UNKNOWN** → keep. | G-N1 |
| **N2** | R order booked from one confirm read, single attempt (both lenses, BLOCKING) | The R order is handled exactly like a branch-2 entry: `reest_n` burned and `reest_cloid/reest_side/reest_qty` **persisted before the send**; `netted_qty` is decremented only by a **venue-confirmed** `filled_qty`; UNKNOWN → send nothing, re-read the same cloid next poll; venue-confirmed CANCELLED-unfilled → re-send allowed up to `REEST_MAX = 3` per consumption event, then RED `netted_shortfall` and drop. ### 4.3 Invariants

- **I1 rest-state identity.** With no executor order in flight, `round(sum(led.qty), 8) == venue.position()`. `netted_qty` is the only permitted ledger/venue gap; it is signed, names exposure the engine currently reports for that leg, and is set only by `_absorb_netted_fill` after a venue-confirmed fill of **our** reduce-only order plus a fresh position read — never from a position read alone, never by a same-sign leg (N8).
- **I2 two order categories, no third.** Every order is either reduce-only (`place_stop` always; `place_market(reduce_only=True)` for closes and the halt flatten) or entry-class non-reduce-only (limit entry, branch-2 market entry, chase, R order), and every entry-class order is justified by the engine reporting that leg this poll, gated on `entries_ok`, not halted, the $10 floor and `_leg_qty` caps.
- **I3 one stop when opposed.** On an opposed ledger exactly one protective stop rests: side `_close_side(sum)`, size `abs(sum)`, trigger = the dominant leg's engine level (or none, with both legs `stop_mode='engine'`, when `abs(sum)` is under the floor — N7). Single/same-side books keep per-leg reduce-only stops as today.
- **I4 re-attribution, never one-sided zeroing.** A reduce-only fill on leg D is absorbed by re-reading the venue and re-splitting both legs so I1 holds; no absorb door may zero one leg while an opposite-signed leg still claims venue exposure.
- **I5 corroboration first, unchanged.** `_stop_backing`'s predicate gates every placement, every close and every subordination (N3); `diverged` still halts — after a second look that re-reads maker fills (N5) and re-reads the position up to three times (N6).
- **I6 opening only toward the engine.** Consumed exposure is re-opened only if the engine reports the leg with matching side and `entry_ts`/`signal_ts` (or a pending whose own entry the venue confirms filled), and only under I2's gates; a blocked re-open leaves `qty 0 + netted_qty` and pages; it never halts and never trades blind.
- **I7 belief before confirm.** Every entry-class order — including R — has its refs persisted before the send and is booked only from a venue-confirmed read; UNKNOWN never books, never drops a marker, never provokes a second send (N2, N4, N9e).
- **I8 halt path untouched.** `_halt_locked` still flattens the venue NET reduce-only off a second read and zeroes only after verifying terminal.
- **I9 a subordinate's cancelled stop is not a vanish** — after corroboration `ok`. On `diverged` it is a vanish and halts as today.
- **I10 forensic cloid letters unique:** E entry, C chase, R reestablish, S stop, X close, `halt-<ts>`; every path has a persisted burned-before-send salt.
- **I11 strategy untouched.** The executor never decides when legs coexist or at what size; it only changes how the venue is made to hold their sum.

### 4.4 Phantom-ledger argument (2026-08-26 class: the ledger claims exposure the venue does not hold, and an order sized off that belief opens a real position)

1. **Reduce-only is not removed anywhere.** `hl.place_stop` stays reduce-only always (gate `test_gate_stops_are_always_reduce_only` binds); every close, the orphan path (now via `_close_leg`) and the halt flatten stay `reduce_only=True`. A stop or close against a phantom leg still cancels/no-ops at the venue.
2. **The only non-reduce-only orders are the existing entry class plus R**, which is deliberately the same class with the same gates plus two more (engine `entry_ts`/side match; fresh exact corroboration). A phantom ledger row cannot mint `netted_qty`: it is set only after a venue-confirmed fill of our own reduce-only order and a fresh read, and only for a leg whose sign opposed the fill (N8) — a phantom produces no fills. If the engine reports the leg and the venue is flat, opening it *is* correct mirroring (`_reconcile_boot`'s own doctrine), and `_maintain_stop` protects it in the same poll.
3. **Corroboration runs before every placement, close and subordination.** The 2026-08-26 shape — ledger `{+0.34 real, −0.14 phantom}`, venue +0.34 — reads `_book_shape` opposed, dominant pullback, want +0.20; `_maintain_stop(pullback)` falls through the churn guard (`stop_qty ≠ 0.20`) → `_stop_backing` diverged (0.34 ≠ 0.20) → second look changes nothing → LEDGER_DIVERGENCE → `_halt_locked` flattens the NET reduce-only off the second read. No non-reduce-only order is ever sent (B1 stays pinned). The phantom-dominant/real-subordinate case (phantom lens SERIOUS #3) now halts instead of stripping the real stop, because the subordinate head corroborates first (N3).
4. **The DISCLOSED LIMIT is narrowed, not widened.** A cancelling phantom pair against a flat venue (`{+0.01, −0.01}`, net 0) is still indistinguishable by a position read — but r2 arms **nothing** for it (a size-0 stop is not a stop), sends no reduce-only close (net 0), and re-opens only what the engine reports, stop-protected. Today "firing one genuinely does open the other side"; under r2 it cannot. A forgotten ARMED ORDER remains detectable only by the F1 open-orders sweep.
5. **Opening sizes never come from a position read.** R is sized from `netted_qty` (the venue-confirmed exposure the venue just closed) capped by `_leg_qty`; a 2-second-stale read cannot inflate it — the failure a plain non-reduce-only flip would have had (SELL 0.02481 against an already-flat venue = 0.02481 naked short).
6. **Blind venue.** `_absorb_netted_fill` touches nothing on a failed re-read; `_reestablish` requires a fresh `ok`; UNKNOWN status reads never book, never drop markers, never explain a divergence away (I7).
7. **Crash safety.** `netted_qty` and `reest_cloid` are persisted before any R send. Crash between the reduce-only close and the R send → `{qty 0, netted N} == venue 0`, self-heals through the same gates. Crash after the R send → boot reads `reest_cloid` (N9b): FILLED → booked and stopped on the first poll; UNKNOWN → `_boot_mismatch` blocks entries and pages (today's chase-crash outcome).
8. **Early booking is a venue-confirmed read only**, and its refs are never cleared while the order may be live (N1), so the inverse phantom (venue holds what the ledger does not, unstopped) the critiques found cannot arise from consumption.

## 5. Honesty box

**Not solved by this fix**
- **The pending-window stopless exposure.** A pullback maker fill has no engine stop level until the next 4h bar; on an opposed book the whole net is stopless during that window because the subordinate's stop is (correctly) gone too. r2 removes the halt and makes the window loud (RED `unbooked_fill_unprotected`); closing it needs the engine to publish a provisional stop with the pending. Today's code has the identical window, silently.
- **Bitstamp-spot vs HL-perp basis** (engine fills the venue does not, or vice versa): the orphan path is corroborated and consumption-aware, not eliminated.
- **The trend's own stopless branch-2 window** (market entry at 16:00, engine reports the position at ~20:00; identity return skips `_maintain_stop`) — pre-existing, untouched.
- **F1 open-orders sweep** — only the optional stop-count page is included; a forgotten armed order is still only detectable by a sweep.

**Disclosed limits (narrowed)**
- A cancelling phantom pair against a flat venue is still indistinguishable from a real one by a position read; r2 arms nothing for it and opens only engine-justified, stop-protected exposure. `_stop_backing`'s docstring and EXECUTOR.md row 66 must say exactly this and no more.
- The subordinate leg has **no venue stop**; its level is enforced by the engine's 60 s Bitstamp poll and mirrored within ~20 s. Its level sits on the *profitable* side of the net, so an outage there risks tracking of the engine's take-profit on the top-up, not loss protection on the account's actual exposure. The venue stop always protects the actual net at the dominant's level.
- Per-leg P&L on a netted venue is ledger-implied: a consumed leg realises `closedPnl` at the netting event and again at its eventual exit — two ⚡ lines per engine trade with a shifted entry basis. Cosmetic; disclosed in the line.

**Costs**
- One taker round trip on the consumed leg per netting event: upper bound ~25/yr (110 episodes / 4.5 y; not every episode ends in a venue-netting event); at incident size ~$0.44 fee + ≤1 bp slippage each — tens of dollars a year today, linear in size. The pullback's maker economics are lost on any portion it re-establishes at market.
- Two extra stop cancel/replace pairs per opposite episode (~50/yr), each with a ~1 s stopless gap.
- Unmirrored windows on the consumed leg: ≤20 s normally; as long as `entries_ok` is false, the executor is halted, or the venue rejects the R order — paged RED `leg_unmirrored`, never traded blind. A new tracking-error source, consistent with doctrine.
- ~500 lines in `mirror.py`, ~100 in `main.py`/docs, ~900 lines of tests: the largest mirror change since the 08-26 chain. Four new persisted fields and a new coverage row.

**What could still halt the book**
- A genuine phantom or divergence (unchanged and correct); a consumption postcondition mismatch that persists 3 polls; an UNKNOWN status read during a stop-out on a single-side leg (existing DISCLOSED LIMIT: an unreadable venue is never explained away); STOP_UNPLACEABLE on real venue rejects; the equity rails, agent expiry, VENUE_CHANGED; a sub-$10 residue (`halt_residue_unsendable`, existing).
- A rollback during a consumed window: the old build drops `netted_qty`/`reest_cloid` with a warning and re-enters that leg via `_enter_from_fill` at fresh size (acceptable; put in the deploy note); a rollback with an R order in flight leaves a position without a ledger row → `_boot_mismatch`.

**Evidence caveats (one line each)**
- No production event log is in the repo; the halt texts and the `reduceOnlyCanceled` rows come from the venue records transcribed in `test_netting_gates.py`, not re-verified live here.
- INC1's absorb ordering (A/B) is unresolved and moot — neither ordering sends an order.
- The measurement is in-sample on the selection fixture, trade-close basis, S4 fee conservative; the fixture ends 2026-07-25, before both incidents.
- The measured "−15.6%" is an attribution, not a price for any coexistence rule (§2).

## 7. Plain-English summary for the operator

Both halts had the same cause: Hyperliquid holds one net BTC position, but the executor keeps two separate legs and two separate protective orders, so whenever the pullback and trend legs were on opposite sides, a stop or a fill on one leg silently closed or flipped the other and the safety check (correctly) refused to trust a ledger the venue no longer matched. There was never a three-hour refused close on 09-10 — the engine's "20:00" exit is just the bar's timestamp, and the venue's stop fired in the same minute as the engine's — and the mystery 0.03303 sell on 09-14 was the executor's own halt flattening the book. The fix keeps every reduce-only order and every existing safety check, and teaches the executor three things: book a maker fill the moment the venue confirms it, keep exactly one stop sized to the real net when the legs oppose (the smaller leg's exit is enforced by the engine and mirrored within ~20 s), and when the venue nets one leg away, put it back only if the engine still holds it, through the same gated entry path used today. Cost: one small taker fill (cents to a dollar at current size) each time that happens, roughly two dozen times a year at most. It does not change when the two strategies may coexist — that is a separate question the measurement cannot answer — and it does not fix the pre-existing up-to-4-hour window in which a fresh pullback fill sits with no stop until the engine publishes its level; it will now page you when that window is open. Until it ships, resuming into a book where S3 and S4 oppose each other can repeat the halt; the failure is fail-closed (a reduce-only flatten plus a manual resume, never a naked position), but expect it.
