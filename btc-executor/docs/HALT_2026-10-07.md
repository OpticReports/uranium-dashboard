## 2. Fault A — engine: a bar was evaluated from the snapshot taken while it was forming

`backend/app/live.py` at 642e72b (= `origin/main` for this app):

- `poll()` L343-347 refreshed bars ONLY when `time.time() >= self.bars[-1].ts + BAR_SECONDS`.
- `refresh_bars()` L187-203 fetched from `bars[-1].ts − 4h` and MERGED everything
  fetched into `self.bars` by timestamp — including the still-forming bar
  (`fetch_4h_bars` L19-21: "Includes the still-forming bar — callers must
  filter on closed-ness"). Only closed bars were persisted.
- So at the 04:00:0x boundary the in-memory window gained a 04:00Z bar
  holding only its first seconds. The minute data says that snapshot must
  have been taken BEFORE the 04:00:xx print at 84,113.14 (a later snapshot
  would have had low < limit and the pre-fix engine would have FILLED) —
  consistent with the executor placing its order at 04:00:23 off the
  engine's publication. From then until 08:00 no refresh ran (`bars[-1]`
  was that forming bar, its end 4h away).
- At 08:00:0x `poll()` called `refresh_bars()` then `catch_up()`. The one
  Bitstamp fetch at that instant returned nothing for the 04:00Z bar
  (`fetch_4h_bars` returns `[]` on any exception, L43-45; `refresh_bars`
  then returns having changed nothing, L192-193). `catch_up()` L205-241 saw
  the stale forming snapshot, now with `bar.ts + 4h <= now`, and processed
  it as the closed bar: low ≥ limit → pending cancelled, S3 flat.
  The "candle not yet finalized at 08:00" alternative is EXCLUDED by the
  minute data: the 84,010.77 low printed at 04:43, so any 04:00Z candle
  served at 08:00 carried it and would have filled.
- The next poll's refresh succeeded (the old rule still fired: `bars[-1]`
  was still the 04:00 bar), overwrote the 04:00Z bar with its final values
  and persisted it (which is why the stored row is correct today), but
  `catch_up` skipped it: `bar.ts <= last_processed` (L210). The damage was
  done in the poll before the retry.
- No `degraded` event is expected on this path: `poll()` refreshes
  `last_data_ok` from the ticker every poll (L304-306), and the OHLC fetch
  failing once does not change it.

The module docstring (L9-11) stated the invariant the code did not keep:
"a 4h bar is processed once wall-clock passes its end boundary AND it has
been fetched from REST; late REST data therefore delays processing rather
than producing a signal on an incomplete bar." A forming bar fetched at its
open satisfied "fetched from REST" and later passed the boundary without a
re-fetch.

What a log line would still add: the 08:00:0x "Bitstamp OHLC fetch failed"
warning (the exception text).

Fix A (engine, `live.py` + `config.py`):
1. record the wall-clock time each bar was last fetched (`_bar_fetched_at`,
   stamped BEFORE the request);
2. a bar is FINAL only if fetched at or after `bar.ts + BAR_SECONDS + bar_final_grace_s`
   (new setting, default 30 s) — a DEFENSIVE margin for Bitstamp's own
   candle finalization, not a branch 10-07 exercised;
3. `catch_up` processes bars in order and STOPS at the first non-final one:
   INFO `bar_not_final` when the boundary poll merely landed inside the
   grace (the expected case, about half of all bars), WARN a poll later,
   RED `bar_stuck` plus a page three polls later;
4. `poll` refreshes whenever a closed bar is still unprocessed. The old
   boundary-only rule did retry after a FAILED fetch (bars[-1] stayed the
   closed bar); what it could not do is refresh again after a SUCCESSFUL
   in-grace fetch had appended the forming bar — the new rule covers both;
5. the fetch window starts at the oldest closed-but-unprocessed bar, so a
   candle the venue once served while forming and then dropped keeps being
   re-requested instead of sliding out of the window;
6. only final bars are persisted (boot trusts every DB row as final);
   rows written from now on are final by construction, and `reset_books`
   marks the closed bars it replays final;
7. CONTIGUITY (round 2): `catch_up` refuses to process a bar when the
   expected next bar (`last_processed + 4h`) is not the one in hand — a
   restart can load a DB with a hole when the venue dropped a candle it
   once served forming — pages RED `bar_gap` once (round 3: the post-loop
   "missing" check is skipped after a deferral, otherwise two keys
   alternated and paged twice per poll), and keeps re-requesting the
   expected bar: the fetch window starts there, so a two-bar hole is
   re-requested from its first bar. `reset_books` refuses to replay over a
   hole in the replayed window (from `start_ts − 4h`; a hole inside the
   indicator warmup only is replayed over and shifts indicator values) with
   HTTP 409.

   OPERATOR RECOVERY for a PERMANENT hole (the venue never serves the bar
   again; the engine stalls by design, `bar_ts` pins on `/exec/target`, the
   executor's 3-bar stale guard blocks new entries, a resting pending keeps
   resting, `bar_gap` / `bar_stuck` paged once):
   1. confirm the venue has no candle:
      `GET https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=14400&limit=1&start=<ts>`;
   2. build the bar from Bitstamp minute candles (`step=60&limit=240&start=<ts>`:
      240 rows aggregate exactly to the 4h row — open = first open, high =
      max, low = min, close = last close, volume = sum; the review did this
      for 2026-10-07 04:00Z) or from Kraken's 4h candle;
   3. insert the row into the engine's SQLite (`/app/data/paperengine.db`,
      table `bars`: `ts_open, open, high, low, close, volume`) and restart
      the service — `boot()` marks DB rows final and `catch_up` processes it
      IN ORDER against the real range, so the pending resolves correctly and
      no book state is lost;
   4. never advance `last_processed` by hand, and never reset the books
      over an open pending (a `POST /books/reset` with `start` after the
      hole succeeds but restarts the books FLAT, dropping a pending the
      venue may already have filled — the 10-07 shape again).
   While stuck the fetch is pinned at the hole and runs every poll: one
   1000-bar page per poll (the helper caps at 30 pages), far inside
   Bitstamp's limits; healthy-path cost is unchanged.

Cost: a signal or exit can publish up to one poll period later than today
(when the boundary poll lands inside the grace); absolute latency after a
bar close is at most grace + one poll period plus the poll's own HTTP time.
Disclosed limit: `/exec/target` carries nothing about a deferred or stuck
bar, so the executor cannot tell a deferral from health; its stale guard
(3 bars) blocks new entries only, and a maker entry already resting for a
still-published pending keeps resting through a deferral — the engine /
venue disagreement window of section 4 grows by the deferral (routine: ≤
one poll; stuck: until the guard). The RED `bar_stuck` / `bar_gap` page is
the operator's signal.

## 3. Fault B — executor: one order-status read became a residue that nothing settled

- after `place_market` it read `order_status(cloid)` ONCE and, on
  `FILLED`/`CANCELLED` with `0.0 < filled + 1e-9 < qty`, booked the
  difference as a residue (`close_partial`, "retrying next poll") — N9e,
  2026-09-16. The epsilon admits `filled == 0.0`, so a terminal ZERO-fill
  read books the WHOLE leg as a residue.
- nothing retried: the single-leg path never set `close_cloid`, so
  `_settle_close` (L4271, which re-reads the venue) never ran for it; the
  residue was pure belief.
- the next polls compared venue net (0.03134) with the ledger sum (residue +
  0.03134) → `diverged`; the trend leg's stop read OPEN → `in_flight` →
  grace twice (`netting_inconsistent` 1/3, 2/3) → third poll →
  `LEDGER_DIVERGENCE` → flatten everything.

Which read: two shapes fit the code, and only the log line's `filled X of
0.93614` tells them apart. (i) `hl.order_status` maps the venue's
`unknownOid` to `CANCELLED / 0` (L613-615) — an info node asked 100 ms

four prints share one millisecond (atomic matching, no transitional
record). (ii) a transitional order record (status already terminal, `sz`

from one read of a source that can lag.

Fix B (executor, `mirror.py` + `hl.py`), re-designed after the first
review round found that settling from the POSITION read merely moved the
single-read flaw (a lagging or part-applied position read reproduced the
same halt):

- `hl.place_market` returns the exchange's OWN answer to the order
  (`statuses[0].filled.totalSz` / `avgPx`, or `resting`). An IOC is matched
  atomically; this is the one read that cannot lag.
- the single-leg close persists its ref (`close_cloid`, `close_qty`,
  `close_net_before`, `close_why`, `close_mode="single"`, `close_sent_ts`)
  BEFORE the send and books the fill from that answer
  (`_book_close_fill`). A reduce-only IOC sized ≤ |net| with the leg's sign
  cannot be resized by the venue, so its filled size IS what left the leg
  (unlike a stop at trigger, N6). Residue = a genuine partial IOC, re-closed
  next poll (stopless for that poll, the disclosed limit).
- no usable answer (the Coinbase adapter, a parse failure, a response that
  raised after the venue accepted the order): `_settle_close_single` reads
  the ORDER RECORD. A FULL fill on the record is certain at any age (no lag
  or transitional shape over-reports an IOC sized ≤ |net|) and books in the
  same poll — the pre-fix behaviour for the one read that is safe. A
  partial books only once the record is older than `CLOSE_SETTLE_MIN_AGE_S`
  (40 s, two poll periods): a terminal read seconds after the send can be
  transitional, and `unknownOid` reads as `CANCELLED / 0`. Everything else
  keeps the ref, with a RED `close_unconfirmed` once per poll whose text
  carries the poll count (the dedupe cannot silence it). Polls are counted
  by `_poll_seq`, not by settle calls — round 2 found three call sites per
  poll, which made the tiebreak fire at age 0 when the other leg's stop
  maintenance corroborated in the send poll.
- the tiebreak runs only when BOTH the age (≥ 40 s) and the poll count
  (≥ `CLOSE_UNKNOWN_MAX_POLLS`, 2) hold — two polls counted from the first
  settle attempt (the send poll on every path; with 20 s polls it cannot
  run before the third poll) — once per poll, in this order: the venue's
  own fills for the cloid (`fill_pnl`, userFills on HL) book if present;
  then the change in THIS LEG'S OWN exposure since the send — the venue
  net minus the other legs' ledger sum, read at the send

  in the window (its stop absorbed, an entry, an R fill) is never
  attributed to this close; a MOVE is then weighed against the other
  legs' orders (the round-4 bullet below), an UNMOVED look against their
  unbooked entries

  as the pullback's partial close and left the trend's size on the venue
  with a flat ledger, no stop and no halt). A FULL move books only when
  it holds on two consecutive polls (a stop reads OPEN in the instant it
  fires: one look cannot tell its fill from this close's when the sizes
  match); a PARTIAL move never books from the position (the record or the
  fills settle it); an UNMOVED position never clears the ref on one read —
  it takes an affirmative `CANCELLED / 0` record on two consecutive
  tiebreak polls (`close_affirm_polls`), or the hard streak

  log keeps every line) — fail loud, no more orders. The never-happened

reads as a tolerated surplus and pages `position_drift`, as before.

## 4. What the fix does NOT change

- The engine's fill rule (`bar.low < limit`, strict) and the executor's
  doctrine that a leg the engine reports flat is closed (branch 3,
  `engine_exit`). If the engine and the venue still disagree on a bar (a
  Bitstamp low exactly at the limit vs an HL print at it), the cost is one
  round trip, not a halt.
- The ~4h `unbooked_fill_unprotected` window on a pullback maker fill.
- The opposed-book dominant exit (position-attributed, 2026-09-16 design).
- Nothing on the carry sleeve.
