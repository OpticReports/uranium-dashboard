# The dollar as a crisis gauge — scoping study and the "Dollar watch" panel (rev 2, built descriptive 2026-11-02)

Casey's question (2026-11-02, after Brent Johnson's "It will die from strength" talk, Santiago
Capital, Oct-2026): use this research and more to build a DXY chart that watches dollar strength
relative to other factors to predict looming crises and to tell when a crisis has subsided; analyse
how the DXY changes future events, which factors create them, and how to chart them. Casey's own
addition from the first chart: the 2008 and 2022 V-spikes are people scrambling for dollars to pay
debts; if the RATE and VOLUME of dollar-seeking slow, that may mark the top of the crisis forming.

Status: the four layers are BUILT, DESCRIPTIVE (§9: the Dollar tab on treasury-canary, GET
/dollar-watch); nothing pre-registered or graded — the pre-registration is drafted (§9.4) and its
freeze waits on Casey's crisis-list answer. Rev 2 restates the band, momentum, gold and 2022 numbers
under the build's single window convention (§9.2). Three finders (A the 1971–2026
event record with real ICE DXY daily data, B the mechanism and the measurable factors with every
endpoint probed, C the academic literature and a pre-registrable design), the orchestrator's own
scoping on the real DXY (monthly 1973–2026, daily 2006–2026), two adversarial reviewers (numbers;
logic and design). Only numbers that survived review appear as fact; corrected values used;
UNVERIFIED labelled. Notes, verdicts, scripts, charts and small data under
`studies/dollar_watch/`.

## 1. Verdict

1. **Johnson's picture is right about the mechanism and wrong about the arithmetic.** The
   rest of the world owes a record $15.1tn of on-balance-sheet dollar credit (+8.1% yoy, BIS
   2026Q2) plus a $129tn FX-swap book (USD leg $113tn, end-2025); a fast dollar rise does
   transmit into credit stress and EM downturns (Obstfeld–Zhou: a 10% advanced-economy dollar
   shock → EM GDP −1.5% after ~8 quarters, in-sample, a growth channel rather than an onset
   predictor). But his band and his "every crisis" statistics do not hold on the data: outside
   85–105 an onset followed within 24 months in 12 of 17 excursions (71%) against a 69% base rate
   on the 25-crisis list, 8 of 17 (47%) against 54% on finder C's 16 onsets, and 3 of 17 (18%)
   against 19% on the five dollar-funding crises proper (strict window, §9.2; finder A's inclusive
   count was 13 of 17); the dollar FELL at peak
   stress in 9 of 24 completed crises (1982, 1987, 1990, 1995, 1998 LTCM, 2011, 2023, 2024, 2025),
   against about 50% of same-length windows (so it rose into peak stress in 15 of 24, more often than
   usual, but far from "every"; the open 2026 episode, +1.1% so far, is provisional); 10 of 18
   fast rises BEGAN inside a crisis. No DXY band or speed threshold beat the unconditional crisis
   frequency on any of three crisis lists. The dollar's rise in a crisis is mostly the crisis
   itself: in 9 of 16 onsets it rose with US yields falling (flight to safety), it does not
   Granger-lead the VIX or TED, and the safe-haven literature puts its R² on risk at 10–60% after
   2007 against a few % before.
2. **What leads the DOLLAR is the price of dollar funding; nothing free leads the crisis.** In
   the two balance-sheet episodes with daily data (2008, 2022) the funding-price series —
   financial CP minus bills, the OFR funding subindex, the Chicago Fed risk index — peaked 139–144
   days before the dollar's peak in 2008 and 87 days before it in 2022 (on a one-day CP print;
   206 days excluding it) and first alarmed 6–18 months earlier (the 2007-08-09 alarm is
   the BNP Paribas week: coincident with onset, ahead of the dollar); in 2020 everything moved
   within a week; in the EM growth scares (2015–16, 2018) they were silent. So the panel's
   nowcast layer is the funding plumbing, labelled as a lead over the dollar, not over the
   crisis; the dollar itself is the coincident-to-lagging confirmation.
3. **The dollar's peak marks the end of the dollar SCRAMBLE, not of the crisis; Casey's
   deceleration rule is a hypothesis to score, not a rule.** The broad dollar's peak dated the
   end of the scramble in every episode since 2008 (2009-03-03, 2016-01-20, 2018-11-27,
   2020-03-23, 2022-09-26) and gold bottomed at or before it (111 days before in 2008). But
   "scramble over" is not "crisis over": in 2008 the funding spreads halved (17-Oct), gold
   troughed (12-Nov) and the deceleration trigger fired (6-Nov) four months and −21% to −28%
   before the equity low. The S&P was higher 12 months after the dollar's ex-post peak in 7 of 12
   fast-rise episodes since 1973 (10 of 12 from a real-time turn), against 76% of all months —
   no edge for equities; the markers were right for gold holders. Casey's observation itself
   holds in the two dollar-funding scrambles with daily data: the 20-day rate of climb peaked
   within 0–19 trading days of the VIX peak and 0–84 index days before the dollar's own peak (2008:
   climb 27-Oct at +12.9% on the rule's own index, 24-Oct on the full H.10 calendar; VIX 20-Nov;
   dollar 3-Mar-2009, +3.6% above the climb peak; 2020: climb and dollar 23-Mar, VIX 16-Mar). As a
   real-time trigger on daily data (parameters chosen on these episodes) it caught 2020 cleanly and
   fired on the FIRST wave in 2008 (22-Sep; broad dollar +17.7% more, S&P −44%) and 2022 (28-Jul;
   broad dollar +5.2% more, S&P −12%); a re-arm variant added after the 2008 miss then caught 6-Nov-2008 (dollar +6.5% more
   over four months, S&P −25%); three euro-crisis mini-episodes (2010–12) resolved within weeks.
   Scored blind on monthly 1973–2005 it has NO edge (dollar peak within 3 months of a firing in
   3 of 10 vs a 42% base). 2022 was not a funding scramble at all (swap lines peaked at $11bn,
   policy quadrant, a nine-month grind). Verdict: a top-of-scramble marker for sudden stops,
   to be frozen and scored before it gets a colour.
4. **Gold.** His drawdown numbers are right (GFC −33%, 2020 −15%, 2022 −22%, dotcom −16%): gold
   is sold for dollars in the liquidation window. Gold ended ABOVE its pre-crisis level in 14 of 24
   completed crises (12 by more than 1%; rev 1 said 10 of 25, listing examples, not the count),
   against about 61% of all same-length windows: that is gold's own drift, not crisis resilience. It
   rose outright in 1973–74 and 1987 while the dollar fell; its trough is the
   earliest free flag that the dollar scramble is ending — valid for gold holders, 10–21% early
   for equities in 2008/2015/2018. The live anomaly: gold −27% to −31% from its late-January 2026
   high (front-month futures vs spot) with the DXY up under 6% and zero funding stress — a gold
   crash without a dollar squeeze, which the dollar-funding mechanism does not explain and which
   must not be read through Johnson's lens.
5. **Today (2026-11-02; the panel's live numbers are in §9.3).** DXY 101.7 (inside every candidate
   band; 6m +3.6%, 12m +1.9% on daily closes; broad dollar 121.3, 78th percentile of ten years). Zero
   dollar-funding stress on every free gauge: CP−bill 5bp (7th percentile since 1997), swap-line
   draws $156m, OFR funding −0.15, EM OAS 1.81%, no reserve sales. Dollar credit outside the US growing 8% — Johnson's own "re-dollarisation"
   phase: the fuel is being added, not burned. What the data describe instead is a rates/oil
   shock: Brent $123 (98th percentile since 2000), US 2y 4.61 above the funds-rate ceiling, SOFR
   above IORB (97th percentile), foreign official custody at a 14-year low, and a safe-haven bid
   that went to gold until January, not to the dollar (the franc WEAKENED 3.7–5.2% against the dollar
   over 12 and 6 months; rev 1 said otherwise). No rule in this study is anywhere near firing.
6. **Recommendation — build the "Dollar watch" panel on treasury-canary DESCRIPTIVE, in four
   layers (§5): dollar state with the regime quadrant as a label; a "dollar-funding nowcast" of
   the free plumbing gauges; a "dollar-scramble peak" gauge (deceleration, funding easing, gold
   turning) that never says "subsided"; the slow state and a dated forecast ledger. Every
   number with its N and base rate; no probability, warning colour or lamp until finder C's
   minimal pre-registration (frozen crisis lists, 1973–2007 fit / 2008–26 test, leave-one-crisis-
   out, the end rule scored per episode) and the frozen deceleration parameters have been run
   and verified. What it cannot do: name a crisis date, give a probability of "the next crisis"
   (16 onsets cannot support one), or see the cross-currency basis, EMBI, CDS or global PMI
   (paid).

## 5. The recommended build — "Dollar watch" panel on treasury-canary

**Layer 1 — dollar STATE (descriptive).** DXY (ICE; daily from Yahoo, FMP fallback) and the broad /
AFE / EME Fed indices; Johnson's 85–105 band as a reference line labelled "ad hoc band; no edge found on

unconditional rate beside it; no probabilities on cells with fewer than ~15 independent episodes.

**Layer 2 — the DOLLAR-FUNDING NOWCAST.** Financial and non-financial CP − bill, the OFR FSI funding /
EM / safe-asset subindices (already loaded; expose the columns), NFCI risk, SOFR99 − SOFR and SOFR −
IORB, Fed swap-line usage with the announcement dates logged as event markers, EMB. Each with its
own-history percentile and its 2008 / 2020 / 2022 alarm dates. Labelled honestly: these series led the
DOLLAR's peak by 3–5 months in the two balance-sheet episodes (n = 2) and were silent in the EM growth
scares; their first alarms came within about two months of the crisis's start as the 25-crisis list dates
it (2007, 2022), i.e. roughly coincident with the crisis. The lead is over the dollar squeeze, which the
16-onset list dates 6–15 months later.

**Layer 3 — the DOLLAR-SCRAMBLE PEAK gauge (Casey's rule, frozen and gated).** Visible only when a
scramble exists (20-day climb ≥ 4% with the 10-year yield falling). Three indicators, shown separately,
never summed into a lamp: (a) deceleration — the climb at ≤ 50% of its 60-day max with the dollar still
within 3% of its high; (b) funding easing — CP − bill and OFR funding back under their 1-year medians;
(c) gold and EMB above their 20-day highs. The dollar's own 3%-off-peak confirms with a 0–4 month lag;
swap-line usage below 10% of its episode peak is the lagging confirmation. The seven historical
firings including the first-wave misses, the 2008 "four months early" case and the 1973–2005 null
stay on the panel permanently. The gauge says "dollar scramble peaking", never "crisis subsided".

**Layer 4 — the SLOW STATE, the Treasury channel and the ledger.** BIS USD credit outside the US
(level, yoy, EME share) labelled "re-dollarising / de-dollarising" per Johnson with the −0.50 yoy correlation
shown as coincident; the one-quarter dollar lead (−0.55 yoy) does not survive on non-overlapping quarters
(−0.36 coincident, −0.29 dollar first) (no fuel-gauge framing: the stock has been at
a record before every crisis and every calm year since 2010); the FX-swap stock semiannually; Fed-vs-
world policy-rate differentials and the US 2-year vs the funds-rate ceiling (separates the policy
quadrant in real time); the Treasury channel the dashboard is named for — 10-year, term premium,
foreign official custody (−6.6% yoy, 14-year low) and the 2025 inverted safe-haven episode — which is
the de-dollarisation evidence that exists and argues against the milkshake, not for it. A dated
forecast ledger: every firing of the momentum signal and of the scramble gauge, the reading, and what
EM FX, spreads and the S&P did over the following 3 / 6 / 12 months.

**Pre-registration before any layer gets a colour or a lamp** (finder C's minimal design, ~half a day
plus one verifier): three frozen crisis lists (A's 25, C's 16, the 5 dollar-funding squeezes — reported,
never inferred on); 12-month momentum thresholds fit on 1973–2007 and scored on 2008–2026 with

**Paid unlocks if it matters**: cross-currency basis (Bloomberg / Refinitiv), JPM EMBI and sovereign

## 6. Honesty box

- n is small: 25 crises on finder A's list (16 on finder C's), ~5 genuine dollar-funding squeezes
  (1982, 1998, 2008, 2020, 2022); clusters, not independent draws. AUROC standard error ≈ 0.07 at
  16 onsets: nothing below ~0.70 is distinguishable from chance.
- Reverse causality is the central problem: the dollar rises because forced deleveraging buys
  dollars; "dollar up → crisis" and "crisis → dollar up" produce identical event tables. Only the
  funding-price leads (3–5 months, two episodes) and the Obstfeld–Zhou real-economy lag separate them.
- The deceleration rule's parameters were chosen on 2008/2020/2022 and sit at the optimum of a sweep;
  its honest record on the motivating V-spikes is 1 clean (2020) and 2 first-wave misses, with the
  re-arm patch added after the 2008 miss; no edge on unseen 1973–2005 monthly data; VIX-scoring
  flatters it (2008-11-06: VIX −20 but S&P −25%).
- "S&P higher a year after the dollar's peak" is 7 of 12 ex post (10 of 12 from a real-time turn) vs
  76% of all months: no edge for equities. An earlier draft said "all 12" from a six-episode proxy run;
  the logic reviewer caught it.
- The base rate is sensitive to the crisis list: on 25 crises any 24-month window has a 69% chance
  of a start; on the 5 dollar-funding squeezes it is ~20%, and no excursion test has the n to beat it.
- Dollar data: ICE DXY daily from Yahoo (1971+) is the only free long daily source reachable (14,177
  rows, identical to a fresh pull); FRED's major-currencies index is the fallback (weights differ;
  ±4% in level). Gold pre-2000 is monthly only; the 2026 gold drawdown is confirmed on FMP spot
  (−30.9% intraday, −28.5% close), not a futures-roll artefact.
- The S&P splice: Shiller's final row (2023-09) is a single-day close, not a monthly mean (+2.4%
