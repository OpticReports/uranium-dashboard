# Finder C — Predictive methods for "when does a capex/valuation boom become a problem", and how to test the earthquake analogy honestly

Date: 2026-10-31. Lens: out-of-sample (OOS) or pseudo-OOS evidence only counts; everything else is labelled in-sample.
Raw material (PDF text extracts, data, scripts) in `./raw/` and `./scripts/`. All numbers below were read from the primary PDFs where I could obtain them (GSY, GHSS, Baron-Xiong, JST, Titman-Wei-Xie, Goetzmann); items I could not open are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Conventions: "crash" = GSY's 40% drawdown within 24 months unless stated; "pp" = percentage points.

---

## 0. One-paragraph verdict

The only bubble-timing literature with real (pseudo-)OOS evidence is the Harvard credit-plus-prices line (Greenwood-Shleifer-You 2019 for industries; Greenwood-Hanson-Shleifer-Sørensen 2022 for countries; Baron-Xiong 2017 for bank credit) plus Danielsson-Valenzuela-Zer 2018 on prolonged low volatility. Their common message: **the size of the run-up raises crash probability but not average return; what separates run-ups that crash from those that don't is credit/issuance, rising volatility, young-firm concentration, and price ACCELERATION — not turnover, not fundamentals (sales growth).** Nothing in the published literature directly tests the earthquake claim (that the *absence of intermediate corrections* predicts crash size); the closest proxies are GSY's "acceleration" (last-year share of a 2-year run-up) and DVZ's "prolonged low volatility", both of which point the same way as the analogy, in-sample. LPPLS/Sornette has an open MIT implementation but no independent, audited OOS record. Investment-based predictors (Titman-Wei-Xie, Cooper-Gulen-Schill, Arif-Lee, Lamont) are robust in the cross-section (2–8%/yr spreads) but weak and horizon-dependent in the aggregate. For the AI cluster the defensible deliverable is a pre-registered pressure gauge scored on the Ken French 49-industry panel (n≈40–46 episodes at the 100%/100% threshold; ≈180 at 50%/50%), applied to AI as ONE out-of-sample observation with wide intervals — never a crash date.

## 1. Literature with numbers

### 1.1 Greenwood, Shleifer & You (2019), "Bubbles for Fama", JFE 131:20–43 (NBER w23191)
Source PDF (read in full, text in `raw/gsy.txt`): https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf ; journal record https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:131:y:2019:i:1:p:20-43

- **Sample/definitions.** US: CRSP share codes 10/11, Fama-French 49 industries (first 48, "Other" dropped), industries with ≥10 firms, VW monthly returns Jan 1926–Mar 2014 → run-ups identified Jan 1928–Mar 2012. Run-up = **≥100% VW return over past 2 years in BOTH raw and net-of-market terms AND ≥50% raw over 5 years** (the 5-year filter avoids rebounds). They do NOT use Ken French's posted industry returns (they re-form industries monthly to include new listings), but report **97.6% correlation** with French's VW industry series — so French's free file is a near-replica. International: 38 countries, 11 GICS-level sectors, 1985/87–2012, Compustat Global.
- **n.** US: **40 episodes**, 21 crash / 19 don't. International: **107** country-sector run-ups in 31 countries, 53 crash / 54 don't. Of the 40 US episodes, the 1929 cluster is 4 (Autos 1928/03, Chemicals 1929/01, ElcEq 1929/01, Utilities 1929/07); the 1999–2000 cluster is 5 (Hardware 1999/03, Software 1999/03, ElecEq 1999/12, Steel 2000/08, Meas&Ctrl 2000/02). Full episode list in `raw/gsy.txt` lines 1495–1575.
- **Unconditional vs conditional crash probability (Table 3 / text).** Unconditional probability of a 40% drawdown in any 2-year window across all industry-months 1928–2013 = **14%** (11% post-1970); international 24%. Conditional on a 2-year run-up of **50%: 20%** (US) / 36% (intl); **100%: 53%** (US) / 50% (intl); **150%: 80%** of 15 US episodes / 67% of 51 intl episodes. Kernel plot (Fig. 3, Panel A, net-of-market): ~20% at ≤50%, rising to ~35% at 100%.
- **Average returns after run-up (Table 1/3).** Mean 24-month raw return after a 100% run-up ≈ **0%**; net-of-market ≈ 0%; excess of RF −10%. So "Fama is right about average returns" at 100% — but at the 150% cutoff, 24-month raw −13%, excess −28% (15 US episodes), intl −17%/−23%. Crash episodes: mean 24-mo raw −42%, max drawdown −60%, prices peak on average **6 months after** the run-up is identified (9 of 21 crashes begin within 3 months; for these 1-yr raw = −41%).
- **Which characteristics distinguish crash from non-crash (Table 4, US; Table 5, intl).**
  - *Volatility level*: not discriminating in US (0.50 both groups vs 0.33 unconditional); strongly discriminating intl (15.4 percentile points, t=5.58).
  - *1-year change in volatility*: crash +0.09 vs non-crash −0.03, diff 0.11, sig 5% (US); intl diff 0.060, t=1.68.
  - *Turnover*: elevated in ALL run-ups (0.68 vs 0.55) but **does not discriminate** (US or intl).
  - *Issuance* (% of firms issuing equity in past year; e.g., Software Mar-1999 = 48%): discriminates at 5% in US; weaker intl.
  - *Age tilt* (EW return minus age-weighted return — i.e., how much the run-up is concentrated in young firms): discriminates at 5%.
  - *Market CAPE* at identification: discriminates at 5% (bubbles that crash occur in high-market-P/E periods).
  - *Book-to-market, firm age*: 10% level.
  - *Sales growth*: **no discrimination** ("all episodes have high sales growth").
  - *Acceleration* = R(t−24→t) − R(t−24→t−12), i.e., **the share of the 2-year run-up that occurred in the last 12 months**; discriminates at 5% in US and intl. This is the closest published variable to the earthquake "unrelieved build-up".
  - Joint SUR F-test that crash and non-crash have identical characteristics: F=4.91 (US), 5.29 (intl) — rejected.
- **Return forecasting (Table 6).** Δvolatility, age tilt, issuance, B/M, CAPE and acceleration predict 24-month raw and excess returns across the 40 episodes; only Δvolatility and age tilt predict net-of-market returns (industry bubbles are entangled with market-level valuation).
- **Trading strategies (Table 8, US, Panel A; net-of-benchmark, switch to risk-free, 2-yr/4-yr).** Exit on run-up alone: +10%/+15% (t 0.89/1.13, n.s.). Exit if **Δvolatility** high: 11 run-ups, 91% crashed, +10%/+12% (t 1.88/1.67). **Acceleration**: 17 run-ups, 76% crashed, +12%/+17% (t 1.84/2.06). **Age**: 15, 80% crashed, +12%/+16% (t 2.04/2.10). **Issuer %**: 13, 69%, +10%/+13%. **CAPE**: 11, 82%, +9%/+15%. Turnover: 19, 47%, ~0. Sales growth: 20, 52%, negative. Joint F p=0.018–0.05. Intl (Panel B): volatility-level exits +9%/+12% at 1-yr/2-yr (t 2.47/2.58) but −0% at 4 years; CAPE +8% at all horizons (t 2.5–3.1).
- **Pseudo-OOS.** The authors explicitly frame the 107 international episodes (1987–2012) as the out-of-sample test of the US-derived characteristics (text lines 189, 423) — direction and significance broadly replicate (volatility, age, issuance, CAPE, acceleration), turnover fails in both. They also report false-discovery tests (Table 7) and acknowledge "we must be cautious in over attributing statistical significance to any individual characteristic".
- **Free data to replicate.** Ken French 49 industries (monthly VW + number of firms) https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/ftp/49_Industry_Portfolios_CSV.zip (downloaded to `raw/ff49/`, 1926-07 to 2026-09); FF market factor https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/ftp/F-F_Research_Data_Factors_CSV.zip (`raw/ff3/`); Shiller CAPE (free, Shiller's site). NOT free: CRSP firm-level turnover/issuance/age → the age-tilt and issuance variables cannot be rebuilt without WRDS. Price-path variables (run-up size, duration, acceleration, intermediate drawdowns, realised vol from monthly returns) CAN all be built from the French file. No replication repository was found (searched; none on GitHub) — https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:23191 .
- **My replication of the episode count on the free file** (`scripts/count_episodes.py`, output `raw/episode_counts.txt`; counts only, no outcomes computed, to keep the test pre-registrable): GSY-style rule on FF49 VW 1926–2026 → **46 episodes in 12 calendar clusters (gap >2 yrs)**; the count misses GSY's 1928–29 cluster because the 5-year lookback needs data before 1926-07 (fixable by waiving the 5-yr filter before 1931). Raw ≥100% only → 210 episodes/11 clusters; raw & net ≥50% → 179 episodes/7 clusters. Notably **FF49 "Chips" crossed the 100% raw 2-year threshold in 2024-06 through 2024-09 but NOT the 100% net-of-market threshold** — the AI run-up in the French semiconductor industry is a 50%-class, not a 100%-class, net-of-market event so far on this data (descriptive; verify before use).

### 1.2 Credit-and-asset-price crisis predictors

**Greenwood, Hanson, Shleifer & Sørensen (2022), "Predictable Financial Crises", JF 77(2) (NBER w27396).** PDF read (`raw/ghss.txt`): https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27396/w27396.pdf ; VoxEU summary https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/predictable-financial-crises
- Sample: 42 countries, 1950–2016 (unbalanced), Baron-Verner-Xiong crisis chronology (unconditional annual crisis-onset probability **4.0%**; JST 2.6%; 12.0% of country-years are followed by a crisis within 3 years).
- **R-zone**: 3-year change in nonfinancial business credit/GDP in the **top quintile (>8.99 pp)** AND 3-year log real equity price change in the **top tercile (>26.56%)**. Household R-zone: household credit/GDP Δ3 > 7.60 pp AND real home price Δ3 log > 12.67%.
- Crisis probability: 1-year 13.3% in business R-zone (vs 4.5% median bin; diff 8.8 pp, n.s.); **3-year 45.3%** (diff vs median bin 37.4 pp, p=0.006). Household: 14% (1-yr), 37% (3-yr). Regression increment plateaus at 38.2% (business) / 30.1% (household). Only **6% of country-years** are in an R-zone; **63%** of crises were preceded by an R-zone within 3 years. US entered household R-zone 2002; predicted 3-yr probability rose 31% (2002) → 51% (2006) including global R-zone share.
- **Pseudo-OOS**: (i) backward-looking quantile cut-offs from 1973: increment 34.1% (business, vs 38.2% full-sample), 23.8% (household, vs 30.1%); (ii) jackknife cut-offs: similar; (iii) ending in 2000 (dropping GFC): business result unchanged; (iv) Welch-Goyal real-time OOS R² for 3-yr crisis forecast: **4.2% OOS vs 6.7% IS** (business R-zone), 0.9% vs 6.8% (household), 4.5% vs 11.1% (both). Honest reading: real predictability, small R², crises "slow to develop".
- Free data: BIS total credit statistics (nonfinancial corporates, households), JST Macrohistory database (free, https://www.macrohistory.net/database/ — not re-verified this session), BVX crisis list (shared by authors). **Relevance to AI**: the business R-zone needs economy-wide business credit growth; the AI cluster's debt (~$570bn 2026 AI-related issuance projected by Morgan Stanley per secondary sources) is large but what matters for this instrument is the Δ3 of total nonfinancial business credit/GDP — check BIS series before claiming the US is "in the R-zone".

**Baron & Xiong (2017), "Credit Expansion and Neglected Crash Risk", QJE 132(2):713–764.** PDF read (`raw/bx.txt`): https://www.princeton.edu/~wxiong/papers/CreditExpansion.pdf
- 20 developed countries, quarterly 1920–2012; credit expansion = 3-year change in bank credit/GDP (standardised per country). Crash = bank-equity-index quarterly log excess return < −30% within 1/2/3 years (3.2% of quarters).
- **Probit marginal effects (Table III)**: +1 SD credit expansion → crash probability **+2.7 pp (1 yr), +3.3 pp (2 yr), +5.4 pp (3 yr)**, vs unconditional 8.0%/13.9%/19.3% → a 2-SD expansion raises crash odds **~50–70%**. Robust to 5 controls (dividend yield, B/M, term spread, I/K, inflation).
- Mean returns: +1 SD → **−11.4 pp** 3-year excess return; conditional on credit expansion > 95th percentile, mean excess return **−17.9% (2 yr, t −2.02), −37.3% (3 yr, t −2.52)**. No formal OOS split; panel breadth is the defence.

**Jordà, Schularick & Taylor (2015), "Leveraged Bubbles", JME 76:S1–S20 (NBER w21486).** PDF read (`raw/jst.txt`): https://www.nber.org/papers/w21486.pdf
- 17 countries, 1870–2013. Bubble = log real asset price > +1 SD above country HP trend (λ=100, annual) AND a ≥15% (0.15 log) real fall within the following 3 years. "Leveraged" = bank credit growth above historical mean in the expansion. Counts: 26 equity-bubble episodes in 46 financial-crisis recessions; 43 in 120 normal recessions.
- **Recession depth (Table 5, no controls; Table 6 with controls)**: no-bubble recession −1.9% yr-1, +6.1% by yr-5. Equity bubble + low credit: ≈ no extra drag (yr-5 −1.3, n.s.; with controls −2.4). **Equity bubble + high credit: yr-3 −3.7**, yr-5 −3.6* (controls: −4.5***, −3.7**). Housing + high credit: yr-5 −8.0** (controls −8.5***). Post-WW2 (Table 7): equity+high credit yr-5 −1.5 (n.s.); housing+high credit −7.5***.
- Takeaway for AI: an *unleveraged* equity bubble bursting is historically cheap for the macroeconomy; the question is whether the AI build-out has become credit-financed at the economy-wide level (JST would say: measure bank credit growth, not just bond issuance).

**Greenwood & Hanson (2013), "Issuer Quality and Corporate Bond Returns", RFS 26(6):1483–1525.** Record: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:26:y:2013:i:6:p:1483-1525 ; HBS WP https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:hbs:wpaper:11-065
- Predictor: share of nonfinancial corporate bond issuance rated speculative-grade (HY share) and an EDF-based issuer-quality index. Finding: when issuance is disproportionately low-quality, subsequent 1–3 yr excess returns on HY and IG are low/negative; **univariate R² up to ~30% at 3 years for HY**; a decline in issuer quality is "a more reliable signal of credit market overheating than rapid aggregate credit growth" (abstract). A working-paper summary cites a 1-SD deterioration ≈ 1.66 pp/yr lower HY financing cost — **UNVERIFIED** (could not open core.ac.uk PDF: HTTP 403; coefficients not confirmed). No OOS test confirmed.
- Free data: SIFMA/FRED aggregate HY vs IG issuance volumes exist (annual, free); a monthly HY share back to 1983 requires Mergent FISD (paid). Relevance: the AI debt wave is predominantly IG hyperscaler paper plus unrated private credit for neoclouds — the HY share of public issuance may UNDERSTATE the quality deterioration (private credit is invisible to it).

**López-Salido, Stein & Zakrajšek (2017), "Credit-Market Sentiment and the Business Cycle", QJE 132(3):1373–1426.** https://www.nber.org/papers/21879 ; FEDS https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/credit-market-sentiment-and-the-business-cycle.htm
- US 1929–2013. Sentiment = fitted value from HY share + (credit) spreads; elevated sentiment in t−2 predicts lower GDP, investment, employment in t and t+1 via a two-step regression. Two-year lag; stock-market-sentiment analogue does NOT work. Effect sizes **UNVERIFIED** (full text not opened). Free inputs: Moody's Baa–Aaa spread (FRED BAA, AAA from 1919, monthly, free: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAA ), HY share as above.

**Krishnamurthy & Muir (2017/2025), "How Credit Cycles across a Financial Crisis", JF 80(3) (NBER w23850).** https://www.nber.org/papers/w23850.pdf
- Pre-crisis: spreads unusually LOW while credit growth is HIGH ("frothy"); crisis severity is forecast by the *jump* in spreads interacted with pre-crisis credit growth. Numbers not extracted (**UNVERIFIED** beyond abstract). Free inputs: Baa–Aaa or HY OAS (FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2 from 1996).

### 1.3 Investment-based predictors (capex → returns)

- **Cochrane (1991)**, JF 46(1):209–237 https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/cochrane_production_based_JF-9gra.pdf — aggregate investment/capital ratio forecasts returns via the production-based Euler equation; magnitudes not extracted (**UNVERIFIED**). Conceptual anchor only.
- **Lamont (2000)**, JF 55(6):2719–2745 https://www.nber.org/papers/w6973 — Commerce Dept survey of *planned* capex: planned investment growth negatively predicts excess stock returns; plans explain >75% of realised aggregate investment growth. Return-regression R² **UNVERIFIED**. Related, Li-Wang-Yu (2021, JME) bottom-up aggregate expected investment growth: IS adj. R² 18.2%, **OOS R² 14.4%** for annual market returns (secondary source via search; **UNVERIFIED** against the paper) — this is the one aggregate investment predictor with a reported OOS R², and the series is rebuildable from Compustat (paid).
- **Titman, Wei & Xie (2004)**, JFQA 39(4):677–700 https://www.nber.org/papers/w9951.pdf (PDF read, `raw/twx_capex.txt`): abnormal capex (CI = capex/avg of prior 3 yrs) spread portfolio (low−high CI) earns **0.168%/month ≈ 2.0%/yr** benchmark-adjusted (t=2.91), 1973–1996; 5-year cumulative 9.9%; stronger for high-cash-flow/low-debt firms (0.227% vs 0.078%/mo). A 2026 practitioner update says direction holds 2006–2026 with weaker effect (https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2026/06/10/capital-expenditures-tech-sector-good-bad-or-ugly — secondary).
- **Cooper, Gulen & Schill (2008)**, JF 63(4): asset-growth decile spread **EW 19.5%/yr (9.1% vs −10.4% risk-adjusted), VW ≈ 8.4%/yr**, 1968–2003; a critique attributes part to CRSP delisting-return omission (https://core.ac.uk/download/13246819.pdf). Summaries: https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1499 .
- **Hou, Xue & Zhang q-factor**: investment factor (I/A) is a core factor; q5 expected-growth factor 0.84%/mo (t=10.27) per Alpha Architect summary https://alphaarchitect.com/building-a-better-q-factor-asset-pricing-model/ — premium for the I/A factor itself **UNVERIFIED** this session. Factor series are free from Lu Zhang's global-q site (not re-checked).
- **Arif & Lee (2014)**, RFS 27(11):3241–3279 https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2195336 — aggregate corporate investment peaks coincide with positive sentiment and are followed by lower market returns, more earnings disappointments, lower macro growth; holds across developed countries and survives controls. Magnitudes **UNVERIFIED** (paywalled).
- **Bottom line**: the firm-level investment effect is one of the most replicated anomalies (2–8%/yr VW); the *aggregate* effect is real but modest and horizon-dependent; **no paper tests sector-level capex intensity peaks (telecom 2000, shale 2014) as a return-reversal signal in a formal OOS design** — the "capital cycle" (Marathon/Chancellor, *Capital Returns* 2016, https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-137-57165-6) is a practitioner framework, not a tested instrument. BCG's 175-industrial study found no consistent capex-level/return correlation (https://www.bcg.com/publications/2017/corporate-development-finance-large-capex-three-ways-beat-odds-capex-investments). Capex guidance: Bae-Biddle-Park (Mgmt Sci 2022) is about managerial learning, not returns (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3896); an Aalto bachelor's thesis finds *downward* AI-capex guidance revisions earn positive announcement returns at five megacaps 2019–2025 (tiny n; https://aaltodoc3.org.aalto.fi/items/155134b0-cd5c-4965-904c-d05553a93155).

### 1.4 Sornette / LPPLS

- **What it is**: fit ln p(t) = A + B(tc−t)^m + C(tc−t)^m cos(ω ln(tc−t) − φ) to a window; "LPPLS confidence indicator" = fraction of nested windows (e.g., 30–120 trading days ending at t) whose calibrated (m, ω, …) pass filter conditions (README example: 0<m<1, 2<ω<15, O_min 2.5, D_min 0.5) — https://github.com/Boulder-Investment-Technologies/lppls (MIT licence, PyPI `lppls`, Nasdaq dot-com example dataset; **README reports no OOS validation**).
- **Proponents' evidence**: Financial Bubble Experiment (sealed, date-stamped forecasts from Nov 2009; https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.0454 ) — mixed self-reported outcomes (IBOVESPA/gold regime change inside window; cotton did not); Chinese bubbles paper admits a failed prediction and that the record is "insufficient to conclude" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.1007); Shanghai 2015 real-time paper (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:chf:rpseri:rp1531); two-century S&P500 study claims fewer false positives than GSADF (in-sample; https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/53236); Demirer-Demos-Gupta-Sornette 2019 QF 19(5):843–858 says the indicators "captured ex-ante" Black Monday, dot-com, subprime (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:19:y:2019:i:5:p:843-858 — OOS metrics not visible).
- **Critiques**: Brée & Joseph (2013), IRFA 30:287–297 (preprint read via ar5iv https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1002.1010): Hang Seng 1970–2008, 11 crashes (≥25% fall within 60 weekdays from a 262-day high); only **7 of 11** have best-fit parameters in Johansen-Sornette ranges; the posited mechanism "does not" underlie the model, data support "only partially"; the 2007 fit did predict the crash day. Brée-Challet-Peirano (2013, QF) "Prediction accuracy and sloppiness of log-periodic functions" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.2010): tc is a sloppy parameter, a non-oscillating super-exponential gives similar tc estimates, earlier "five bubbles, no false positives" claims used lenient crash criteria. Grobys (2026 preprint; **UNVERIFIED**) argues ADF-residual tests on bounded-parameter fits have inflated size. Monschang-Wilfling (2021) show PSY/GSADF date-stamping is frequency-sensitive and low-powered under GARCH (https://www.wiwi.uni-muenster.de/cqe/en/publication/158768).
- **Honest assessment**: no independent, pre-registered, audited OOS hit-rate/false-alarm table exists in the public record; the method is identifiable only weakly (sloppy tc, multiple local minima); its confidence indicator is a *descriptive* measure of super-exponential + oscillatory fit quality. Usable as ONE descriptive panel on a dashboard (free, open code), NOT as a scored predictor.

### 1.5 SOC / power laws / dragon kings
- Gabaix (2009) "Power Laws in Economics and Finance" https://www.nber.org/papers/w14299 : cubic law of returns (tail exponent ≈3) — a *distributional* fact, unconditional, no forecast content. Johansen-Sornette "Large stock market price drawdowns are outliers" https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0010050 and "Dragon-kings" https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4290 : the largest drawdowns deviate from the exponential/stretched-exponential body; claim that ~2/3 of drawdown dragon-kings were preceded by LPPL bubbles (in-sample, ex post). A Sornette-supervised thesis found no dragon kings at 1-minute resolution. Bak-style SOC gives no calendar information by construction (criticality is stationary). **Verdict: descriptive. The ε-drawdown definition (positive moves smaller than ε·σ do not end a drawdown; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.5037) IS useful operationally** for counting intermediate corrections in the earthquake test.

### 1.6 Stage models (Minsky/Kindleberger; Perez)
- Kindleberger himself: distress "is not an easily measured condition" (quoted at https://www.hussmanfunds.com/wmc/wmc130805.htm); Loomis Sayles: the Minsky model is "qualitative (as opposed to quantitative!)" (https://www.loomissayles.com/internet/InternetData.nsf/0/B1F8BC5B2A8A05A285257AC6004AFE01/$FILE/Liquidity-Cycle.pdf). The only empirical operationalisation found is a survey-sentiment working paper (TKK dp173, https://ideas.repec.org/p/tkk/dpaper/dp173.html; abstract only). **No dated, pre-specified stage indicator exists.**
- Perez (2002) *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital*: installation → frenzy → turning point → deployment; ICT installation from 1971, "frantic nineties" frenzy, turning point 2000/2008 and now described by Perez as "the longest turning point in history" (https://medium.com/iipp-blog/a-long-delayed-golden-age-or-why-has-the-ict-installation-period-lasted-so-long-b3471a0aaaeb ; Stratechery discussion https://stratechery.com/2021/the-death-and-birth-of-technological-revolutions/). Dates are assigned ex post and have been revised; **no falsifiable operationalisation or academic OOS test found** (Silverberg's long-wave survey is the closest critical source: https://www.merit.unu.edu/publications/rmpdf/2003/rm2003-015.pdf). Use as narrative framing only.

### 1.7 Drawdown-based measures / the earthquake claim
- **Direct literature: none found.** No paper tests whether the count/depth of intermediate corrections during a run-up predicts the eventual crash size. Searched: "intermediate drawdowns", "corrections reduce crash risk", "price path smoothness bubble". Nearest results are drawdown risk-measurement papers (Geboers-Depaire-Straetmans 2023 J. Risk https://risk.net/journal-of-risk/7958269/peak-to-valley-drawdowns-insights-into-extreme-path-dependent-market-risk) and bull/bear dating algorithms (Tor Vergata RP576 https://ceistorvergata.it/RePEc/rpaper/RP576.pdf).
- **Indirect, supportive**: (i) GSY acceleration (above) — convex run-ups crash more; (ii) **Danielsson, Valenzuela & Zer (2018), RFS 31(7):2774–2805** https://www.riskresearch.org/papers/DanielssonValenzuelaZer2015/ — 60 countries, up to 211 years: *prolonged* low volatility (deviation below a slow trend) predicts banking crises in-sample AND out-of-sample; a 1% fall in volatility below trend sustained 5 years → +1.01% crisis probability; **AUROC 0.76 [0.72, 0.80]** (FEDS Note https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/low-risk-as-a-predictor-of-financial-crises-20180509.htm); raw volatility level does NOT predict; (iii) Brunnermeier-Sannikov "volatility paradox" (theory: low exogenous risk → higher leverage → bigger endogenous crash; https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/document/123); (iv) Minsky's "stability breeds instability" (untested slogan); (v) Brunnermeier-Rother-Schnabel (2020, RFS 33(9)) bank systemic risk rises with bubble *length and size* (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:33:y:2020:i:9:p:4272-4317); (vi) an ECB WP on housing booms since 1980 reports boom persistence/magnitude correlate with bust size and GDP cost (https://ECB.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1071.pdf; authors not confirmed).
- **Indirect, contrary**: GSY find volatility *level* not discriminating in the US and *rising* volatility (not calm) preceding crashes — i.e., US industry bubbles that crash get MORE jittery in their last year, which cuts against "quiet = dangerous" at the 12-month horizon. The earthquake claim and the GSY Δvol result can both be true only if "no 10%+ corrections for 24 months" and "rising monthly vol in the final year" are different objects (plausible: vol can rise via up-moves). The test must measure both.
- Goetzmann (2016) "Bubble Investing" (NBER w21693, read `raw/goetzmann_bubble_investing.txt`): 42 markets 1900–2014, 3,514 market-years; after a 1-year real doubling, P(halve next year)=6.9%, P(halve within 5 yrs)=17.2%; after a 100% rise over 3 years, 4.6% / 9.8% (unconditional 5-yr 8.4%). Doubling again is about as likely as halving. **Base-rate anchor for the "market" (not industry) level.**

## 2. THE EARTHQUAKE TEST — a pre-registrable study on free data

**Data.** Ken French 49 industries, VW monthly, 1926-07 → latest, plus "Number of Firms" (≥10 filter) and FF market return (Mkt-RF + RF). Already downloaded: `raw/ff49/49_Industry_Portfolios.csv`, `raw/ff3/F-F_Research_Data_Factors.csv`. Shiller CAPE as the one market-level covariate. Nothing paid.

**Episode definition (frozen).** Primary: GSY rule — 24-month VW return ≥100% raw AND ≥100% net-of-market, 60-month raw ≥50% (waived before 1931-07), ≥10 firms; signal months within one industry separated by ≤24 months merge into one episode; episode date t0 = first signal month. Secondary (power): 50%/50%. Expected n (counted, `raw/episode_counts.txt`): **46 primary episodes in 12 calendar clusters (1926–2024)**, ~50 after restoring 1928–29; **179 secondary episodes in 7 clusters**. International GICS sectors (GSY's 107) are NOT free → the OOS extension needs Compustat Global or an ETF/MSCI sector proxy from ~1995 (shorter, fewer episodes).

**Per-episode predictors (all computed on data ≤ t0).**
1. Run-up size: 24-month net-of-market return (continuous) and 36-month.
2. Duration: months since the industry's last 20% drawdown (from a trailing 36-month high), capped at 60.
3. **Intermediate corrections** (the earthquake variable): (a) count of distinct ε-drawdowns ≥10% during the 24-month window (ε = 1 monthly SD so small up-months don't end a drawdown); (b) count ≥20%; (c) deepest intermediate drawdown; (d) "unrelieved share" = fraction of the 24 months spent within 5% of the running high.
4. Realised volatility: SD of monthly returns over months t0−12..t0 and its change vs t0−24..t0−12 (GSY Δvol analogue, free version).
5. Acceleration: R(24m) − R(first 12m of the 24) (GSY definition).
6. Covariates: market 24-month return; Shiller CAPE; 10-yr Treasury change (FRED, free); decade dummies; cluster id.

**Outcomes.** Y1 = max drawdown from the t0 level over months t0+1..t0+24 (continuous); Y2 = 1{Y1 ≤ −40%} (GSY crash); Y3 = 24-month net-of-market return.

**Pre-registered hypotheses (one-sided, α=0.05, no peeking).**
- H1 (earthquake, binary): episodes in the bottom tercile of intermediate-correction count have P(crash) ≥ **15 pp** higher than the top tercile.
- H2 (earthquake, continuous): rank-correlation between "unrelieved share" and max drawdown ≤ −0.25.
- H3 (GSY replication on free data): acceleration tercile spread in P(crash) ≥ 15 pp — this is the positive control; if H3 fails on the free data the pipeline is wrong.
- H4 (discriminant validity): intermediate-correction count adds to a logit already containing run-up size + acceleration + Δvol (likelihood-ratio p<0.05). This is the hypothesis that actually matters: the analogy is only useful if it is not just acceleration under another name.
- Pre-specified null: run-up size predicts P(crash) (GSY) but NOT 24-month mean return — if we find return predictability from size alone, suspect a bug.

**Validation.** Leave-one-*cluster*-out (not leave-one-episode-out: the 1929, 1937, 1968, 1980, 1999–2000, 2007, 2021–22 clusters share a market crash and are not independent draws); report the OOS Brier score and AUROC of the pre-specified logit vs a base-rate forecast; Welch-Goyal expanding-window OOS R² for Y1 starting at the 10th episode; permutation p-values across episodes within cluster; the 50%/50% sample as a robustness/power panel, NOT as a second chance.

**Power (simulated, `raw/power_calc.txt`).** With n≈40 (terciles of 13), base rate ~50%: a 15 pp difference has **power 0.17**; 25 pp 0.33; 35 pp 0.54; 45 pp 0.74. Halves of 20: 15 pp → 0.22, 35 pp → 0.70. Pooling GSY's 107 international episodes (terciles of ~36): 15 pp → 0.40, 25 pp → 0.73. The 50%/50% panel (terciles of 60): 15 pp → 0.52, 25 pp → 0.88. **Conclusion: at the GSY threshold the test can only detect a LARGE effect (≥35 pp); a null result is uninformative; pre-register the 50%/50% panel as the primary powered test and the 100%/100% as confirmatory, or accept that the study is descriptive.**

**Confounders to declare.** (i) market-wide crashes (1929–32, 1937, 1973–74, 2000–02, 2008, 2020, 2022) convert *any* run-up into a "crash" — use net-of-market drawdown as a secondary outcome and cluster fixed effects; (ii) rate regimes (1980 commodity cluster vs 2021 cluster); (iii) the 1929 and 2000 clusters contribute 9 of GSY's 21 crashes — the result must survive dropping each; (iv) the French file re-forms industries only in June and excludes firms without book data in early years → run-ups in new-firm-heavy industries are *understated* vs GSY (97.6% correlation, but the bubble months are exactly where they differ); (v) survivorship: FF49 industry definitions were designed in 1997 with knowledge of history; (vi) overlapping signal months → the merge rule must be frozen before looking at outcomes.

**The gulf.** Even if H1–H4 hold across 46 US episodes and 107 international ones, the AI cluster is ONE draw from a distribution whose estimated conditional crash probability will carry a ±15–20 pp interval; and the cluster spans several FF49 industries (Chips, Hardware, Software, BusSv/ElcEq for power equipment) at once, as 1999–2000 did — the same correlated-draw problem GSY flag. "Predictive across industries historically" licenses a *conditional probability*, not a statement about this episode's outcome; the dashboard must display it as such.

## 4. Base rates and overfitting — the critical section

- **How many independent tech-capex episodes exist?** At the industry level, GSY's 40 run-ups collapse into ~12 calendar clusters (my count on free data: 46 episodes / 12 clusters). Of those, the "new-technology capex boom with a supplier/recipient structure" sub-type is at most **five**: 1920s utilities/radio/autos, 1960s computers-conglomerates, 1980 energy, 1999–2000 telecom-internet, 2021–22 EV/crypto-adjacent. Perez's framework counts five technological revolutions in 240 years. Any predictor "fitted" to the tech-capex sub-type has n≈5 and is storytelling; any predictor fitted to the 40-episode industry panel and then *interpreted* through the sub-type inherits the panel's power (low) and loses the sub-type's specificity.
- **Why in-sample stories dominate here.** GSY study ~12 characteristics on 40 episodes and themselves warn about multiple comparisons; the Sornette literature's "no false positives" claims rest on lenient crash definitions (Brée-Challet-Peirano); the capital-cycle literature is written by its practitioners; every stage model is dated ex post. The R-zone's OOS R² of 4.2% is what honest predictability of rare crises looks like.
- **What a defensible instrument looks like.** (1) ≤5 pre-registered signals with published thresholds, each with a citation to a panel result above: run-up size (GSY), acceleration (GSY), Δvolatility (GSY), intermediate-correction count (the user's hypothesis, H1/H4 above), and ONE credit signal (BIS Δ3 business credit/GDP top quintile >8.99 pp per GHSS; or HY share/private-credit share as a flagged-unverified proxy). (2) Scored on the FF49 panel with leave-cluster-out validation, Brier score vs base rate published BEFORE the AI reading is computed. (3) Applied to the AI cluster as ONE observation: output = conditional P(≥40% drawdown within 24 months) with a bootstrap interval, plus the panel's realised calibration curve so the reader can see how wide "wide" is. (4) An explicit statement that the 100%/100% net-of-market threshold has/has not been met by each FF49 industry in the cluster (Chips met raw-100% in 2024 but not net-100% — see §1.1).
- **What the honest deliverable is.** A *pressure gauge* (accumulated net run-up × unrelieved share × months since last 20% correction, each shown as a percentile of the historical panel) + a *conditional probability panel* (GSY unconditional 14% → 53% at 100%, 80% at 150%; R-zone 4% → 45% at 3 yrs if credit qualifies) + a set of **falsifiable throttle-back tripwires** stated in advance (e.g., "if FF49 Chips net-of-market 24m return exceeds 100% AND acceleration > 50% of the run-up AND no ε-drawdown ≥10% in 24 months, the gauge reads 'red' and the position rule is X") — never a crash date, never a tc.

## (a) Two candidate study designs (one maximal, one minimal)

**MAXIMAL — "Panel earthquake test + credit overlay"**
- Data: FF49 VW monthly 1926–2026 + FF market + Shiller CAPE + FRED 10y; BIS credit (nonfinancial corporates, households, US) for the R-zone overlay; CBOE SKEW/VIX as descriptive panels; optional paid extension (Compustat Global sectors 1985–) for the OOS international leg.
- Pre-registered hypotheses: H1–H4 in §2 at 50%/50% (primary, n≈180) and 100%/100% (confirmatory, n≈46); positive control H3; R-zone overlay H5: "episodes occurring while the US is in the business R-zone have ≥15 pp higher P(crash)".
- Expected n: 46/179 episodes; 12/7 clusters. Power: 0.17 for 15 pp at n=46; 0.52 at n=179 (terciles).
- Decision it supports: whether the intermediate-correction count earns a slot in the pressure gauge (H4) and what conditional probability + interval to display for the AI cluster.
- Cannot do: time the crash; separate the AI cluster from the market; replicate GSY's issuance/age/turnover (CRSP) — so it cannot test whether the earthquake variable is a proxy for young-firm issuance.
- Cost: ~2 weeks of one analyst; two independent verifiers (BLOCKING-tier because Casey may act on the number).

**MINIMAL — "GSY replication + one new variable"**
- Data: FF49 + FF market only.
- Pre-registered hypotheses: H3 (replicate GSY's 20%→53%→80% crash-probability ladder and the acceleration spread on free data) and H1 only (bottom vs top tercile of ε-drawdown count, 100%/100%). Two hypotheses, two numbers, one table, one figure (crash probability by run-up size and by correction count, with binomial CIs).
- Expected n: 46 episodes / 12 clusters. Power 0.17–0.54 depending on true effect — stated up front as "this is a descriptive replication plus a pilot".
- Decision it supports: go/no-go on building the maximal study; and a sanity-check that the free data reproduce GSY before any dashboard quotes GSY's numbers.
- Cannot do: control for credit, acceleration or volatility jointly (no H4), so it cannot say the earthquake variable is *incremental*.
- Cost: 2–3 days; one verifier.

## (b) Five facts most relevant to the decision
1. A 100% 2-year net-of-market industry run-up raises the 24-month probability of a 40% drawdown from 14% to **53%** (80% at 150%), but leaves the *average* 24-month return ≈0 — the problem is tail risk, not expected return (GSY Table 3).
2. What separates run-ups that crash from those that don't is **acceleration, rising volatility, issuance, young-firm concentration and high market CAPE — not turnover and not sales growth** (GSY Table 4/5; replicated in 107 international episodes).
3. Economy-wide credit is the multiplier: business R-zone (Δ3 credit/GDP >8.99 pp AND equity Δ3 >26.6%) → 3-yr crisis probability **45%** vs 4% unconditional, OOS R² 4.2% (GHSS); unleveraged equity bubbles cost ~0 extra GDP, leveraged ones −3.6 to −4.5% at year 3–5 (JST Table 5/6).
4. **Prolonged low volatility (not level) predicts crises OOS, AUROC 0.76** (DVZ 2018) — the only published result that is structurally the earthquake claim, but at the country-banking level, not the industry level.
5. On the free French file, **FF49 Chips crossed GSY's raw-100% threshold in Jun–Sep 2024 but never the net-of-market-100% threshold**; LPPLS has open code but no audited OOS record; the earthquake hypothesis has **no direct test in the literature** and a 15 pp effect is undetectable at n≈46 (power 0.17).

## (b) Five open questions
1. Is the US (or the hyperscaler-weighted economy) actually in GHSS's business R-zone on BIS data — i.e., has nonfinancial business credit/GDP risen >8.99 pp over 3 years? (Decides whether the credit multiplier applies; checkable in an hour, free.)
2. Does the ε-drawdown count carry information *beyond* GSY acceleration and Δvolatility (H4)? Without this the analogy is a relabelling.
3. Can the GSY ladder (20%/53%/80%) be reproduced on the French file given the 97.6% correlation and the June-rebalance/new-listing differences — especially for the 1999–2000 and 2021 clusters?
4. How should the AI cluster be mapped to FF49 industries (Chips, Hardw, Softw, ElcEq, BusSv?) and how is a multi-industry episode scored — as one draw or several correlated ones?
5. Is the circular/vendor-financed and private-credit portion of AI funding visible in ANY free credit series (HY share, BIS, Fed Z.1), or is the credit leg of the instrument blind to exactly the financing the thesis worries about?
