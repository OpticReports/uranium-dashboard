# Adversarial logic-and-design review — AI capex cycle scoping (review2)

Reviewer: independent of finders A/B/C and the orchestrator's scoping. Date 2026-10-31.
Scratch: `review2/confound_checks.py`, `review2/within_vol_checks.py`, `review2/damage_timing.py` (all run with `python3 -I` on the existing `scoping/out/episodes.json`, `scoping/raw/`, `scoping/raw_fred/`). Number spot-checks are NOT redone here (separate verifier).

## VERDICT: PASS_WITH_FIXES

The three finder passes are careful and mostly honest about n. The reasoning fails in four places that would mislead a reader acting with money: (1) finder A's headline "the user's causal order is reversed" is an over-claim — the record re-orders one link and otherwise supports the chain; (2) the earthquake scoping, in both its first and revised readings, draws a conclusion from a panel whose outcome variable is mostly "did the market crash", whose effective n is ~27 calendar clusters (5 crash clusters), and in which the correction-count effect has no sign under cluster resampling; (3) the R-zone chart's title ("the credit multiplier is NOT armed") is true of the instrument and false as a reassurance, because the same instrument did not arm in 2000Q1 either; (4) the recommended build would score the AI cluster on a panel rule the AI cluster does not currently satisfy, and would display a crash probability with a binomial interval that understates the real uncertainty by a factor of several. All four are fixable by rewording and by two scope cuts; none requires new research.

## Findings, ranked

### BLOCKING

**B1. Earthquake scoping — both readings over-interpret a panel whose outcome is the market's crash and whose effective n is ~5 crash clusters.**
Claim attacked: (first reading) "the naive earthquake analogy runs the wrong way; smooth run-ups were not the dangerous ones; size and volatility were"; (revised) "confounded by volatility, not refuted; the pre-registered test must hold volatility and acceleration fixed".
Counter-argument, with the computations:
- Outcome contamination. Spearman(sector future max-DD, market future max-DD) = **+0.76**; 60% of the 104 "crashes" coincide with a ≥30% market drawdown; when the market did not fall ≥30%, the crash rate is 12% (vs 89% when it did). The outcome is largely a cluster-level market event.
- Effective n. 408 episodes fall into **27 calendar clusters** (gap >12m); five clusters carry crash rates ≥50% (1928-29, 1935-37, 1967-68, 1971-72, 2007-08). The 1933-34 cluster alone is 35 episodes with an 11% crash rate; 1995-2005 is 38 episodes.
- Rebound confound is real and runs the OTHER way from the one the orchestrator expected: run-ups that began with the market ≥40% below its 3-year high (n=83, 1933, 1942-46, 2009-10) crashed **7%** vs **30%** for the rest; Spearman(market DD at window start, future DD) = **−0.31**. These post-crash doublings are the safest run-ups in the panel, not a source of spurious crashes. Removing them (variant B) leaves the correction-count association intact (+0.14), so the rebound story does not rescue the analogy.
- Volatility and corrections are the same variable: Spearman(corr10, vol) = **+0.75**, Spearman(corr10, max_int_dd) = **+0.82**. Stratifying on vol and then varying correction count conditions on a near-collinear window statistic; whatever is left is residual composition.
- The within-volatility reversal: low-vol 15/106 vs 1/30 (Fisher p=0.20); mid-vol 17/40 vs 27/96 (p=0.11); high-vol 3/3 vs 41/133 (p=0.03, n=3). Mantel-Haenszel OR 2.65 pooled. Composition in the low-vol cell is identical (raw24 1.10 vs 1.10, accel 0.45 vs 0.45) so it is not size in disguise — but **6 of the 15 low-vol zero-correction crashes are 1928 and 3 are 1968**: the "reversal" is the 1929 crash seen through smooth 1928 run-ups. In the high-vol cell the 3 zero-correction episodes have raw24 1.98 vs 1.30 — that one IS size.
- Decisive check: logit crash ~ raw24 + vol + corr0 + accel with a **cluster bootstrap over the 27 clusters**: raw24 coefficient 2.9 (90% CI 1.7 to 4.3, robust); vol 0.07 (CI −0.6 to +4.3, not robust); corr0 +0.08 (CI **−0.61 to +0.80**, P(>0)=0.65); accel −0.26 (CI −1.4 to +0.7). The correction-count effect has no sign once clusters are the unit.
- Panel F (GSY primary, n=60): Spearman −0.14 has a standard error of ~0.13. Noise.
Does the claim survive? **First reading FALLS** ("runs the wrong way" is not supported once clusters are the unit). **Revised reading SURVIVES WITH REWORDING**: "confounded by volatility" is true but incomplete; the honest statement is "undetermined at any power this data can supply". Is the within-vol reversal Simpson's worth building on? **No** — it is one cluster (1928-29) plus three observations; it is not evidence for the analogy, it is the absence of evidence against it.
The single statement the memo may make about the analogy: *"On 100 years of US industry data, whether a +100% run-up had intermediate corrections does not reliably predict whether it crashed: the raw association runs against the analogy, the volatility-matched association runs with it, and neither has a determinate sign once the 27 calendar clusters are treated as the unit of evidence (cluster-bootstrap 90% interval for the zero-correction effect −0.6 to +0.8 log-odds). The analogy is untested, not refuted and not confirmed; run-up SIZE is the only feature that survives."*
Minimal additional cut that would settle it: none at this n will "settle" it. The informative cut is (a) outcome = net-of-market drawdown, (b) unit = cluster (cluster-robust or leave-cluster-out), (c) run-ups measured net of market, (d) report the 1928-29 cluster separately. If the sign still straddles zero — expected — the analogy is dropped from the gauge and kept as a displayed descriptive (months since last 10%/20% correction), not a scored input.

**B2. The recommended gauge scores the AI cluster on a rule the cluster does not meet, and the probability it would display is not the probability the reader will hear.**
Claim attacked: (ii) "a pre-registered pressure gauge scored on the FF49 panel … with the AI cluster as one out-of-sample observation".
Counter-argument: as of 2026-09, FF49 Hardw is +149% raw / **+84% net**, Chips +91% raw / +41% net. Neither meets the GSY primary rule (≥100% net) that generates the quoted 53%; Chips no longer meets even the raw-100% rule. The AI cluster is in-domain only for the 50%/50% secondary panel, whose crash rate is 27% (variant C) and whose conditional spread by size is modest. Any dashboard that shows "53%" or "P(crash)=40% ±7pp (Wilson)" next to the AI cluster is (a) quoting a rule the cluster has not triggered and (b) quoting a binomial interval for a panel of 27 clusters as if it had 136 independent draws. The reader will hear a crash probability. What makes the reader believe an unwarranted number: a single headline percentage; a Wilson interval; the word "out-of-sample"; the 1999-2000 overlay aligned on its ex-post peak.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING AND TWO CUTS**: (1) the gauge displays *percentile ranks* of the AI industries' run-up size and volatility within the historical panel and the panel's realised crash rate for that percentile bin, with a cluster-bootstrap interval — never a point probability for "AI"; (2) the acceleration and correction-count features are displayed, not scored, until H3/H4 pass; (3) the GSY 53%/80% ladder may be quoted only with "the AI industries have not met this rule net of market" in the same sentence.

### SERIOUS

**S1. "Equity peaks before capex, so the user's causal order is reversed" — re-orders one link, does not refute the chain.**
Claim attacked: finder A bottom line 1 and §4A.
Counter-arguments: (a) Measurement artefact? Partly not: in the two episodes with high-frequency capex measures the lead is real — telecom real comm-equipment peaked Q4-2000 vs equity Mar-2000 on quarterly data (+9m), rig count peaked Dec-1981 vs Oil equity Nov-1980 on weekly data (+13m). For UK-1845, Japan-1989 and 1929 the capex series are annual and the "lead" is within measurement resolution plus a construction lag; those three should be labelled "coincident-to-lagging, annual data". (b) Capex put-in-place is a flow that lags orders by 6-18 months by construction; equity prices orders. Equity leading capex is therefore the null, not a finding, and "capex is a lagging indicator of the bust" is a tautology about the series chosen. (c) The decisive point: the user's chain ends in "crash", and finder A conflates "equity peak" with "crash". Computed on the FF49 monthly data (`damage_timing.py`): the share of the sector's peak-to-trough loss that came AFTER the capex peak was **44% (Telcm 2000), 40% (Chips 2000), 40% (Oil 1980-82), 56-61% (Util 1929-30), 55% (ElcEq 1929-30), 49% (Oil 2014)**; the share of the MARKET's loss after the capex peak was **61-67%** in every debt-financed episode; and the remaining drawdown from the capex-peak level was 53% (Telcm), 68% (Chips), 71-88% (1929 utilities/electrical). The recession started after the capex peak in 1929, 1981 and 2001. So the record is: sector equity tops (unobservable in real time) → capex throttle (observable) → the majority of the market's loss, the recession, the canaries, the spread peak. That IS the user's chain with the first link re-ordered.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING**: replace "the user's causal order is reversed" with "the sector's own equity peak precedes the capex peak by 0-13 months on the episodes with quarterly-or-better capex data; the capex throttle is a mid-bust confirmer that preceded roughly two-thirds of the broad market's loss and the recession in every debt-financed episode". What to watch instead of capex, and is it measurable: vendor guidance (n=1 lead: Lucent −2m; Nortel was +11m — do not call vendor guidance "the" leading canary on one observation), sector profits (telecom profits fell from 1996 — a 4-year lead is not a signal), structures investment (−3m, n=1). Measurable today: finder B's guidance ledger, accrued capex (`CapitalExpendituresIncurredButNotYetPaid`), purchase commitments, TSMC monthly revenue, Nvidia DSO. Yes, but each is a new series with no historical calibration; they are tripwires, not predictors.

**S2. R-zone reading — true of the instrument, misleading as a conclusion.**
Claim attacked: chart title "The credit multiplier is NOT armed" and "aggregate leverage is not where this cycle's risk sits".
Counter-arguments: (a) The GHSS business R-zone **did not arm in 2000Q1 either** (+8.3 < +8.99) and a −45% market / −78% Nasdaq / recession followed. The instrument is calibrated to BVX banking crises (2008-type), not to tech-capex busts (2000-type). "Not armed" therefore says: a 2008 is not set up; it says nothing about a 2000. The chart's title invites the second reading. (b) Denominator: Z.1 nonfinancial business debt/GDP is 73.6% now vs 63.9% in 2000Q1 — a higher LEVEL, with the 3-year change negative because 2021-23 nominal GDP inflated the denominator (and 2020Q2 base effects). Business debt growth has accelerated from +2.2% yoy (2024Q1) to **+5.1% (2026Q2)**, corporate bonds +5.4%, still below nominal GDP +6.3%. "Not armed, but turning" is the honest reading; at +7% debt growth vs +5% nominal GDP the 3-year change reaches only ~+4-5pp by 2029, so the R-zone will stay off through any plausible 2027 throttle — i.e. this signal will contribute nothing to the AI reading while having been fitted to 1929/2008. (c) Sector invisibility: comm-equipment was 1.2% of GDP in 2000 and $192bn of telecom HY 1997-2000 was ~1.9% of 2000 GDP; the existing tab's $682bn of AI-trade issuance 2024-26 is ~2.1% of GDP — the sector credit leg is already telecom-scale in GDP terms, IG rather than HY, and invisible in a 3-year aggregate change because other borrowers deleveraged. Finance-lease liabilities (MSFT $71bn), SPV debt (Hyperion), private credit to neoclouds and vendor equity (NVDA $47.9bn) are partly or wholly outside Z.1's "debt securities + loans".
Honest free sector substitute: build from XBRL (2009+, quarterly) for the 5 spenders + CRWV/NBIS + ORCL: `LongTermDebt` + `FinanceLeaseLiability` + `CapitalExpendituresIncurredButNotYetPaid` over OCF, and the cluster's share of total US corporate bond issuance (SIFMA total, free annual) — plus the existing issuance tally. Not free: private-credit exposure, SPV terms, CDS. The R-zone belongs on the dashboard as a "2008-type multiplier" panel, explicitly labelled as not having fired in 2000.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING**; the chart title FALLS as written.

**S3. Finder C's positive control (H3, acceleration) already looks like it fails on the free data, and the design does not say what happens then.**
Claim attacked: "H3 is the positive control; if H3 fails on the free data the pipeline is wrong."
Counter-argument: on the scoping panel acceleration terciles crash at 28/26/23% (wrong direction), Spearman +0.05; above-median vs below-median 24% vs 27%; on the strict n=21 subset 45% vs 70% (reversed); post-1945 20% vs 15%. The scoping's `accel` (r12/raw24) is not GSY's definition (R(t−24→t) − R(t−24→t−12), a level difference), and GSY measured it on their own re-formed industries, so this is not yet a failed replication — but the design must state in advance: if H3 fails with GSY's definition, the memo may not quote GSY's characteristic results (Δvol, acceleration, issuance, age) as applicable to this panel, and the gauge collapses to size alone. The orchestrator's draft treats H3 failure as "pipeline bug"; it may instead be the free data being unable to reproduce GSY, which is a finding about the deliverable's ceiling.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING** (pre-register the H3-fails branch).

**S4. Finder B's chain — leading/coincident/lagging is mislabelled in places, and two metrics are stories.**
Genuinely leading for a cash-capex throttle: guidance language (by definition; the EFTS query with 0 hits is too narrow — hyperscalers say "moderate the pace of growth", "more measured", "digest", not "reduce our capital"), accrued capex and purchase commitments (one quarter), Google's `CapitalExpendituresIncurredButNotYetPaid`. Reporting-lead but economically coincident: TSMC monthly revenue (leads Nvidia's print by 1-2 months; coincident with the spend), RPO (a record of contracts signed, and circular when the obligor is an unfunded lab — ORCL's $664bn is dominated by one counterparty whose ability to pay is itself a function of the ring; RPO growth is a vanity metric unless shown with RPO/revenue and the share backed by labs). Lagging: Census data-center construction (put-in-place, 2-month lag, follows orders 6-12m), BEA equipment contribution (quarterly, revised), useful-life changes. Vanity or story: (a) the **dispersion metric** — "neoclouds rolled first, classic late-cycle sequencing" is a narrative with n=1, and the analogue contradicts it: finder C says recipients (Hardware/Software) crashed first in 2000 and telecom followed, while finder A's own KF-calc has Telcm peaking 2000-03, Chips 2000-03, Comps 2000-08 — the spender led. The two finders disagree and neither is cited for the dispersion claim. The equal-weight 5-name spender basket is also ORCL-dominated (−33% YTD). Display it; do not call it a phase indicator. (b) ORCL 5.95% 2055 at 73.6 is a 30-year bond; its price moves with the long end as much as with credit. Show spread to Treasury or do not show it. (c) RunPod H100 $/h is one vendor's list price. (d) CRWV iv30 107 and short interest are single-name distress reads; fine as tripwires, not as cycle indicators.
capex/OCF > 1: meaningful as the point at which the group becomes dependent on external finance — which is exactly what the historical record says matters (debt-financed busts → recession 6/6). Three honest caveats: quote TTM (0.96), not the single quarter (1.17; AMZN OCF is Q4-heavy); it EXCLUDES finance leases (MSFT $5.2bn, AMZN $1.5bn in Q3) and buybacks/dividends, so the true funding gap is larger; the circularity (labs' cloud payments funded by hyperscaler/Nvidia equity) inflates spender OCF only slightly today (lab revenue is low-single-digit % of $740bn TTM OCF) — the circularity is material on the RECIPIENT side (Nvidia revenue from neoclouds/labs it has financed), which is where DSO and non-marketable equity belong. The memo must not say OCF is "inflated" without a number; it should say the ratio is a lower bound on external-finance dependence.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING**; the dispersion "phase" claim FALLS.

**S5. Finder A's "6 of 6 debt-financed + tightening → recession" and "7 of 9" are hand-built on heterogeneous episodes and partly circular.**
Counter-argument: the classification "debt-financed" is made ex post with knowledge of the outcome (Insull pyramids, telecom HY, Penn Square); the 1929 and 1981 recessions began BEFORE or with the capex peak (NBER Aug-1929, Jul-1981), so "capex bust → recession" has the arrow at least partly reversed in two of the six; and "monetary tightening into the peak" is not a feature of today's cycle (not established in the material either way — the memo must state the current Fed path before using this conditional). Finder A labels the tally UNVERIFIED; the memo must carry that label and not promote "6/6" to a headline.
Survives? **SURVIVES WITH REWORDING** ("in the five-to-six episodes we can classify, every debt-financed boom was followed within 24 months by a recession; the classification is ours and ex post").

### MINOR

**M1. "Credit spreads bottomed at the equity peak, so they are useless as a warning."** True on Baa-Aaa for three episodes. But GHSS/Krishnamurthy-Muir's point is that LOW spreads with HIGH credit growth is the warning state — the level is the signal, read jointly with issuance quality, not the direction. Reword: "spreads confirm, they do not warn; a low spread during heavy issuance is itself the late-cycle state".

**M2. Canary framing.** "Lehman-type canaries fail 8-31 months after the peak" is correct and useful; the memo should add that the real-time-observable pre-failure signals (vendor-financing balances, receivables growth, IRU-style swap revenue) have direct 2026 analogues (NVDA DSO 59.6d, non-marketable equity $47.9bn, CRWV backstop) and that these are canary PRECURSORS, measurable now. That reframes the user's canary idea into something the dashboard can hold.

**M3. Analogue overlays.** Overlays of telecom 2000 aligned on the ex-post equity peak are theatre on a dashboard that cannot know today's peak. Align overlays on an observable anchor (quarter of peak yoy capex GROWTH, which Spectra dates to 2026; or the first guidance cut) and show the full fan of episodes, not a single best-fit.

**M4. "Peak capex growth in 2026" (Spectra) vs the user's "throttle back".** Growth peaking at +118% yoy with 2027 guided up at every company is deceleration from an extreme, not a throttle. The memo must keep the two distinct: the transition that mattered historically was capex LEVEL turning down (Q4-2000), not growth peaking (1999).

## Attack-list answers in one line each

1. Equity-before-capex: real on the two episodes with quarterly/weekly capex data, within resolution on the annual ones; it re-orders one link and does not refute the chain — ~40-60% of the sector's loss and ~60-67% of the market's loss came after the capex peak. Watch guidance, accrued capex, commitments, TSMC, DSO — measurable, uncalibrated.
2. Earthquake: not a selection artefact of rebounds (those are the SAFE run-ups) but an artefact of (a) outcome = market crash, (b) 27 clusters / 5 crash clusters, (c) corrections ≡ volatility (ρ=0.75). Correct statement: undetermined at any power this data has; size survives, nothing else does under cluster resampling.
3. R-zone: right instrument for a 2008, wrong for a 2000 (it did not fire in 2000Q1); aggregate is deleveraging but accelerating; sector leg is telecom-scale in GDP terms and partly outside Z.1; free substitute is an XBRL-built cluster leverage series.

5. Priced-in: none of the proposed measures is a measure of what is priced in; all are sentiment or positioning. The one honest statement the free data support: "Nvidia trades at ~13x consensus FY28 earnings against ~26x trailing — a peak-cycle multiple; the equity market already discounts an earnings decline after FY28, which is its own version of 'capex growth peaks in 2026-27'. Build: (i) descriptive tab — keep, align overlays on observables, add the macro-transmission and Treasury panels; (ii) gauge — keep as percentile ranks with cluster-bootstrap bands, two scored features, AI cluster shown as not meeting the primary rule; (iii) tripwires — keep, widen the language query, date-stamp a forecast ledger. ## Required wording for the memo

Allowed (the five sentences):
1. "In every capex boom we can measure, the sector's equity peaked 0-13 months before the capex peak; the capex throttle then preceded roughly two-thirds of the broad market's loss and, in the debt-financed cases, the recession and the canary failures by 8-31 months."
2. "After a two-year +100% net-of-market industry run-up, US history gives a 53% chance of a 40% drawdown within two years (GSY, n=40, ~6 clusters); the AI industries on the Ken French data have reached +84% (Hardware) and +41% (Semiconductors) net of market and have NOT met that rule."
3. "Run-up size is the only price-path feature whose link to subsequent crashes survives treating the ~27 historical calendar clusters as the unit of evidence; volatility, acceleration and the count of intermediate corrections do not."
4. "The economy-wide credit multiplier (business credit/GDP, 3-year change −4.5pp vs the +9pp danger line) is not armed, but it also did not arm in 2000; the AI cluster's own debt wave is already telecom-2000-scale relative to GDP and is only partly visible in aggregate credit."
5. "Hyperscaler capex equals operating cash flow on a trailing-four-quarter basis (0.96; 1.17 in Q3 alone, before leases and buybacks); every company guides 2027 higher, so what Spectra calls peak growth is a deceleration from +118% yoy, not a throttle — the historical transition that mattered was the capex LEVEL turning down."

Forbidden (the three sentences):
1. Any sentence of the form "P(crash) = X%" attached to the AI cluster, with or without an interval. (Allowed: a percentile rank in the historical panel and that bin's realised crash rate with a cluster-bootstrap band.)
2. "Smooth run-ups are the dangerous ones" / "corrections relieve pressure" / "the earthquake analogy is confirmed (or refuted)". (Allowed: the single statement under B1.)
3. "The user's causal order is reversed" / "capex is a lagging indicator of the bust" / "the credit multiplier is not armed, so aggregate leverage is not where the risk sits". (Allowed: the S1 and S2 rewordings.)

Labels that must travel with any number: "7 of 9 / 6 of 6" → "hand-tallied, ex-post classification, UNVERIFIED"; "53% / 80%" → "GSY rule not met by the AI industries net of market"; "1.17" → "single quarter; TTM 0.96; excludes finance leases"; "−4.5pp" → "did not exceed the threshold in 2000Q1 either"; "neoclouds rolled first" → "n=1 analogue, and the 2000 sequencing is disputed between our own finders".
