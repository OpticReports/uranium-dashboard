Clone Robotics: does the hand know how hard it is gripping?

Clone builds humanoids on water-hydraulic artificial muscles. These exhibits cover the sensing question, the muscle durability question, and the price. Everything here passed an adversarial check; figures marked (?) come from an unreadable memo and are leads, not facts.

No touch sensingBy published design: "only proprioceptive and visual feedback." No fingertip, contact or slip sensors anywhere in Clone's disclosures.

Force sensing can beat fingertip touch, if it is accurate

Same policy (RDP), two sensing channels, three contact-rich tasks. Force estimated from the arm's own joint-torque sensors, with no skin, scored above GelSight fingertip touch every time. That is the case for Clone's design choice. The catch: Clone has never published how accurate its pressure-based force estimate is, and geometry-only models for water McKibben muscles are off by about 9–10%.

Muscle life is the binding constraint

A hand muscle doing 8 grasps a minute sees 0.96M cycles a year on one shift, 1.92M on two and 4.03M around the clock. Bars show how long one muscle lasts at each published rating. At two shifts, only the memo's unreadable 10M target lasts more than a year, and the best public durability figure was measured on air at 0.3 MPa, well below Clone's ~0.7–1.0 MPa.
