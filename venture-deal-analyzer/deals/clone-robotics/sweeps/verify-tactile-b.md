**Restated range [ANALYST]:** gains clearly credited to touch run **+14 to +35 pp** on contact-rich lab tasks. The sweep's "+14 to +45 pp" is too wide. Naive tactile input can add nothing or hurt. The largest single gain in the sweep's sources (RDP) comes from **proprioceptive force**, the same category as Clone's sensing.

## 3. Strongest points the sweep missed

**In Clone's favour: the sweep's own RDP source shows intrinsic force sensing matching or beating fingertip touch.** In RDP, force/torque estimated from the arm's built-in joint sensors outscored optical fingertip tactile on all three contact-rich tasks (0.95/0.87/0.70 vs 0.90/0.77/0.48). Simply adding touch to a vision policy gave no gain (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02881). ForceVLA's +23.2% also came from a wrist force/torque sensor, not skin.
- What this means: "force-aware" matters more than "skin" for many contact-rich tasks.
- Limits: Flexiv's joint torque sensors are dedicated, low-hysteresis devices on a rigid arm, measuring the wrench at the tool tip. Clone's force estimate comes from pressure on hysteretic McKibben muscles, read through tendons, at each finger. The principle transfers. The accuracy is unproven.

**Against Clone: the "no touch needed" evidence depends on close-up cameras that Clone does not disclose.**
- ALOHA, behind ACT and Gemini, has "front, top, and two wrist cameras" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705).
- ViSk finds wrist cameras "can be critical to performance in tasks involving fine alignment" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17246).
- Figure 03 added palm cameras for exactly this occlusion problem (https://www.figure.ai/news/introducing-figure-03).
- Clone's disclosed kit is "4 depth cameras in the skull", with no wrist or palm camera (https://clonerobotics.com/android/).

So Clone lacks *both* things that substitute for each other in the literature: touch and close-range vision. The sweep's "not strictly needed" pillar rests on systems that have wrist cameras.

*Runner-up against:* shipped hardware lags the spec. Torso 2 (Dec 2024) had "182 sensors" for 910 fibres and 164 DoF (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gl0GnzPIOl4), and Protoclone (Feb 2025) "over 200 sensors" for ">1,000 Myofibers" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H7dhwFcuUn0). At those counts, per-muscle pressure sensing plus per-joint angles cannot both have been present. The sweep's verdict line "Every muscle has a pressure sensor" holds for the 2022 hand (35 for 36) and the Alpha spec (320), but not for the 2024–25 torsos, and the hands only regained joint-angle feedback around April 2026.

## 4. Engineering read on the LP's crux (ANALYST)

- **Can pressure plus length give usable grip-force control?**
  - *Firm and power grips:* yes, in principle.
    - Pressure-only grasp-force estimation and contact-loss detection are demonstrated on soft fluid actuators (Wang & Wang 2020).
    - Proprioceptive tendon hands reach <0.2 N force-estimation error, about 2% of a 10 N finger (DexHand 021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03481).
    - Self-sensing McKibben contraction exists (Felt, Chin & Remy, IEEE/ASME TMech 2016, doi:10.1109/TMECH.2015.2493782, title-level only).
  - *Light contact, slip and contact location:* unproven to absent.
  - *Measured accuracy on Clone's hardware:* none published. The 10% figure is an uncalibrated-model number, so treat the sweep's ±1 N as a pessimistic placeholder, not a measurement.
- **Is fingertip touch required?** Not for autonomy as such. It is a consistent +14 to +35 pp on fragile, occluded and insertion tasks, and leaders now ship it (Figure, 1X, Atlas, Sanctuary, Sharpa). The sweep does not overstate the *direction*; it overstates the *size* and credits some force effects to touch.
- **Net:** Casey's belief is **partly true**. No touch sensing is disclosed. Force sensing is disclosed (pressure per valve channel) but unmeasured, and on the 2024–25 torsos probably sparse. The decisive gap is touch *plus* close-range vision together.
