## 1. Executive read (8 lines)

1. **Demand is real but small, Chinese, and mostly non-productive.** About 13,000 humanoids shipped in 2025 (Omdia). More than 22,000 shipped in H1 2026, and more than 50,000 are forecast for 2026 (Counterpoint). The top Chinese vendors account for roughly 80 to 97% of units. Over 60% of H1 2026 units went to entertainment, research or data collection; only 17.7% went to manufacturing plus logistics.
2. **Paid humanoid work is still tiny, even for the US leaders.** Agility, the most commercial US humanoid company, reported audited revenue of $1.78M in FY2025 and $0.17M in H1 2026 (SEC S-4/A). BMW still calls the Figure work a "pilot". Apptronik has disclosed no unit counts. Tesla's external deliveries start around end-2027. 1X has not confirmed any shipments.
3. **The work that pays today does not need a dexterous hand.** Agility's revenue comes from moving totes with modular end effectors. Amazon's Vulcan picks with suction and stows with paddles, using force-feedback sensors. Dexterity is the long-run bottleneck, but it is not today's buying criterion. That pushes Clone's hand advantage out in time.
4. **Clone's best honest shot is a stationary dexterous torso, sold before the biped.** It could sell (i) to research and data-collection buyers, who took 27.0% of H1 2026 humanoid units, and (ii) for high-mix bench work such as aerospace or electronics assembly. It matches what Clone has actually built and needs no legs. It also appears to sit outside the FCC's "mobile robot" Covered List (ANALYST). Housekeeping is the plausible second act from 2028 onward, and Dyna, Figure and 1X already contest it.
5. **The memo's data/foundation-model thesis is weaker than presented.** Physical Intelligence showed that transfer from human video emerges even on parallel-jaw grippers. NVIDIA's EgoScale got gains of more than 30 points on a 7-DoF three-finger hand from human pretraining. I found no study showing that a more anatomically human hand transfers better. VLA progress lifts every hand, so it does not make Clone's hand the moat by default.
6. **On Casey's crux, the public evidence supports him.** Ablation studies show touch and force sensing add about 8 to 35 points of success on contact-rich tasks. Figure 03 (fingertip sensors that detect 3 g), Atlas ("tactile") and Unitree Dex3-1 (optional tactile arrays) all advertise touch. Clone's pages advertise only "Myofiber proprioception". A secondary source adds about 500 sensors, including muscle length and force. I found no public claim of fingertip tactile sensing. DD must confirm this.
7. **Policy cuts both ways.** On 2026-07-28 the FCC Covered-Listed foreign-produced mobile robots (humanoids and quadrupeds), "regardless of the nationality of origin". That blocks new Chinese models, but it also blocks new Polish-built Clone models. The only ways in are US production or Department of War Conditional Approval tied to a time-bound US onshoring plan. Only 2 robot approvals exist so far: Husqvarna and ANSCER.
8. **Timing: Clone's September 2027 biped MVP arrives late.** By then Chinese vendors will have shipped tens of thousands of units. Agility will be filling a milestone-conditional order for $300M / 1,000 Digit 5 robots, and Figure's BotQ and Tesla's Fremont line will be ramping. Clone must win on hand and cost. On cost, Unitree already lists the G1 at $13.5K and the R1 from $4.9K, below Clone's own "<$20K" target.

### (c) The data/foundation-model thesis: does a more human hand transfer better?

- Physical Intelligence (Kareer et al., arXiv 2512.22414, RSS 2026): "human-to-robot transfer emerges once the VLA is pre-trained on sufficient scenes, tasks, and embodiments". It nearly doubles performance in settings seen only in human data. **VERIFIED**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22414
- The PI robots used **parallel-jaw grippers**: the action space is "6 DoF left arm end-effector trajectory + gripper…" on ARX arms. Gripper actions are learned "from robot data only". Human data transfer "parallels cross-embodiment robot transfer" (UR5 to ARX). **VERIFIED**: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22414v1
- **ANALYST:** human video improves robots that have no human-like hand. That undercuts the memo's claim that anatomical similarity is what unlocks human data.
- NVIDIA EgoScale (arXiv 2602.16710, February 2026): 20,854 hours of egocentric human video. +54% average success over no pretraining on a 22-DoF Sharpa hand. On a Unitree G1 with a 7-DoF tri-finger hand, human pretraining gave "over 30% absolute improvement in success rate across both evaluated tasks". The authors call human motion "a reusable, embodiment-agnostic motor prior". **VERIFIED**: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16710
- **ANALYST:** EgoScale supports high-DoF hands as the best way to use human finger data, since its main platform is a 22-DoF hand and the shirt-folding and bottle-unscrewing results are there. But it also shows the gains are not exclusive to such hands. Commercial 22-DoF motor-driven hands already exist (Sharpa Wave, Tesla's reported 22-DoF hand: SECONDARY, https://notateslaapp.com/news/4866/tesla-officially-begins-optimus-robot-production). Clone is therefore not the only "human-kinematics" option.
- DexUMI (arXiv 2505.21864) closes the human-to-robot hand gap with a wearable exoskeleton plus visual inpainting, reaching 86% average success across two different robot hands. **SECONDARY** (via search summary): https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.21864. **ANALYST:** the embodiment gap can be engineered around for non-anatomical hands.
- **NOT FOUND:** a controlled head-to-head showing that an anatomically closer hand (tendon/muscle, human joint layout) beats a kinematically simpler hand on transfer from the same human video. I searched arXiv-oriented queries for "embodiment gap retargeting kinematic similarity" (results above). The search budget ran out before a second pass.
- **ANALYST verdict on (c):** VLA progress mostly commoditises the brain and leaves the body to be judged on reliability, cost and sensing. That is partly the founder's thesis. But the evidence says human-video transfer does not require Clone's anatomy, so the hand has to win on measured task performance per dollar, not on data compatibility.

### (d) Policy: US restrictions and what a Poland-based team implies

- **FCC Covered List, 2026-07-28:** the FCC added "advanced robotic devices" (defined as "mobile robots, such as humanoids and quadrupeds") produced in foreign countries. The interagency determination covers foreign-made products "regardless of the nationality of origin". New models are barred from FCC equipment authorization, which most electronics need "prior to importation, marketing, or sale in the U.S." Existing authorized models, devices already bought, and federal-government purchases are unaffected. **VERIFIED** (FCC fact sheet): https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423682A1.pdf
- **Exemption route:** DoW "Conditional Approval". As of 2026-09-18 the only robot approvals are Husqvarna AB 305v/310v/420v/440v IQ platforms (2026-09-09) and ANSCER Robotics AR250/AR650/AR1250 (2026-09-18). Both are "subject to… continuous compliance with the onshoring plan outlined in its conditional approval application". **VERIFIED** (FCC DA 26-996): https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-996A1.pdf
- **ANALYST:** Sweden and India have obtained approvals, so allied makers can get through, but only with an onshoring plan.
- **Definition and exclusions:** the robot definition has four prongs, including locomotion or ground movement, operation at a distance, more than 4.4 lb, and connectivity of at least 200 kbps. "Fixed/nonmobile robots" are excluded. An application needs a full BOM with country of origin for each component and "a verifiable US manufacturing and onshoring plan that is time-bound", with quarterly progress updates. **SECONDARY** (Cooley, 2026-07-31): https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-07-31-foreign-produced-power-inverters-and-advanced-robotic-devices-added-to-fcc-covered-list
- Whether a "domestic end product" carve-out applies to robots, as it does to drones, is unconfirmed. The Covered List robot entry shows only the Conditional Approval exception. **CONFLICT/NOT FOUND**: DA 26-996 Appendix A vs search summaries. The FCC FAQ page returned HTTP 403 and DA 26-786 returned 429.
- **ANALYST (Clone implications):**
  - A Polish-built mobile Clone cannot get new FCC authorization for US sale without US production or a DoW approval.
  - A **stationary torso** bolted to a cell plausibly falls under the "fixed/nonmobile" exclusion. That is a second reason the torso is the realistic first US product. Counsel must confirm.
  - Chinese models authorized before 2026-07-28 remain sellable, so the "China shut out" tailwind is partial.
- **Other restrictions:**

It is procurement-only. **SECONDARY**: https://letsdatascience.com/news/house-passes-restrictions-on-foreign-humanoid-robots-fd5a7cd4 ; https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/09/mapping-china-in-the-national-defense-authorization-act-for-fy-2027-part-i-the-expanding-acquisition-perimeter/
  - GUARD Act (introduced 2026-06-03, Moolenaar/Obernolte/McClellan): FCC-listing of "adversary-produced humanoid and quadruped robots". It names China, Russia, Iran and North Korea, not allies. **VERIFIED**: https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/media/press-releases/moolenaar-obernolte-mcclellan-introduce-legislation-to-ban-dangerous-chinese-robots
  - American Security Robotics Act (Cotton/Schumer/Stefanik): procurement bar on adversary unmanned ground vehicles, including humanoids. One source says it never left committee. **SECONDARY**: https://groundtruth.day/news/the-humanoid-robot-ban-is-a-bill-that-never-left-committee.html
