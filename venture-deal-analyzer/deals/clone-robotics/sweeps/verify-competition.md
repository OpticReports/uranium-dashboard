## 2. What the sweep missed

**Strongest missed in Clone's favour: Clone has grip-force feedback, and the leaders' primary force channel is the same kind.**
- Clone discloses 320 muscle pressure sensors "for muscle force feedback" and claims "accurate Myofiber proprioception" (first-party). A pressure-regulated muscle **commands and caps** its force by setpoint.
- The leaders' own descriptions put actuator proprioception first. BD's Atlas hand "rel[ies] on proprioception for agile dexterous behaviors", and its sim-to-real skills run on proprioception alone (therobotreport, 2026-10-01). 1X: "every joint reports force".
- Touch is described as "complementary". On Casey's literal question ("know how hard it's gripping"), the answer is **partly no**: Clone has indirect grip-force sensing. What it lacks is **touch**, meaning contact location, slip and light-contact detection.
- Second point: at the corrected **$200M** post, Clone is ~1.23x Mentee's last mark and ~0.95x 1X's A2 (unreproduced), not 1.4–2.2x.

**Strongest missed against: the "DoF lead" is a counting artefact.**
- Clone's own Alpha spec says "**26 degrees of freedom in the hand, wrist, and elbow**" (D8GOK2Ld.js, http://www.clonerobotics.com/android/).
- Competitors count differently. Sharpa's 22 is active DoF in the hand only. 1X's 25 is 22 finger/palm plus 3 wrist, with no elbow.
- Strip the elbow and wrist, and Clone's hand is ~20–23 DoF (ANALYST): parity, not a lead. That is the sweep's first "where Clone genuinely leads" item and the memo's "the hand … is where Clone leads".
- With the cost lead also shrinking on a like-for-like basis (row 17a) and no touch, Clone has no verified hardware lead left. What it has is a compliant, back-drivable muscle (CLAIMED) and a low claimed hand BOM (unaudited).
