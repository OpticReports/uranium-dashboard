## Strongest point the sweep MISSED in Clone's favour
**The core IP is not all the departed founder's pre-company work.** The Form C credits Michał Baran (Chief Designer, at Clone since 2022) with having "invented 5X miniaturized fast valves" and with "a new technology for mass production of Clone Hands" (printed p.22: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1958550/000195855023000003/cloneformc.pdf). Clone's site dates Myofiber's "monolithic musculotendon units" to "2021" at Clone and names its own "Aquajet valve technology" (https://clonerobotics.com/android/). Robozaps reports the valves are "now built in-house" as of 2026 (https://blog.robozaps.com/b/clone-protoclone-review). If those documents hold up, a material part of the current valve, muscle and manufacturing stack was developed inside the company after incorporation. That partly offsets the Koźlik chain-of-title risk, though it still needs the assignment documents (DD Q2).

## Strongest point the sweep MISSED against Clone
**Clone's own site settles Casey's crux, and does not settle it in Clone's favour.** The live Android "Nervous System" copy says the robot was designed "with **only proprioceptive and visual feedback**". Its sensors are depth cameras, IMUs for joint angles, and per-muscle pressure sensors "for muscle-level force feedback" (https://clonerobotics.com/android/, organ-system copy in the site's JS bundle `/_nuxt/D8GOK2Ld.js`). The sweep recorded tactile sensing as NOT FOUND. In fact it is a deliberate, published design choice: no touch or slip sensing. That holds unless Torso 4 changes it, and only the company can answer that (DD Q6).

A second miss in the same direction: Caplight shows total funding of **$7.14M** against the CEO's "~$17M" (https://www.caplight.com/company/clonerobotics). The financing history is unverified even at the level of its total.
