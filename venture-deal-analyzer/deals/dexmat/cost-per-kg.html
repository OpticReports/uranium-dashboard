Price per foot — the unit wire is actually sold in

Per foot is the documents' native unit: no mass conversion, no linear density, no band. It is the firmest ground in this analysis — and it is where the sponsor's framing is weakest.

There is no single incumbent price. The memo quotes silver-plated copper wire at "$2.86–3.15/ft" and argues against that. Its own workbook uses two tiers — $2.63/ft for military aero, missiles and space, $0.73/ft for commercial airlines, drones and the rest of the market — and those reconcile exactly to the workbook's stated market size (2028: 2,023.2 M ft at sector prices = $1,590.7M vs its stated $1.59bn). The tier the memo benchmarks against is 3.0% of the market by volume, 9.9% by value.

"As quoted" is the roadmap cost + the memo's stated 20% markup, except the first row, which is DexMat's actual posted list. "Copper-equivalent" scales by 1.56 — the conductor needed to match 26 AWG copper's conductance, since the roadmap's product delivers 64.1% of it.

Why 1.56x — and why it survives the basis question

$1.33/m ÷ $1,389.20/kg = 0.9574 g/m, which is 0.830x the mass of 26 AWG copper. Two readings, one multiplier:

  • Nominal-gauge reading (parsimonious, uses documented material). 0.9574 g/m at 1.30 g/cc is 0.73645 mm². At the shipping tow's 6.5 MS/m that carries 64.1% of 26 AWG copper's conductance at 83.0% of its mass — which fits the fact pack's existing note that buyers speccing ≤77% of copper's conductance already get Galvorn lighter than copper. Matching copper outright needs 1/0.641 = 1.560x more conductor.
  • Equal-resistance reading. Requires a material at 7,800 S·m²/kg — density-invariant, and 1.56x the shipping tow (5,000), 1.25x the deck's best filament (6,250), 1.21x copper itself (6,473). No document claims such a material and no DexMat product is one. Same 1.560x gap to the tow.

Both give 1.5601. The per-foot comparison is therefore robust to the interpretation — unlike the $/kg table, which is not.

What the per-foot view changes

  1. The October milestone is parity, not a win. $0.48/ft becomes $0.759/ft copper-equivalent against a $0.73/ft incumbent for 97% of the market. The memo presents this step as beating $2.86–3.15/ft, which is true only of the 3%-of-volume military tier.
  2. The first genuine price win is 2028–29, at the 30 t demo plant ($0.120/ft vs $0.73) — two years later than the framing implies, and one plant further out.
  3. What a buyer faces today is $8.49/ft, not $1.56/ft. That is 18x what 97% of the market pays and 5x the military tier. The "at/below cost parity today" claim is a statement about DexMat's cost, not about any price a customer can buy at.
  4. Galvorn never reaches copper's metal content. The 2030–31 endpoint is 4.8x the $0.0047/ft of copper in a foot of 26 AWG — and that floor falls to $0.0034 at a mid-cycle copper price, widening the gap 37%.

Two things this comparison cannot settle. (1) Galvorn's roadmap figures are bare fibre; the copper lines are finished insulated wire. Insulation, jacketing and qualification are real per-foot costs on the Galvorn side that nothing in the record quantifies — the comparison flatters Galvorn by exactly that amount. (2) The workbook's $2.63/ft sits 9–20% below the memo's own $2.86–3.15/ft quote for the same product, and no document states the gauge for either. A separate research pass on both is in flight.

Cost per kilogram — the anchor, and what it does not settle

No DexMat or sponsor document states a $/kg; factpack.md records $/kg: [UNKNOWN] — never disclosed. Rev 1 therefore shipped an 8.92x band. Casey supplied $1,389.20/kg after the new plant, which pins a linear density of 0.9574 g/m ($1.33/m ÷ $1,389.20/kg) and collapses the band.

The anchor ties out to the sponsor's own arithmetic. $5,000,000 (deck's 3 t/yr revenue target) ÷ 3,000 kg nameplate ÷ 1.20 markup = $1,388.89/kg — $0.31 from the supplied figure, a residual we cannot account for. A second, independent corroboration: the memo's October list price of $0.48/ft converts at 0.9574 g/m to $1,645/kg, within 1.3% of this table's $1,667/kg pilot price. Both point at the same step. But note what the anchor is: the sponsor's model restated in mass units, not a measured cost.

Which plant? The live ambiguity is pilot vs upgrade, not pilot vs 30 t. The memo attributes "$0.40/ft ($1.33/m)" to the "current plant upgrade," targeted October 2026 — not to a 3 t/yr pilot. Those may be the same event (300 kg → 3,000 kg is the 10x) or two different ones. The 30 t reading is ruled out by the tie (its $1,222.22 is 12% off). Also: the memo's own "$0.40/ft ($1.33/m)" is internally inconsistent by 1.35% ($0.40/ft = $1.3123/m); if the anchor was computed off $0.40/ft, density is 0.9447 g/m and every $/kg rises 1.35%.

Findings that survive every basis question

1 · The "long-term $0.001/m" claim is below the only fibre benchmark we have

$1.04/kg — 479x to 1,915x below the $500–2,000/kg CNT-fibre cost class in factpack.md, and 1/12.8 of copper metal. The intuition is a feedstock argument (DexMat buys its CNT powder, so its fibre cannot cost less than the powder), but we have no powder price in the record — our own P3 question concedes feedstock $/kg is unknown, and the memo records a plan for an exclusive-offtake CNT plant with DexMat as shareholder. So state it as what it is: 479–1,915x below the only CNT-fibre benchmark we hold.

2 · The 2034 model needs more CNT than two named producers make

Fig. 1's $4.501bn 2034 revenue at $0.048/m = 93.8bn metres = 89,773 t/yr of fibre. factpack.md names LG Chem at 6,100 t/yr and OCSiAl at 100 t/yr — cited there as evidence CNT powder is industrialised, not as a global total. So the honest claim is 14.5x the two producers named in our record, and 29.9x the entire 3 kt plant, of which the model builds one. True global MWCNT capacity is an open number and a P2 ask.

3 · The October price cut is mostly margin, not cost

All in $/m, so no basis question touches it. $8.49/ft → $0.48/ft is a 17.7x price cut against a 3.84x cost cut; gross margin goes 82% → 15.5%. "~99% margins" in the DD notes cannot coexist with the deck's own $5.11/m cost at the $8.49/ft list.

4 · Our own record's copper-parity line is wrong — and "parity" needs one benchmark, not two

factpack.md L64 says the roadmap "only reaches approximate copper parity ($0.04/m at 26 AWG)"; memo.md makes the same claim without figures. Against copper metal ($0.01544/m) $0.04/m is 2.59x dearer; against the memo's "expected copper wire" at $0.15/ft ($0.49213/m) it is 12.3x cheaper. Neither is parity, and the two benchmarks differ by 32x. The sponsor's TAM story is "30% of global copper wire," so the wire benchmark is the operative one — but $0.04/m is a bare-conductor cost and $0.15/ft is a finished insulated-wire price, so even that comparison is not like-for-like.

Honesty box
  • The per-foot section is the solid part. It uses the documents' native unit and its 1.56x adjustment is identical under both readings of the anchor. The per-kilogram table is not equally solid.
  • The anchor's provenance is unconfirmed on four axes we cannot close from the file: which plant (pilot vs the memo's "current plant upgrade"), cost or price, fully-loaded or variable, and which SKU. It ties to the sponsor's own arithmetic, which makes it internally legible but not independently verified.
  • Only one row of the $/kg table is supplied. Every other row is derived from the deck's $/m roadmap at the anchored density.
  • Corrections carried in this revision: the earlier "implied conductivity 10.14 MS/m" was a density-mixing artifact (the density-invariant figure is 7,800 S·m²/kg); and the earlier "REVERSED" parity finding was itself wrong — rev 1's 0.772x / $10.34/kg stands for the shipping product.
  • Copper at $13.39/kg is the January 2026 record, and it flatters Galvorn: a mid-cycle $9,500–10,000/t raises every x-copper figure 34–41% and drops the parity threshold to $11.45–12.05/kg.
  • Bare fibre vs finished wire. Galvorn's roadmap figures are bare conductor; every copper comparator is finished, insulated, qualified wire. Insulation, jacketing and qualification are unquantified per-foot costs on the Galvorn side. The comparison flatters Galvorn by exactly that amount.
  • The 144 t/yr scale-up model is not like-for-like — it models a direct-spun process, a different route from DexMat's wet spinning — and it is 1/21st the scale of the 3 kt plant.
  • SKU mixing remains live. The "$22/m → $4/m" line is a third, inconsistent cost curve (4.31x and 3.01x the roadmap's steps, reduction rate 81.8% vs 74.0%).
  • Not modelled: yield/scrap, insulation and cabling conversion, feedstock CNT price path, and the annealing penalty (a high-purity space grade loses up to 4x conductivity, needing up to 4x the conductor per equivalent foot).
  • Verification: three counter-agent passes. The first framing returned FAIL (wrong mass basis, invalid cross-check); an arithmetic pass caught six errors; the rev-2 pass returned FAIL on the inference chain and its nine required corrections are carried here. Rev 2 was published before its pass completed — a sequencing breach of the standing rule, and the reason its parity finding was wrong on the live link for a period.
