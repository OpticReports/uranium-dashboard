Galvorn: $/kg today, and as it scales

No DexMat or sponsor document states a $/kg — the deck, the Sept-2026 memo and the model workbook are denominated entirely in metres and feet. Rev 1 of this page therefore shipped an 8.92x band. Casey has since supplied the missing input: production cost after the new plant is $1,389.20/kg. That pins the linear density and collapses the band to a single curve.

The anchor ties out exactly, and that tells us where it came from. The deck's 3 t/yr revenue target is $5M on 3,000 kg of nameplate capacity. At the memo's own 20% markup that is $5,000,000 ÷ 3,000 ÷ 1.20 = $1,388.89/kg — the supplied figure to within 0.02%. So $1,389.20/kg is the 3 t/yr pilot step ($1.33/m), and it is the sponsor's own model arithmetic restated in mass units rather than an independent cost disclosure. It is internally consistent; it is not external corroboration.

What the anchor reveals about the product being priced

$1.33/m ÷ $1,389.20/kg = 0.9574 g/m. Work backwards from that:

This is the fact pack's standing pattern, now inside the cost roadmap. The record already establishes that the deck quotes best-filament performance as if it were the product, and that copper leads the shipping Galvorn 1000 tow by 8.9x volumetrically. The cost curve is denominated in a conductor built at 10 MS/m. If the real shipping tow (6.5 MS/m) is used instead, the same electrical job needs 1.56x more mass per metre — so every $/kg below understates cost-per-delivered-function by about half as much again.

What the anchor changed, and what it didn't

REVERSED · the per-kilogram parity finding

Rev 1 said Galvorn must reach 0.772x copper's $/kg for per-metre parity, because the equal-resistance tow is 1.29x heavier than copper. On the anchored density that flips: at 0.9574 g/m this conductor is 0.830x copper's mass, so it may cost 1.205x copper's price per kilogram — $16.13/kg at the Jan-2026 record — and still tie per metre. Galvorn gets a modest mass dividend here, not a penalty. The 2030–31 endpoint of $41.78/kg is 2.6x that threshold, so it still does not reach parity with copper metal; but the direction of the argument was wrong in rev 1 and is corrected here.

SURVIVES · the "long-term $0.001/m" claim is below its own feedstock floor

$1.04/kg — 479x to 1,915x below the independent CNT-fibre cost class of $500–2,000/kg, and 1/13th of copper metal. DexMat does not make its own CNT powder, so its fibre cannot cost less per kilogram than the powder it buys, at any yield. Arithmetically impossible, and it is in the deck.

SURVIVES, now pinned · the 2034 model needs more CNT than the world makes

At the memo's own Phase-3 cost + 20% markup ($0.048/m), Fig. 1's $4.501bn 2034 revenue requires 93.8 billion metres — 89,775 t/yr of fibre, against ~6,200 t/yr of world named CNT powder capacity (LG Chem 6,100 + OCSiAl 100). That is 14.5x global supply, and 30x the entire 3 kt plant, of which the model builds one. Rev 1 could only bound this at 2.5–22.6x; it is now a single number.

UNCHANGED · the October price cut is mostly margin, not cost

All in $/m, so the anchor does not touch it. $8.49/ft → $0.48/ft is a 17.7x price cut against a 3.84x cost cut; gross margin goes 82% → 15.5%. The "~99% margins" in the DD notes cannot coexist with the deck's own $5.11/m cost at the $8.49/ft list.

UNCHANGED · our own record's copper-parity line is still wrong

factpack.md L64 says the roadmap "only reaches approximate copper parity ($0.04/m at 26 AWG)"; memo.md makes the same claim without the figures. Both are wrong on both benchmarks: $0.04/m is 2.6x dearer than 26 AWG copper metal ($0.01544/m) and 12.3x cheaper than the memo's own "expected copper wire" at $0.15/ft. Neither is parity. Load-bearing, since it is our rebuttal of "3x cheaper than copper by 2031."

The beachhead, stated correctly

The deck's parity claim is a cost claim: $1.56/ft to make, against silver-plated copper wire (SPCW) wholesaling at $2.86–3.15/ft. At the price DexMat actually posts today, $8.49/ft, it is 2.7–3.0x dearer than SPCW — and even a 20% markup on cost ($1.87/ft) is not what it charges. Price parity in its own beachhead arrives only with the October $0.48/ft, which lands after the 2026-09-28 close. The per-kilogram table is the right lens for the bulk-copper TAM story and the wrong lens for the business that exists.

Honesty box
  • The anchor is the sponsor's own arithmetic, not an independent cost. $5M ÷ 3,000 kg ÷ 1.20 = $1,388.89, matching the supplied $1,389.20 to 0.02%. It makes the model internally legible; it does not verify that DexMat can actually produce at that cost. A cost figure that falls out of a revenue target is a plan, not a measurement.
  • Every other row is still derived from the deck's $/m roadmap at the anchored density. Only the 3 t/yr row is supplied.
  • The density is 0.9574 g/m only if the anchor is the 3 t/yr pilot step. If "the new plant" means the 30 t demo instead, density is 0.1512 g/m and every figure is 6.3x higher. The exact 100.0% utilisation tie makes the pilot reading near-certain, but it is an inference.
  • The roadmap prices a 10 MS/m conductor. The shipping tow is 6.5. Cost per delivered function is ~1.56x worse than every figure here.
  • Copper at $13.39/kg is the January 2026 record, not spot on 2026-09-12. Rescale the parity line with 1.205 x (copper $/kg).
  • The dates are the company's. Carbon fibre took ~50 years to fall ~40x; a 128x decline in 5 years has no precedent in advanced fibres. We converted units, we did not endorse a schedule.
  • SKU mixing remains live. The "$22/m → $4/m total Galvorn cost" line is a third, inconsistent cost curve — 4.31x and 3.01x the roadmap's corresponding steps, with a different reduction rate (81.8% vs 74.0%).
  • Not modelled: yield/scrap per step, insulation and cabling conversion, feedstock CNT price path, whether $5.11/m is fully-loaded or variable cost, and the annealing penalty — a high-purity space grade loses up to 4x conductivity, needing up to 4x the mass per equivalent metre.

Rev 2 has not yet had an independent adversarial pass — one is running. Treat the reversal above as the most likely thing to move.
