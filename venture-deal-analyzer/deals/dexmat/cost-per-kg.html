Galvorn: $/kg today, and as it scales

No DexMat or sponsor document states a $/kg anywhere. The deck, the Sept-2026 memo and the model workbook are denominated entirely in metres and feet; the fact pack already records $/kg: [UNKNOWN] — never disclosed. Converting needs one number nobody has given us — the linear density of the SKU the roadmap prices — and the two available readings of "26 AWG equivalent" differ by 8.92x. So the answer is a band, and the band's width is itself the finding.

The fork. The roadmap is tagged "26 AWG equivalent." Read as equal cross-section (a fibre the same physical size as 26 AWG copper), Galvorn masses 0.1674 g/m. Read as equal DC resistance (a fibre carrying the same current — 8.92x the area, 1.29x copper's mass), it masses 1.4936 g/m. Same roadmap, $/kg figures 8.92x apart. 26 AWG copper: d = 0.40489 mm, A = 0.12876 mm², 1.1537 g/m; Galvorn properties are the shipping SKU (Galvorn 1000 tow, 6.5 MS/m, 1.30 g/cc).

Which reading — the discriminating test

Take the deck's own by-plant revenue targets and the memo's own stated markup ("at just 20% markup over production cost"), and solve for the kilograms that must be sold. Equal-cross-section fits inside the plants. Equal-resistance does not.

Equal-resistance would require DexMat to sell 1.6–8.7x more product than its own plants can make. The 30 t row landing at 97% of nameplate is a tight enough fit to suggest the sponsor's model is built on ~0.167 g/m. Two further checks agree — and the second needs no markup assumption:

  • "2M+ metres produced" vs "capacity 15 → 300 kg." Equal-x-section → 335 kg cumulative ≈ 1.1 years at the new line. Equal-resistance → 2,987 kg ≈ 10 years at a capacity that was 15 kg until recently. Only the first is possible.
  • The sponsor's own weight claims are equal-volume claims. Memo p.3: "less conductive per cross-section, but 6x lighter." 8.96/1.30 = 6.89 — the density ratio, true only at equal volume. At equal resistance Galvorn is 1.29x heavier than copper and the whole Phase-1 weight pitch reverses sign.

Central case is therefore equal-cross-section. An earlier draft of this analysis used the resistance basis and was 8.92x too low throughout; the counter-agent caught it. Neither reading is established from the file — this is an unresolved fork, not a settled judgement, and it is now P1 ask #1.

What survives the 8.92x fork

These four hold under both readings — which makes them more useful than the table itself.

1 · The "long-term $0.001/m" claim is below its own feedstock floor

DexMat does not make its own CNT powder, so its fibre cannot cost less per kilogram than the powder it buys, at any yield. $0.001/m is $5.97/kg central, $0.67/kg at the lower bound — 84x to 2,985x below the independent CNT-fibre cost class of $500–2,000/kg, and below copper metal. Not a stretch target; arithmetically impossible. It is in the deck.

2 · The sponsor's 2034 model needs more CNT than the world makes

At the memo's own Phase-3 cost + 20% markup ($0.048/m), Fig. 1's $4.501bn 2034 revenue requires 93.8 billion metres a year:

BasisFibre neededvs world named CNT powder capacity (~6,200 t/yr)

The workbook's 2040 $67.2bn needs 234,500–2,092,500 t/yr — 38x to 338x. It is also 5.2–46.4x the entire 3 kt plant, of which the model builds one. Strongest finding here, and basis-independent.

3 · The October price cut is mostly margin, not cost

$8.49/ft → $0.48/ft is a 17.7x price cut against a 3.84x cost cut ($5.11/m → $1.33/m). Gross margin goes 82% → 15.5%. The "~99% margins" in the DD notes cannot coexist with the deck's own $5.11/m cost at the $8.49/ft list — that pairing gives 82%, and for 99% to hold, cost would have to be $0.28/m, 18x below the deck's own figure. The documents cohere in exactly one place: October 2026, where $0.48/ft on $1.33/m is an 18% markup ≈ the memo's stated 20%.

4 · Our own record's copper-parity line is wrong

factpack.md L64 and memo.md both say the roadmap "only reaches approximate copper parity ($0.04/m at 26 AWG)." Wrong on both available benchmarks: $0.04/m is 2.6x dearer than 26 AWG copper metal ($0.01544/m) and 12.3x cheaper than the memo's own "expected copper wire" at $0.15/ft ($0.492/m). Neither is parity. This is load-bearing — it is our rebuttal of the sponsor's "3x cheaper than copper by 2031." Separately, the sponsor's two copper benchmarks contradict each other: $0.063/ft is "copper price parity" in 2028–29, then $0.15/ft is "expected" copper wire in 2030–31 — 2.38x apart in two years, an unstated copper price rise.

The per-kilogram framing cuts against DexMat

Because the shipping tow needs 1.29x copper's mass to carry the same current, matching copper's $/kg is not cost parity. Galvorn must reach 0.772 x copper's price per kilogram — $10.34/kg at the Jan-2026 record — before a metre of equal-resistance Galvorn costs what a metre of copper costs. Even the optimistic lower-bound endpoint of $27/kg is 2.6x that. (Not an independent result: 0.772 is the gravimetric conductivity ratio 5,000/6,473 already in the fact pack, restated in dollars.) At equal resistance it also needs 8.92x the cross-section — ~3x the diameter, ~9.5 gauge sizes larger — which is a connector, conduit and bend-radius problem, not a price problem.

What that does not touch: the beachhead. Silver-plated copper aerospace wire wholesales at $2.86–3.15/ft against Galvorn's $1.56/ft today. Per foot, in the market DexMat actually sells into, it is already cheaper. The per-kilogram table is the right lens for the bulk-copper TAM story and the wrong lens for the business that exists.

Honesty box
  • Nothing here is a DexMat disclosure. $/kg has never been given by the company or any sponsor document. This is our conversion of their $/m roadmap.
  • The band is 8.92x wide and the fork is unresolved. We favour equal-cross-section on three independent tests, but "favour" is not "establish." Every headline figure could be the lower-bound column.
  • The ~$30/kg literature model is NOT corroboration, and an earlier draft of this analysis wrongly used it as such. It models a 144 t/yr plant against DexMat's 3,000 t/yr — 21x the scale — and it is the same unbuilt-plant study the fact pack already cites. On the central basis the 3 kt endpoint ($239/kg) sits 8x above it: a tension, not support.
  • Copper at $13.39/kg is the January 2026 record, not spot on 2026-09-12. It flatters Galvorn by raising the parity target. Rescale with 0.772 x (copper $/kg).
  • The dates are the company's. Standing view unchanged: carbon fibre took ~50 years to fall ~40x. A 128x decline in 5 years has no precedent in advanced fibres.
  • SKU mixing is a live risk. At least seven distinct products appear across the file. The "$22/m → $4/m total Galvorn cost" line is a third, inconsistent cost curve — 4.31x and 3.01x the roadmap's corresponding steps, with a different reduction rate (81.8% vs 74.0%) — so it is not the same series rescaled.
  • Within the equal-resistance family alone the answer still spans 1.7x depending on which property table is used: deck filament (10 MS/m, 1.6 g/cc) → $4,299/kg today; tow (6.5, 1.30) → $3,421; the memo's declared 6 MS/m tape → $3,174; 800 tow (4.8, 1.30) → $2,539.
  • Not modelled: yield/scrap per plant step, insulation and cabling conversion cost, feedstock CNT price path, whether $5.11/m is fully-loaded or variable cost, and the annealing penalty — a high-purity space-qualified grade loses up to 4x conductivity, raising every equal-resistance $/kg figure by up to 4x again.
