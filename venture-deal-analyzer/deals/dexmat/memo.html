The sponsor model, autopsied

The Delta4 model's "Base Case" nets LPs 102x ($10B IPO, year 10); its "Bull Case" nets 1,091x ($91.8B). Five compounding assumptions produce those numbers, and the downside branch is simply absent:

Model assumptionThe problem
0.05% (2028) → 30% of global copper wire by 2040No advanced material has ever done anything like this. Carbon fiber: 50 years, never 30% of anything.
Share ramp triggers on "3x cheaper than copper by 2031"Contradicted by the sponsor's own deck — its cost roadmap reaches copper parity only at the 3kt plant in 2030–31.
10x P/S multiple at exitThe sponsor's own comp set prices wire makers at 1–2x (Belden 1.9x); at 2x, the "$10B base case" is ~$2B.
0% option-pool refresh across 5 roundsRealistic pools push dilution from the modeled 32–42% to 50–70% — cutting exit ownership up to ~2x.
Series A at $100M post within 1 yearA 2.2x markup on a ~$1M run rate, assumed rather than argued.

The load-bearing fact: which conductivity basis?

Per unit mass (what matters when weight is the cost), Galvorn is essentially at parity — the deck's own table shows 6,150 vs copper's 6,300 S·m²/kg. Per unit volume (what matters for general wiring), it is not close, anywhere, ever. And the same table contains the ceiling: aluminum's specific conductivity is 12,200 — 2x Galvorn's at ~1/100th the price.

The one verification call that decides this deal

  1. The signed multi-year Galvorn purchase contract: counterparty identity, committed minimum volumes, binding offtake vs frame agreement. Binding + named → the offtake gate is met and the EV's left tail thins → defensible tail bet despite the price. Frame agreement or refused disclosure → pass.
  2. Secondary: executed status and volumes of the Tokai Rika ($3M/yr) and NeuroBionics ($1M/yr) POs; whether "50% margins" is gross or EBITDA; Rice license scope/exclusivity; and ask the CEO directly about the founding-date narrative — the quality of the answer is itself diligence data.
