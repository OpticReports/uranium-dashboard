## Market structure (comps sweep)

- **Pricing:** $20M cap = **2x the Carta pre-seed median (~$10M for
  $250K–$1M rounds)**; but normal-to-cheap against YC-batch AI norms
  (S25 median ~$30M [SECONDARY]). The cap is defensible **iff** the YC
  acceptance is real and you accept the AI-lab framing over
  sports-vertical software. Priced as skating software it is rich on
  every published distribution. Solo founders close VC rounds at
  roughly half the base rate [Carta].
- **The precedent set is thin exactly where the thesis needs it.**
  General Intuition works because Medal yields ~2B clips/yr with
  NATIVE action labels (button presses) from 10M MAU. Competitive US
  skating is ~60K people and video carries no native action labels —
  pose must be estimated, which labs can do on free footage themselves.
  **No sports-video → robotics data license at material scale has ever
  been disclosed.** Largest disclosed data license anywhere: Reddit ↔
  Google, ~$60M/yr. Shutterstock's entire multi-buyer AI licensing:
  ~$138M/yr. The deck's "$2–3B data & model contracts" exceeds every
  anchor by ~an order of magnitude.
- **Sports-video realized ceiling, 20 years:** Second Spectrum $198.3M
  (SEC-verified) and Hudl ~$900M private mark [SECONDARY] — both
  required league-official status or all-sports mass market. Mocap
  pure-plays exit small and undisclosed to strategics (Sony ×2, Epic,
  Amazon). No skating software company has demonstrably reached $5M ARR.
- **Skating reality:** 102 ISU member federations; USFS ~246K members
  of which ~186K are Learn-to-Skate; the deck's $10M ARR needs ~100%
  federation penetration or ~10K paying athletes at $1K.

## What is genuinely differentiated (steelman, kept honest)

The counterfactual — "the version of the jump you could have landed,"
generated under physics constraints — does not exist in any incumbent
product found (OOFSkate = jump metrics; Aiving = wearable sensors; ISU
CV = judging support). The 20-year IJS archive as a pre-labeled
preference dataset is a real, clever observation. And the founder's
physics-informed-ML training is exactly the right toolbox for
physics-constrained generation. The open question is whether any of
that survives contact with: no distribution, taken federation lanes,
no native action labels, and a licensing market that has never paid
eight figures for human motion data.
