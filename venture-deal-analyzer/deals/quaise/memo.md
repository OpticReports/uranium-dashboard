### What the Google verification changed (red team's full concession)

De-risked: demand for the first ~$600M of output; sponsor credibility on
the rest of the data room; financing gravity (project debt/equity closes
far easier against a Google offtake); the IPO narrative. The $24B
framework defines a credible bull-case demand pipeline.

### What it cannot change

- Offtake dollars are **contingent revenue, not capital** — zero flows
  unless plants deliver power.
- The physics gap is untouched: 118 m vs 10–20 km, vertical-only, dry
  granite; no directional capability; completions/casing/cement for
  400–500°C do not commercially exist.
- **Obsidian phase 1 is conventional drilling** — Google's diligence most
  plausibly validated the conventional-phase economics and the Newberry
  resource, not mm-wave drilling. A contingent offtake is a cheap option
  for Google (which also signed Kairos and a non-binding Fervo
  framework), not skin in the game.
- Conditions precedent unseen: with COD dates and termination rights
  unknown, a Vogtle-style slip — near-certain for FOAK per base rates —
  could zero most of the $600M.
- Fervo took 9 years to ~100 MW with proven drilling; first power 2030
  remains an against-the-record target.
