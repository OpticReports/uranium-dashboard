Quaise Energy Series B SPV: the price was never the binding constraint

Delta4 Capital “Energy Fund I Coinvest SPV” · $293mm pre / $443–493mm post · 2% one-time + 20% carry · target close 2026-08-12. Three of the four prior flip conditions are genuinely met. The deal still fails — on duration and on a tail that is the same bet counted twice.

Kill

Do not close on Aug 12. The disclosure removed the objection the last review happened to write down. It did not remove the two that decide the outcome: an expected hold long enough to crush the IRR, and an upside branch that requires Quaise to be worth more in 2033 than Fervo is worth today at peak mania — with zero electrons delivered.

Attack 1 — the price

“Cheap” is a statement about Fervo, not about Quaise

$293mm pre only reads cheap against a comparable that is itself the anomaly: Fervo listed in May 2026 on $138,000 of revenue and carries a $7.7–10bn mark. Price the SPV forward instead. At a $470mm post, with a realistic 45% cumulative dilution to a 2029–30 listing, here is the company value each LP outcome actually requires.

Attack 2 — duration

The flip condition was written in multiples. The deal fails in IRR.

The logged EV trees explicitly do not discount time — the honesty box says so. Restate the same multiples as annualised returns and the conclusion inverts. A 2% one-time fee looks cheap precisely because it removes the sponsor’s clock; it does nothing for the LP’s.

Attack 3 — the floor

The 50 MW “floor” is a project that costs more to build than it is worth

The bounded-bear case values a not-yet-built 50 MW contract at 10–15× hypothetical EBITDA and calls the result a $250mm floor under Quaise’s equity. That arithmetic never subtracts the plant.

Attack 4 — the tail

The tail probability and the tail multiple are the same bet, counted twice

This is the modelling error, not a rhetorical point. The tree treats P(tail fires) and the tail’s exit multiple as independent. Both are functions of one variable — sector sentiment in 2029–33. A de-rating simultaneously shrinks the multiple and makes the financing that gets Quaise there harder to raise. Apply both together and the audited EV crosses below 1.0×.

Attack 5 — what the disclosure also revealed

Two new negatives arrived in the same document as the three positives

The offtaker holds warrants. A customer paid in equity to sign is not an arm’s-length validator. The single strongest fact in the deal — a sophisticated buyer’s technical diligence — becomes a free call option written by Quaise, and the PPA’s pricing is now presumptively below market because part of the consideration was paid in stock. Confirm whether those warrants sit inside the $293mm pre; if not, the real entry price is 3–7% higher than quoted and they convert precisely at the IPO.

The hold ends before the proof. IPO 2029, first power 2030, first plausible mm-wave well later still. Obsidian Phase 1 uses conventional rigs, so Series B money buys a me-too superhot developer competing with Mazama on the same volcano with the same equipment — plus an R&D option. The exit is scheduled ahead of the evidence. That is not an accident of the plan; it is the plan, and it makes the LP’s return a bet on 2029 public-market appetite rather than on drilling.

And the governance does not compensate. A board observer seat belongs to Delta4, not to the LP, carries no vote, is typically revocable and excludable from executive session, and usually comes with confidentiality that bars passing anything to LPs. The LP gets no pro-rata, no vote, no transfer rights, and a 20% carry with no disclosed hurdle — meaning the sponsor takes a fifth of a 7% IRR.

The loss path

How the money most likely goes away

Not an explosion. Obsidian Phase 1 gets built with conventional rigs and broadly works, late and dear — first power 2032–33 rather than 2030. Millimetre-wave never reaches commercial depth with completions. The 2029 IPO does not happen. Quaise raises a large Series C in 2028–29 at flat-to-down with a senior liquidation preference and pay-to-play; the SPV holds no reserves and its LPs have no pro-rata, so it cannot follow and is subordinated. The company sells to Nabors, JERA or Mitsubishi in 2033–35 for $400–700mm, the senior money clears, and Series B nets 0.3–0.8× after eight years. Death by dilution and duration, not by physics.

Before Aug 12 — ranked

What must be answered in three days

  1. Price per share, in writing, versus the July 7 first close — with a fully diluted cap table. “Same round” is not “same price.” The table must show the offtaker’s warrants, any SAFEs or converts, and the option-pool refresh inside the $293mm pre. One email. If the sponsor cannot produce this in 72 hours, that is the answer.
  2. Offtake conditions precedent: COD long-stop, termination-for-delay, and the warrant vesting trigger. A redacted extract suffices. If the warrants vest on signature while the PPA terminates on a 2031 COD miss, the offtaker holds a free option and the “$600M” is not bankable — no flip at any price.
  3. Charter and stock purchase agreement: preference stack, anti-dilution, pay-to-play. Is Series B pari passu with A/A1, 1× non-participating, broad-based weighted average? The “50% downside cap” is a preference claim, and a preference is junior to whatever prices next.
  4. The SPV’s LPA: carry hurdle, voting proxy, term, and ongoing costs. Demand an 8% preferred return before carry. Confirm who votes the shares and whether the sponsor may sign a pay-to-play amendment without LP consent. Ten years of fund administration is not $1,000 — find the annual line.
  5. Delta4 itself: prior SPV realisations, GP commitment, and any fee from the issuer. “Energy Fund I” implies no DPI. How much of the $25mm is the GP’s own money, and is Delta4 receiving placement economics from Quaise or the lead?
  6. Second-tranche and side-letter parity. Written confirmation that no later tranche prices differently, plus disclosure of MFN, information and ROFR rights granted to Prelude, JERA and Idemitsu.

Recommended action: request a two-to-four week extension. A company that closed $134mm five weeks ago does not need a $100k–$25mm cheque in 72 hours. If the extension is refused, that refusal has answered item 4 by itself.

Flip conditions

What would make me a buyer

All four, checkable: (1) price per share equal to the July 7 close on a fully diluted basis with warrants inside the pre; (2) offtake CPs showing a COD long-stop no earlier than 2033 and warrants vesting on delivery, not signature; (3) an 8% preferred return ahead of the 20% carry, plus a written wind-up or distribution-in-kind obligation if no liquidity by 2033 — this converts the duration risk from unbounded to priced; (4) one named third-party engineering review, commissioned by neither Quaise nor Delta4, of the Newberry resource and the 400–500°C completion path.

With all four, my tree moves to roughly 55/25/14/6 with a 2033 wind-up floor, EV ≈ 1.6× net at a bounded ~6.5-year hold — a 7–8% IRR. Still below a venture hurdle, so even then this is a small, deliberately undersized position, not a $25mm allocation. Absent condition (3), I am a seller of this structure at any price.
