THE RESULT
CADY reasons within a structured hierarchy of knowledge — and knows the limits of her own certainty. Given an object’s properties, she climbs her own taxonomy to assemble what it is, classifies it,
and shows her work; and when the evidence only reaches a general category, she says so honestly (“it is a vehicle, but I cannot say which kind”) instead of guessing.

HOW IT WAS PROVEN
CADY was taught a two-branch, three-level taxonomy of everyday objects — vehicles and tableware — with shared traits at the parent level and distinguishing traits at each item. She was then given
objects described only by their properties, never told what they were, and asked to reason out their identity. The demonstration ran live over her saved knowledge graph; her FPGA reasoning core
was untouched throughout — her learned-pattern count held constant at every step, a verifiable sign the demonstration rode her knowledge layer without disturbing her cognition core.

WHAT WAS PROVEN — FIVE TESTS, ALL PASSED

1     Climbs the hierarchy to know an object’s full nature. Told an item has wheels, four-plus wheels, and an enclosed cabin, she concludes car — showing that “wheels” is inherited from the
      broader category vehicle, while “enclosed cabin” is the car’s own distinguishing trait. She reasoned upward through the taxonomy, not off a flat list.
2     Discriminates on the right trait among near-identical siblings. A motorcycle and a bicycle share almost everything. She separates them on the one trait that differs — motor vs. pedal-powered
      — and states that distinction explicitly.
3     Supplies missing knowledge by reasoning. Told only “utensil, elongated, bowl-end,” she concludes spoon — supplying a fact she was never given (“used for eating”) by recognizing the traits
      belong to tableware and inheriting the category’s properties.
4     Refuses to guess when the evidence is insufficient. Given traits that fit either a motorcycle or a bicycle but without the deciding one, she does not pick. She reports it is consistent with both
      and names the exact missing trait that would decide it.
5     Places things at the right level of certainty — the headline result. Told only that something “has wheels,” she concludes: it is a vehicle, but the specific kind cannot be determined — and
      names exactly what she would need to narrow it down.

WHAT IT MEANS

1     Recognition by reasoning, not memorization. A neural net abstracts objects into opaque weights nobody can inspect. CADY reaches the same kind of conclusion by reasoning over named,
      readable properties — every step auditable. Ask her why she concluded “car” and you get a real answer that traces to specific facts. The capability a black box hides, made transparent.
2     Graded, honest confidence — a differentiator and a safety property. A conventional AI forced to classify outputs “motorcycle, 41%” and buries the fact that it is really only sure the thing is a
      vehicle. CADY tells you precisely how far her certainty reaches. A system that knows the limit of its own evidence — and says so — is exactly what high-stakes domains (finance, security,
      infrastructure, defense) require. It does not bluff.
3     It mirrors how reasoning actually works. People recognize hierarchically: wheels → vehicle → car → sedan as detail arrives. CADY demonstrably does the first stages of this — in plain logic you
      can read. Structured cognition, not pattern lookup.
4     It builds on everything proven before it. This is the fourth proven rung in a coherent progression, each step adding capability on the same untouched reasoning core. Not a one-off demo — a
      system getting provably more capable along a deliberate path.

THE PROVEN LADDER
Each rung was proven live, on the same reasoning core, each auditable and built additively on the last.
1     The cognition loop. She learns a fact, retains it after the source is removed, refines it with new information, and reasons across her memories to a conclusion no one handed her.
2     Reasoning to a verdict. She applies a learned rule to classify something by its properties, explains which rule fired, and refuses to guess when the evidence is insufficient.
3     Learning from tools, at scale. A real perception tool labeled 60 distinct objects; she learned all 60 as her own knowledge; the tool was removed; she retained and recalled all 60.
4     Hierarchical reasoning & graded recognition. This milestone — she reasons within a multi-level taxonomy, assembles an object’s full nature from inherited and specific traits, and honestly
      places things at the right level of certainty.

The shape of the story: from “learns a fact” to “reasons within a structured hierarchy of knowledge, and knows what it does not know” — every step transparent, every step on owned silicon, every step proven.


THE BOUNDARY — PRESERVED
What this milestone is: transparent, auditable reasoning over properties — from an object’s traits to its identity, or honestly to its category, within a taught hierarchy, showing every step.

What it is not: it is not “she sees an object and identifies it on her own.” The properties were provided. Perceiving them directly from raw vision — looking at a spoon and extracting “elongated,
bowl-end” herself — is the genuine frontier and the next major build. Nor is it a neural-net-style learned abstraction: the reasoning is over named facts, by design.

CADY reasons transparently over an object’s properties to identify it and to know the limits of her certainty; perceiving those properties directly from raw vision is the next frontier, and the focus of
the funded roadmap.
