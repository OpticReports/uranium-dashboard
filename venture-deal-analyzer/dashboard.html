The instrument

Fixed weights, anchored 1–5 scales, scores committed independently before any aggregation. Hover each segment for why it's weighted that way.

Market 30Market & why-now — 30. Outcome data says weight the horse: tracking firms from business plan → IPO, business lines almost never change while most founder-CEOs are replaced (Kaplan-Sensoy-Strömberg, J. Finance 2009). "Why now" must be falsifiable — timing explained ~42% of outcome variance in Bill Gross's 200-company analysis, and industry-vintage effects are huge (52% of 1983 computer startups IPO'd vs 18% of the 1985 cohort). Team 30Team — 30. VCs rank team #1 (47% call it the single most important factor — Gompers et al. 2020, 885 VCs), but this rubric scores execution evidence, not charisma: performance loads on general ability + execution skills, not interpersonal traits (Kaplan-Klebanov-Sorensen, J. Finance 2012). Structured, anchored scoring predicts at r≈.42 vs r≈.19 for the unstructured meetings VCs actually run (Sackett et al. 2022). Moat 15Product & moat trajectory — 15. At early stage only counter-positioning and cornered resources are assessable; scale/network/switching powers get built later (Helmer's 7 Powers stage mapping). So the rubric scores the moat sequence, not today's moat — and asks whether the incumbent's rational response is self-harming. Trac 10Traction — 10, reported as demand / delivery (v1.1). The split is the single best-evidenced change in the instrument: expert evaluation predicts commercialization where the risk is TECHNICAL (+6pp per SD of evaluator interest on a 28.5% base in R&D-intensive sectors) and is weak-to-insignificant where the risk is DEMAND (Scott-Shu-Lubynsky, 652 MIT ventures). Scoring them separately, with different confidence, is what the data supports. It also fixes a v1 defect the counter-agent caught: averaging demand-5 against delivery-1 produced a "3" indistinguishable from a genuinely at-benchmark company, destroying the information the outlier verdict needs. Legacy detail: Provenance-weighted: verified > claimed, and sector-overlay gates apply (paid offtakes for deep tech, flattening cohorts for consumer, burn multiple/NDR for SaaS). Sponsor-sourced numbers are scored skeptically until documents verify them. Comp 10Competition — 10. Three rings: direct, adjacent, incumbent response. ~74% of incumbents respond only after year two — the question is whether the wedge compounds inside that window. Crowded ≠ disqualified: market pioneers fail ~47% of the time and hold ~10% long-run share; Google was ~the 12th search engine. 5Deal & price — 5 at seed. Seed entry price is a second-order return driver (AngelList: across a 5x valuation spread in seed deals, no difference in markup rates or multiples) — so it's mainly an ownership/fee-drag input. But the rubric switches on full entry-multiple discipline at Series B+, which is why Quaise's unpriced entry scored a unanimous 1.
fact pack + provenance tagsEvery number is tagged VERIFIED / CLAIMED / SPONSOR / CONFLICT / UNKNOWN. Defense against the two documented LLM failure modes: hallucinated quant (~8–15% on unsourced financial claims) and garbage-in from founder-provided data. 5-scorer independent panelSame fact pack + rubric, five independent runs, median + IQR reported. This is a reliability gate (IQR > 1 on any dimension = rubric ambiguity, fix and re-run) — never a training signal. A system can be perfectly repeatable and consistently wrong ("reliability without validity"). red teamA standing adversary prompted to kill the deal — a genuinely adversarial dissenter, not an assigned role (assigned devil's advocates measurably underperform authentic dissent). Counters LLM sycophancy toward the pitch narrative, observed in ~58% of cases across frontier models. Red-team and panel disagreements are logged, not averaged away. pre-mortem + WWHTBT"It returned <1x — why?" Prospective hindsight improves risk identification ~30% (Mitchell-Russo-Pennington 1989). The bull case is decomposed into what-would-have-to-be-true conditions graded evidenced / plausible / heroic — conjunctive heroics are where deals die. hurdle ledger: venture vs execution riskNew lens (v1.0, verified 2026-08-09). Every deal's risk decomposes into VENTURE hurdles (exogenous: does the physics work, does the market/regulator/partner cooperate — the team can barely move these, they resolve on dates, and failure is absorbing) and EXECUTION hurdles (endogenous: talent, operations, FOAK factory ramps — team-quality-sensitive, usually late-not-never, and correlated with each other because the same organization is the common cause). Architecture copied from drug development's phase-transition pricing (52%/29%/58%/91% published rates); execution anchors from RAND pioneer plants (FOAK: ~2x cost, half below 75% capacity yr 1), Merrow megaprojects (65% miss), Flyvbjerg's 16,000-project overrun table. Each hurdle becomes a DATED ledger forecast — hurdles resolve in months, exits in a decade, so calibration accrues years faster. Full template: templates/hurdle-ledger.md. forecast ledgerProbabilities anchored to base rates (~65% of venture deals return <1x; ~4% return ≥10x — Correlation Ventures, 21,640 financings) and logged before the decision. Resolved on schedule; Brier-scored per rubric version. This is how the methodology becomes provably good rather than plausibly good.
