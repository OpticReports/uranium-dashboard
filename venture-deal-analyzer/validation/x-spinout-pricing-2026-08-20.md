# Study — how X (Alphabet) spinouts actually get priced, and what a
# "20–25% discount to the Series A" can mean

VERIFIED after adversarial review, 2026-08-20. Prompted by Casey
confirming a ~$40k direct angel allocation into **Bellwether**'s Series
A at a 20–25% discount, alongside the Series X Capital fund position.
Counter-agent verdict on the first pass was **NOT SAFE TO PUBLISH**;
nine required corrections were applied before this file was written.
Both passes' audit trails are in the session log.

## The question that matters

When a fund says "we invest in Series As at a 20% discount," the
discount must be measured against *something*. Three candidates:

- **(a)** a concurrent price set by an unaffiliated lead;
- **(b)** a price the fund itself sets, because no unaffiliated party
  is pricing the round;
- **(c)** marketing language with no contractual referent.

## Finding 1 — for Series X-led rounds, (b) is the best-supported read

X's own account: Series X Capital gets **"the very first external look
at our pipeline"** — an ACCESS right. Nothing in the X or Series X
record describes a pricing preference of any kind. X also states Series
X **"often acts as the sole investor in the initial funding rounds."**

The record matches that:

| spinout | year | who led / priced | unaffiliated lead? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taara | 2025 | Series X Capital | no |
| Chorus | 2025 | Series X Capital | no |
| Verily Health | 2026 | Series X Capital | no |
| Anori | 2026 | Prologis + Builders VC (Series X involved) | **yes** |

Where Series X is sole or lead investor, the only reference price is
one it sets, or negotiates bilaterally with X/Alphabet. **(a) is
structurally impossible in the sole-investor case** and implausible even
in the co-led one: Prologis has no incentive to price a round and then
let a co-investor buy the same security cheaper, and no such term is
documented anywhere. (c) cannot be excluded and overlaps (b).

**Heavy lifting warning:** Alphabet is a **minority LP in Series X**,
deliberately (Teller: if Alphabet were sole LP "the fund would be
inside of Alphabet"). So "unaffiliated" is a matter of degree, not a
clean binary, even for Series X itself.

Honest wording, per the counter-agent: we **found no** public precedent
for a standing contractual discount to a corporate-lab spinout's
third-party-led priced round. "No precedent exists" is unfalsifiable
and is not claimed.

## Finding 2 — a wrong claim, corrected twice

**Round 1 claim (WRONG, stated to Casey):** "Anori is the only X round
an unaffiliated party ever priced."
**Round 2 claim (ALSO WRONG):** "Anori is the fifth."
**Verified:** Anori is **at least the sixth**, and its supposed
distinctiveness — an unaffiliated lead sitting *alongside* Alphabet
money — has at least two clean precedents.

| # | spinout | year | lead (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flux | 2014 | "Led by DFJ" — **Google Ventures also participated** |
| 2 | Dandelion | 2017 | "led by Collaborative Fund" ($2M seed) |
| 3 | Malta | 2018 | "led by Breakthrough Energy Ventures" ($26M A) |
| 4 | 280 Earth | 2024 | "Builders VC, Gideon Yu, and **Alphabet, Inc.**" ($50M B) |
| 5 | Tidal | 2024 | "Brian Zingale, Perry Creek Capital, who led the round" |
| 6 | Anori | 2026 | Prologis + Builders VC |

**Flux (2014) and 280 Earth (2024) are the precedents** for the
unaffiliated-lead-alongside-Alphabet structure — the first by twelve
years, the second by ~22 months.

**Heritable Agriculture must NOT be cited as unaffiliated-led.** X's
blog says only "funded from FTW Ventures, Mythos Ventures, and SVG
Ventures" — no lead designated, amount undisclosed, Alphabet
participated. Only aggregators name FTW as lead.

**Unresolved discrepancy on Anori's lead:** X's own pages say Prologis,
Builders VC *and* Series X led; TechCrunch says Series X merely
participated. Logged, not resolved.

## Finding 3 — valuations ARE sometimes disclosed; the earlier
## universal was false

A first-pass headline claimed "zero disclosed post-money valuations,
any X entity, any round." **Refuted by Waymo**, which X's own projects
page lists as `"exitPath":"Graduated, Alphabet Company"`:

> "We have raised a $16 billion investment round valuing the company at
> **$126 billion post-money**." (Waymo, Feb 2026; also $5.6B at $45B,
> Oct 2024)

**The claim survives only when scoped:** among X's *independent-company*
spinouts, no disclosed post-money was found in any primary source.
Disclosed round SIZES for that group: Flux $8M (2014), Dandelion $2M
(2017), Malta $26M (2018), Anori $26M (2026), plus 280 Earth $50M
(Series B, not first) and Skip ~$6M.

Scale correction: x.company/projects lists **31 projects**, of which
**10** are "Graduated, Independent Company" — not the ~17 entities the
first pass claimed to have reviewed.

## Finding 4 — the one real concurrent-discount precedent (Aaru)

TechCrunch, 2026-03-03, verbatim:

> "Recent rounds employing this scheme include Aaru's Series A… raised a
> round led by Redpoint, which invested a large portion of its check at
> a $450 million valuation, The Wall Street Journal reported. Redpoint
> then invested a smaller portion at a $1 billion valuation, and other
> VCs joined at that same $1 billion price point."
> "Multiple investors told TechCrunch that until recently, they had
> never encountered a deal where a lead investor splits their capital
> between two different valuation tiers in a single round."
> "In most cases, founders offer a discount to top-tier VCs because
> their involvement serves as a powerful market signal…"

Read carefully, this **cuts against** the Bellwether structure rather
than supporting it: the discount goes to the **lead**, as compensation
for signalling; it is measured against a headline the **company** chose;
and it is so rare that professional investors had never seen it. Also
note Redpoint took a slice at $1B too, and the $450M is TechCrunch
citing WSJ (paywalled — not read at primary).

## Finding 5 — capitalization and IP terms are undisclosed, universally

- Alphabet "would retain a significant ownership stake"; for Verily "a
  significant minority stake." **Never quantified for any spinout.**
- **No IP-license-for-equity or royalty terms appear in ANY spinout
  announcement** (Anori, Chorus, Taara, Tidal, Malta, Dandelion, 280
  Earth, Heritable, Verily). This is a documentation gap, not evidence
  that outright transfer is the norm.
- Correction: in the Mineral wind-down, **John Deere ACQUIRED** a
  technology suite; Driscoll's was the licensee.
- One documented retained right: **X holds a board observer seat at
  Anori.**
- Verily IS an X graduate ("an X alum"; "Verily initially graduated
  from Google X ten years ago") — an earlier claim that it was not is
  wrong. It is a recap/carve-out, so a poor comp, but Series X **led**
  that round, which is evidence about Series X's pricing role.

## Consequence for the analyzer

1. **No stage- or sponsor-preference prior is encoded from this.** As
   with the PitchBook study, the usable output is a set of questions,
   not a coefficient.
2. **The Bellwether discount cannot be scored until the reference price
   is identified.** If Series X prices the round, the base case per
   Finding 1, then the 20–25% is not an arm's-length discount and the
   x1.25–x1.333 EV uplift in `deals/bellwether/factpack.md` must not be
   taken at face value.
3. **Standing ask for any spinout deal:** who sets the price; is there
   an unaffiliated lead; what is the discount contractually measured
   against and who certifies it; what does Alphabet retain, in equity
   and in IP-licence terms.

## Methodological note — a reproducible summarizer failure

Both the research pass and the counter-agent, independently, fetched
`x.company/blog/posts/seriesx` and had a summarizer return **"$300
million"** as if quoted from the post. **The figure is not in the post**
— it is the headline of a linked article. The number is genuine in
Verily's own release ("a $300 million investment round led by Series X
Capital"), but that URL reliably induces the error. Logged because it is
exactly the failure mode the counter-agent rule exists to catch, and it
fired twice.
