Does deal count turn before deal value?

A viral chart claims M&A deal count is the early-warning series and aggregate value is flattered by mega-deals. We tested it. The answer is not the one either side wants: the claim is untested, and untestable at annual frequency — not refuted.

Both series, indexed to 2000 = 100

One axis, both series indexed to a common base — a dual-axis version of this chart can manufacture any lead you like by choosing the two scales. Shaded bands are NBER recessions. Circles mark strict interior local maxima.

What the eye should catch: the two lines peak in the same year at every major turn — 2007, 2011, 2018, 2021. The single exception in 26 years is 2015, and it runs against the claim: value turned down while count climbed for three more years.

The lead-lag correlation is not stable, and the test has no power

Correlation of Δvaluet with Δcountt−1 — the "count leads" test — across six specifications. The shaded band is the region where a correlation is statistically indistinguishable from zero at n=24.

Every specification lands inside the band. Three are negative, three positive. The sign of the headline number is carried by the 2021 SPAC/COVID spike alone.

The honest reading. At annual frequency, changes in deal count and deal value are strongly contemporaneous (r = +0.883, n = 25, p < 0.001). Neither leads the other at a one-year lag: count-leads ranges −0.19 to +0.07, value-leads +0.07 to +0.27, all p > 0.2, sign specification-dependent. This does not refute a sub-annual lead. Annual data cannot resolve a ~12-month lead — a true 12-month lead would appear in annual data as exactly the contemporaneous correlation we measure. At n = 24 the critical |r| for p < 0.05 is 0.404 and the minimum detectable |r| at 80% power is 0.545; detecting a moderate lead of r = 0.30 would need n ≈ 85 annual observations, or roughly three and a half centuries. Settling this requires monthly or quarterly deal data.

ClaimStatus
M&A activity rolls over ahead of recessionsConsistent with the data. Both series peaked 0–2 years before every recession in the window (2000→2001, 2007→2007, 2018→2020).
Count is the early-warning series; value lagsDies — of insufficient power, not contrary evidence. Count and value peak in the same year in all three episodes. The one direct observation of a divergence (2015) runs the other way.
Volume is flattered by a handful of mega-dealsSeparately true, and measurable. Average deal size in 2025 is ~$632M against a $450M mean for 2000–2024 — the highest in the 26-year series. This is a level diagnostic, not a timing signal.
