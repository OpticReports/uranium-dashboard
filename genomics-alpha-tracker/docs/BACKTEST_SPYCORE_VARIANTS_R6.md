**Multiple comparisons, across all three rounds: 25 arms.** Round 6's hypothesis was read off round 5's results (D2 led round 5), so the honest denominator is the campaign, not the round — 22 arms from rounds 4-5 plus these 3. Three statements, in the order of how much work they do:

2. **Bonferroni is also the wrong instrument** for arms that are near-identical portfolios: their tests are massively correlated and it over-corrects. Note the max-T is run against THIS round's incumbent `INC-3070SPY-SWEPT`, so its raw p-values are not the ones rounds 4-5 published against `INC-B5`.
3. **The multiplicity that actually matters is in no denominator at all:** the selection of R2-A itself over rounds 1-3, B.5's hindsight-fitted weights (they still set the comparator in rounds 4-5 and they still shape which hypotheses reached this round), and the choice of window. 25 arms is the SMALL multiplicity, and no resampling correction computed inside these 1433 days can reach the large one.

## What round 6 was, and why these three arms

Rounds 4 and 5 tested cores nobody is running. The live blend3070 book is 30% R2-A / 70% SPY; an arm that improves a B.5 core requires changing the core first, which is a much larger decision. Round 6 asks the smaller and more relevant question: D2 was the best arm of round 5 — does its correlation trigger also help on the SPY core the live book actually holds?

| arm | what it tests | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | D2's rule on a SPY core: 15% when trailing 60d corr(sleeve, SPY) < 0.30, else 5%, monthly | D2 led round 5 on Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar and was the only arm to win 2 of 3 sub-periods |
| E2 | the CONTROL for E1's weight level: static 10% on the same SPY core, monthly | round-5 verifier H1 — the trigger's claim is about TIMING, so the level has to be held fixed to see it |
| E3 | the same trigger at the LIVE levels: 30% / 10% | the only version adoptable without also changing the book's size — the live book already runs 30% |

All three route the sleeve's idle CAPITAL to BIL (the live convention and round 5's mechanism), rebalance monthly, and hold SPY as the core, so the ONLY thing under test is the allocation rule. The sleeve's realized correlation to SPY over the real window is 0.292 on daily returns.

**The prior was registered too, and it was specific:** *"The b5 study measured the SPY-core optimum near 15%, with the live 30% costing only ~0.006 Sharpe — an order of magnitude smaller than the B.5-core penalty that motivated D2."* An arm cannot clear a bar that needs its interval to exclude zero on an effect that small, and the intervals below (+/-0.183 to +/-0.234) are 30-39x wider than it.

## Protocol (fixed before results, carried over VERBATIM from round 5)

- Windows: REAL 2020-12-03..2026-08-19 (n=1433) and SYNTHETIC-EXTENDED 2016-08-23..2026-08-19 (n=2511), identical dates across every comparator within a window.
- **Incumbent: `INC-3070SPY-SWEPT` — NAMED BY THE REGISTRATION.** Rounds 4-5 pick the best of the three registered incumbents by BIL-excess Sharpe; round 6's registration names its comparator in advance instead, and gives the reason: *"Judging against the dead-cash version would hand every arm a free +0.040 it did not earn."* The other incumbents (INC-B5, INC-3070SPY, INC-SPY) are still computed and still printed in every table; they are not this round's bar.
- Measurement basis: daily MTM total returns on adjusted closes; CAGR calendar-annualized at 365.25; Sharpe/Sortino annualized sqrt(252); drawdown peak-to-trough within each window.
- Sortino convention: standard — downside deviation over N (round-4 counter-agent m1). Sharpe and Sortino are printed on BOTH risk-free bases; a Sharpe and a Sortino from different bases never share a column, and no table below mixes rf bases unlabelled.
- Costs 10 bps per side on traded notional. Tail sleeve = TAIL (registered, identical for every variant) (it is inside the B.5 comparators, not inside a SPY-core arm).
- **Rebalance rule is REGISTERED:** monthly calendar for all three arms. No absolute band is used in this round.
- **Warm-up default is REGISTERED and it is each arm's own LOW leg** — E1 5%, E3 10%. This is round-5 counter-agent M3 applied in advance rather than after the fact: there, `monthly_weights`' inherited 30% default held a weight D2's contract never named on 81 of 1,433 days and decided its verdict.
- Sleeve: FROZEN R2-A dollar curve (data/r2a_daily.json), reused verbatim; daily invested notional from data/r2a_exposure.json. Idle CAPITAL routed to BIL in every arm and in the incumbent.
- Bootstrap: paired circular block bootstrap on the Sharpe DIFFERENCE, 21-day blocks, 4000 draws, seed 20260822, basis BIL-excess daily returns; both series resampled with the SAME block starts so the difference keeps its date pairing. The Bonferroni tail is read off a 40,000-draw run of the same seeded sequence, whose first 4,000 draws ARE the registered bootstrap.
- Survival requires ALL FOUR: 1. Sharpe AND Sortino beat the incumbent on the full real window; 2. the 95% bootstrap CI on dSharpe excludes zero; 3. it beats the incumbent in >=2 of 3 equal sub-periods; 4. it does not lose the synthetic window by more than 0.10 Sharpe.

## E1 - E2 — the registered control for the weight level

The registration builds E2 for one purpose: *"E1 minus E2 isolates the timing from the level. Without this control a win by E1 is uninterpretable."* E1 and E2 hold the same sleeve, the same SPY core, the same monthly rebalance and the same cash routing; they differ only in whether the sleeve weight moves with the trailing correlation. The contrast gets its OWN paired bootstrap — differencing two arm-vs-incumbent rows would throw away the pairing that makes it tight, which is the error round 5's M1 was about.

| window | E1 Sharpe(BIL) | E2 Sharpe(BIL) | dSharpe (E1-E2) | 95% CI | boot p | dSortino(BIL) | dCAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| REAL (adjudication) | 0.7895 | 0.7818 | **+0.0078** | [-0.0456, +0.0619] | 0.756 | +0.0173 | +10 bps |
| SYNTHETIC-EXTENDED | 0.8091 | 0.8161 | -0.0070 | [-0.0497, +0.0402] | 0.784 | -0.0050 | -3 bps |

E1's mean applied sleeve weight over the real window is 8.82% against E2's flat 10%, so the two arms are close to level-matched by construction and the contrast is close to a pure timing measurement. **The interval spans zero** ([-0.0456, +0.0619], p=0.756), so the timing effect is not distinguishable from zero on this window. On the 25-arm bar the same contrast is 99.800% CI [-0.0795, +0.0930]. This contrast is a REGISTERED diagnostic, not a fifth criterion: it cannot rescue an arm that failed the bar and it is not used to.

**And it changes sign between the two windows** — +0.0078 on the real window, -0.0070 on the synthetic-extended one, each with an interval several times its own size. Two point estimates of opposite sign, both inside the noise, is what a null timing effect looks like; it is not evidence the trigger hurts either.

E3 needs no separate level control, because **its level control IS the incumbent**: `INC-3070SPY-SWEPT` is a static 30% sleeve on the same SPY core under the same cash convention, so the registered bar already measures E3's re-timing against the level the live book holds. But E3's mean applied weight is 17.65%, not 30%, so `INC-3070SPY-SWEPT` is a level control only in the sense that it is the level the live book runs — E3's +0.0247 dSharpe mixes the trigger's timing with a lower average exposure, and this round registered no static 18% arm to separate them. **The E1-E2 row is the only clean read on timing this round contains**, and it is the one the registration asked for.

## Per-arm resolution: what this round could have detected

A null is only worth acting on if you know what size of edge it ruled out. A known TRUE edge is injected into each arm's daily excess returns (a constant shift sized to raise its Sharpe by exactly delta) and criterion 2 is re-run.

**Both power conventions are printed, and the earlier "detection floor" framing is not reused.** Round 4 quoted an imported +/-0.4 noise floor that was never measured on these arms; it was retracted (round-4 counter-agent M4). ## Honesty box

- **The prior was registered and it held.** The contract said before any code existed that E1-E3 were expected to produce another null and that E3 in particular had almost no room. That is what happened. This is not a vindicated forecast — it is a round whose registered effect size (~0.006 Sharpe) was already 36x below its own 80%-power resolution.
- **In-sample status, and it is total.** Every number here is measured on the same 1433 days that generated the hypothesis: D2 was picked out of round 5 by reading round 5's results on this window. The R2-A sleeve carries survivorship, universe/alias hindsight and exit-engine overfit risk inherited from R2-A's own selection in rounds 1-3, and its 30% weight came from an H13 sweep against a SPY core — the same core E3 is tested on, so E3 is being judged against a level that was itself fitted here. **No CAGR in this document is a forecast.**
- **B.5's hindsight weights still touch this round, even though no round-6 arm holds B.5.** `barbell-lab/config/portfolio.yaml` fixed those weights on 2026-08-11 with no derivation record and the data ends 2026-08-19. They set the comparator in rounds 4-5, which is how D2 came to be the arm worth carrying forward, and they appear in this document's tables as `INC-B5` and in the synthetic window's splice.
- **The window is 5.7 years** (1433 trading days, 2020-12-03..2026-08-19) and it contains one bear market and one long expansion. Sub-period criterion 3 splits it into three ~1.9-year thirds, which is a weak stability test and is reported as one.
- **The synthetic window is a FLOOR, not a point estimate.** Proxy splice, unadjusted, no manager alpha added; 1078 of 2510 return-days use a proxy for at least one B.5 constituent (73% of B.5's weight sits in funds proxied somewhere in the window). Round 6's own arms hold only R2-A, SPY and BIL, all real throughout — but criterion 4 compares against a spliced comparator, so the floor matters to the verdict.
- **The tail sleeve question (#1) is still unresolved.** TAIL is the registered tail sleeve for every B.5 comparator in this campaign, identical across arms so it cannot flatter one over another — but the question of what the tail sleeve should be has never been answered, and it is inside every `INC-B5` number printed here.
- **The idle-cash routing is a favourable assumption, in every arm AND in the incumbent.** Idle sleeve capital earns a full day of BIL from the prior close with no execution lag and no cost on the implied daily cash movement. `ibkr-executor/app/blend.py` sweeps idle sleeve cash to BIL for real, but the live sweep is skipped while any book order is pending, leaves a residual under max($50, one BIL share), can be clamped to zero by `BLEND_BUDGET` headroom and only fires on the next cycle. Because the convention is applied to arms and incumbent alike, it does not tilt the contrast; it does inflate all of them.
- **What was NOT modeled:** taxes; bid-ask and market impact beyond a flat 10 bps per side; borrow, margin or liquidity constraints; the 20% short-vol bot the config pairs B.5 with; PHYS premium/discount; fund-closure or capacity risk; IBKR credit interest on unswept USD.
- **Nothing is promoted.** No config change, no weight change, no live-book change. There are no survivors to nominate; had there been, they would have become HYPOTHESES.md entries at most. Refinements to the window, threshold or weights require a round-7 registration — the round-6 contract fixes 60d / 0.30 / monthly / 15%-5% and 30%-10% and says so.
