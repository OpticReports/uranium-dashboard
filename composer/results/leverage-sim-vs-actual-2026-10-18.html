Simulated 3x funds against the real ones, where both exist

Before each fund launched, the 1990 to 2026 backtest used a simulated version of it: 3x (or 2x) the daily return of its 1x ETF or index, minus T-bill financing and fees. These are the same simulations run over the years the real funds trade, plotted against the real prices. Growth of $1 from each fund's first day, log scale.

Basis and limits

  • Drift = the simulation's annual return minus the real fund's (log, % a year); correlation = of daily returns. The 1x-ETF simulations' fee term was fitted on these same years (so their small drift is partly by construction; TNA's is not fitted); the index simulations use a fixed 1.8% a year and are not fitted.
  • Nobody could trade these funds before they launched (the first 2x ETFs in 2006, the first 3x in late 2008); before that, 3x daily exposure meant index futures or margin. Results before launch are what HG's rules would have done had the funds existed.
