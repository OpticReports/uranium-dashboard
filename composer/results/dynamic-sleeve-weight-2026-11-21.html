A sleeve weight that moves with the market

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 63). The sleeve at 50% while a market state is on and 15% while it is off, and the mirror, for 30 states: valuation, rates, oil, and how far SPY and QQQ sit from their 20-, 50- and 200-day averages. Each is compared with a book that holds one constant sleeve weight equal to the switching book's own average. Simulated, in sample; not a forecast.

  • No state works as a sleeve-weight switch. 0 of the 9 primary states pass the rule set in advance, in either direction, and none did on the 113 other combinations of threshold, weight pair and lag the reviewer ran (simple-average stretch states and nine others). The best of all 30 states adds +0.040 log-points a year over its constant book (SPY stretched above its 50-day simple average, less sleeve while on) and does not survive the checks.
  • The weight matters far more than the timing. Holding 15% sleeve instead of 50% all the time is worth +0.17 log-points a year since 2015-06 on same-close fills (+0.86 against +0.69, deepest fall 26% against 17%); +0.10 one close late, where every constant book falls 43% to 52%. No switch adds more than about a quarter of that, and the worst costs about a third (-0.053).
  • Defensive when the market is expensive: no support. CAPE has been in the top tenth of its history on 98% of days since 2015-06 and above its own 10-year median on 93%, so there is almost nothing to switch on. On the rebuilt 1994 to 2015 years more sleeve while CAPE was above its 10-year median cost -0.043 a year (2 regimes; -0.007 one close late).
  • Defensive when the market is stretched above its 20- or 50-day average: the wrong way round, in one stretch. More sleeve while SPY is stretched above its 50-day average cost -0.053 a year since 2015-06 (p 0.044, 0.40 after correction); the mirror, less sleeve, added +0.040 (its own p 0.11, 1.00 after correction). 2020 is 83% of that; it is -0.002 on the rebuilt years and -0.002 on the all-real years since 2021-06. A lead from one stretch, not a rule.
  • Risk-on when the market is washed out: right in drawn-out bear markets, badly wrong in the 2020 crash. Less sleeve while SPY was far below its 50-day average added +0.020 a year on 1994 to 2015 (p 0.17) and +0.041 on the all-real years since 2021-06 (p 0.087, 7 regimes, 77% of it 2022), and cost -0.047 a year on 2015-06 to 2020-11 (2020 alone -0.28 log-points; deepest fall 4.6 points deeper). Net since 2015-06: -0.004. A lead, not a rule.
  • Rates and oil: nothing. Yield level and direction move the result by +0.002 and -0.003 a year; oil above its 200-day average has opposite signs on the two samples (-0.037, p 0.15; +0.008).

1. What was tested

The sleeve weight is 50% while a state is on and 15% while it is off; the rest is split equally between Holy Grail and KMLM. The mirror swaps the two. A state read at one close moves the weights by the next close. The yardstick is a book with one constant sleeve weight equal to the switching book's own average, so a rule gets no credit for simply holding less sleeve.

What three of the switches added or lost over time

Running total of the policy's daily growth less the constant book's, from 1994 (more sleeve while the state is on). A rule that worked would climb steadily. Each stretch is measured against its own average weight; the yardstick resets at 2015-06.

3. Leads (none actionable; p-values here are uncorrected)

  • Less sleeve while the market is stretched up: one stretch. Sample: 12% of days since 2015-06 in 15 regimes, about 12 switches a year. SPY above its 50-day average: +0.040 a year over the constant book (deepest fall -1.1 points), its own p 0.11; QQQ +0.033 (p 0.19). Mirror: more sleeve on those days loses -0.053. All of it is 2015-06 to 2020-11 (+0.085); -0.002 on the all-real years since 2021-06 (8 regimes) and -0.002 on 1994 to 2015; 2020 is 83% of the total and its best 20 sessions are 113%; +0.007 one close late. The registered time-shift test reads p 0.004 here, but it is too generous: the sleeve's gap to the other engines swings 27% more on stretched days. What it would take: five all-real years show nothing; a second stretch like 2019 to 2020 would be needed to revive it.

5. What this does not settle

  • In sample, and short for slow states. Eleven recent years hold five or fewer regimes of the valuation and rate-level states; those ideas are "not shown", not ruled out.
  • Forward earnings were not tested (no point-in-time history of forward estimates is on file); CAPE and the earnings yield against real bond yields stand in. CAPE and CPI are today's vintages.
  • One pair of weights and one threshold per state, fixed in advance. The reviewer ran 113 other combinations of threshold, weight pair and lag on the simple-average stretch states and nine others, without looking for a winner; none passes, and none has a gaining direction with p below 0.05 since 2015-06.
  • Real, rebuilt, modelled. Real funds: Holy Grail from 2015-09, the sleeve from 2020-12, KMLM from 2021-06. Before that: rebuilt funds (Holy Grail 2006-07 to 2015-08; the KMLM fund 1991-04 to 2020-12), and shape only for Holy Grail before 2006-07 and the sleeve before 2015-06; VIX funds before their launch are a model: on the real VIX term structure from 2006-07, on a curve fitted from the VIX alone before that, and of a market that did not exist before 2004. From 2015-06 to 2020-11 the sleeve's rules run on mostly real funds. The 2015-06 sample has no sessions from 2020-12-02 to 2021-06-01.
  • Not modelled: taxes on about a dozen switches a year, each moving 35% of the book; settlement (a move between symphonies settles the next evening; one session is assumed); both books are reset to their weights daily at no cost.
  • QQQ is dividend-adjusted here; a trader sees unadjusted prices. The reviewer found that changes about 20 sessions and flips two of the smallest margins.
  • Multiplicity: 30 states, two directions, four samples; corrected only across the nine primary states. Same-close fills in the engine paths; the one-close-late columns are the bound. Measurement basis: daily mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points of each switch's turnover.

  • "No state works" held up under every variant, and the code has no look-ahead.
  • One bullet of my first draft was false. I wrote that "risk-on when washed out" showed only on the rebuilt years. It also shows on the all-real years since 2021-06 and fails badly in 2020; the two cancel in the 2015-on total. Rewritten, with a table by stretch.
