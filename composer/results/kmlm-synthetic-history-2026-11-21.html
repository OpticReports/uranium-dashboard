Rebuilding the KMLM fund to test the switcher on a longer history

Study of 2026-11-20 (addendum 58), corrected after three rounds of independent review. The KMLM fund is 6 years old, so it was rebuilt from its index rules and 22 futures markets and checked against the real fund. In sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • Keep the 10-day switch. That verdict rests on the real fund (addendum 57). Re-run here on corrected fund data, no conclusion in it changes.
  • The rebuilt fund is a far better stand-in than the old one, but it did not pass the bars set in advance. Daily correlation with the real fund is 0.893 against a bar of 0.90 (old stand-in: 0.61). The switch reads the same way on 96.5% of days (old: 87%), and the symphony run on it holds the same funds on 97.1% of sessions. Because a bar was missed, the rebuilt years are read for shape, not as evidence.
  • Read for shape, the rebuilt years do not contradict that. On 2008-2020 no variant can be told from the live rule: the best reaches t 1.94 where 2.71 was needed (and it differs from the live rule on only 30 days; the next best reaches 1.2). Shorter windows and moving averages lean behind; longer windows lean ahead there and are behind on the real fund. The two periods do not rank the windows alike (rank correlation 0.22).
  • By-product, not evidence: the switch on years it was not fitted on. On 2008-2020 the live switch beat its mirror image by +0.43 log-points a year (0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year; t 2.5, checked after the fact; 1 of 60 time-shifted copies of the switch matched it). Two crisis years carry it: without 2008 and 2020 it is +0.21 (t 1.3), and against simply staying risk-on it is zero without 2008, a year when most of the tree's funds are models.
  • On rebuilt history the live rule looks much rougher than on its real years (all simulated). Rebuilt 2008-2020: +81% a year, with falls of 47% (2018-09 to 2018-12) and 46% (2015-03 to 2016-02). Modelled 1991-2008: +30% a year and a fall that reaches 81% by 2008-10. Real years: +329% a year, 38% deepest fall.
  • History reached: 1991, not 50 years. A third of this symphony trades volatility funds and the VIX starts in 1990.
  • Corrections. Independent review caught two defects in my futures loader: the first build read stale closes in 2009-2020, and my first fix dropped sessions (a six-month hole in sugar in 2023). Numbers I gave from either build are withdrawn; everything here is from the twice-corrected data, re-checked by the reviewers who found the defects.

8. Candidate edges checked on the way

None clears the bar. Listed because the standing rule is to report them.

  • The switch itself (section 5). On 2008-2020 (3,231 sessions, rebuilt, shape only) it beat its mirror by +0.43 (t 2.5); placebo: 1 of 60 time-shifted copies as good. Concentrated: 59% of it is 2008 and 2020, and against a fixed mix at the switch's own risk-on share it is +0.14 (t 1.5). Even with a rebuild that passes its bars it would still rest on a year of modelled funds (2008). Nothing to change.
  • Longer windows (14 to 60 days). Ahead on 2008-2020 in 11 of 14 variants (average +0.038, t 0.7); behind on the real fund in 14 of 14 (-0.133). One pattern counted many times, not repeated on the real fund.
  • 11 and 12-day windows. Ahead on all three stretches (rsi11, rsi12, with and without the ranked picks), by +0.006 to +0.014 on the rebuilt years: far inside the noise; the rebuilt fund cannot resolve differences this small, and on the first reviewer's alternative corrected data all four turn negative.
  • Sizing, not edge: simulated on rebuilt data this symphony fell 47% (2018-09 to 2018-12) and 46% (2015-03 to 2016-02). Taken alone at 34% of the book that is about 16 points; what the whole book did in those months is in addendum 59.

9. What this does not settle

  • The rebuilt years are not evidence-grade. One bar missed on the whole overlap; fidelity of the switch cannot be checked before 2021 at all.
  • The real years are at least partly in sample for the live rule (its description carries a 2022-04 backtest date; when it was last tuned is an open question), and they carry most of every "behind" result for short windows and moving averages.
  • Data. Futures files end 2024-03 and are a public research dataset whose terms were not verified. The JGB has no independent close to check against, and the gilt in 2009 and 2020 could not be matched; about an eighth of market-sessions use a print stamped after the equity close. Four commodity markets hold a later contract than the fund.
  • Before 2008 several legs are models; SVIX and ZVOL are modelled through 2008-2020 as well.
  • Not covered: the 12 threshold variants on the long history; 2020-12 to 2021-05; decisions other than at the close.
  • Measurement basis: daily close to close, trades filled at the same close, 5.9 basis points per unit of turnover. Section 5 was run after the fact and two years carry it. Leveraged funds are index times leverage until their launches (2008-2010).

10. Addendum 57, corrected fund data

Yahoo's adjusted KMLM price understates the December 2022 distribution ($2.48 against the $4.04 paid; the fund's site could not be reached to confirm at source, but $4.04 reproduces the prospectus's 2022 return). Re-run on the corrected series: the switch differs on 6 sessions, no variant changes sign, the same five are ahead, the ranking is unchanged (rank correlation 0.99). Individual long-window results moved by up to 0.07 log-points a year. No conclusion of addendum 57 changes; the live rule's simulated return moves from +321% to +329% a year.

12. Method notes for next time

  • A check against the real instrument certifies only the years it covers. Check the raw data against an independent source in every year before building on it: the stale closes sat in 2009-2020, outside the overlap.
  • Reviewers who did not write the loader found the defect in it, and then a second one in my fix. Keep that pass, and re-run it on the fix.
  • Set the fidelity bars before building, and hold to them when the result is close.
  • A rebuilt series can reproduce large effects and still not resolve small ones; measure that on the overlap before claiming more power.
  • Check a fund's own distribution record against the data vendor's adjusted prices.
Independent verification

Three rounds of review by agents that did not write the code; the full log is research/kmlm_synth_2026-11/VERIFICATION.md.

  • Round 1 found a blocking data defect. My loader read stale futures closes in 2009-2020, outside the window the fidelity check covers. It also found the first report overstated its evidence ("three times sharper").
  • Round 2 found two defects in my fix (dropped sessions, including six months of sugar in 2023; and a wrong basis for choosing between readings of the data), and that my new claim about the switch was overstated. All corrected; both earlier builds' numbers are withdrawn.
  • Round 3 cleared the second fix. An independent rebuild matches the series exactly; no session is dropped; no value uses a later day; every headline number reproduces. The report was cleared once a wrong median and a recovery date were corrected.
  • What the reviewers agree on: every honest correction of the data gives the same picture on 2008-2020 (no variant near the bar), and the rebuilt fund is fit to be reported as a better stand-in than the old one, read for shape only.
