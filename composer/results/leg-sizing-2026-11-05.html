KMLM switcher: what a smaller leveraged leg costs, in CAGR per point of drawdown saved

The live symphony runs one leveraged leg at 75% of its value (a 3x fund, or two of TECL / SOXL / SVIX at 37.5% each) and a 25% VIX sleeve. On 2026-11-05 it held SOXL at 75% into a −33% session and lost 26.7%. Every row below re-sizes that leg on the engine's own reconstructed daily path (1995-06..2026-11-04; synthetic before each fund's launch; the rules' own years after 2015-06), with the engine's own costs on the variant's own turnover. Decisions are untouched: only how much of the symphony the leg gets, or which instrument earns it. No winner is picked; the curve is the result.

A. Growth of $1, 1995-06..2026-11-04 (log scale)

As built against four caps (the released weight sits in PULS, the tree's own cash leg), the vol-target with the same mean leg weight as the 37.5% cap, the 2x substitution and the 1x reference. The caps are a pure sizing trade: every cap keeps the path's shape and its Sharpe (1.30 → 1.30 over 31 years, 2.16 → 2.16 since 2015) and gives up CAGR roughly in proportion. The vol-target bends the shape: it cuts exposure after every shock, which on a dip-buying engine is also when the recovery is earned.

Left: 1995-2026, where the as-built path spends 2006-2011 under water (a 5-year stretch in the synthetic era: 2007 −49%, 2011 −38%). A cap scales the hole; the vol-target shallows the entry and lengthens the exit (longest time under water 1,826 → 2,738 sessions over 31 years).

C. The trade-off: CAGR against max drawdown, one point per variant

Right and up is better. The cap family traces a near-straight line from as built toward cash; its slope is the price of a point of drawdown: 1.1-1.6 pp of CAGR per pp over 31 years, 4.1-5.2 pp since 2015. The vol-target family sits to the lower-left of the caps over 31 years (it cuts the recoveries as much as the crashes, so the 2006-11 drawdown barely moves) and to the upper-left since 2015 (it was in the right place in Feb 2020 and Nov 2026). VIX gates, the 2x and 1x substitutions and the single-name cap are single points. Hover a point for its numbers; the full table is below.

F. By-products: where the leg's return comes from (the EDGE rule)

The leg's own daily return (its holdings at 100%) on the 6,279 sessions it is on, split by the holdings' realized 20-session vol at the prior close, by the prior VIX close, and by branch. Two asymmetries beyond the sizing trade-off: the leg earns most when its own vol is highest (the mirror of a vol-target, and the reason vol-scaling pays 3-16 pp per point over 31 years), and the SOXL dip-buy branch — the one that fired into 11-05 — is the only branch with no measured return, while the TECL and LABU dip-buys and the UVXY pops carry the path. In sample on the engine's own path; the placebo for a branch draws the same number of random 21-session blocks of leg days.
