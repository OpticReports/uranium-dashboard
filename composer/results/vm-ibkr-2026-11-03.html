A long-only value / momentum stock sleeve in IBKR: what it has earned

Two kinds of evidence. Ken French's long-only portfolios (CRSP/Compustat, survivorship-free, point-in-time, value-weighted, gross of costs): the top momentum, value and profitability sorts, and the big-cap rows of the size-sorted versions, which are the names an IBKR sleeve would trade. Then the live ETFs that implement the same ideas, net of fees, as they actually traded. Benchmark: the US market (SPY where it exists; SPY and the CRSP market track at 0.99 correlation).

B. The honest chart: rolling 10-year excess return vs the market

Annualized CAGR of each sleeve minus the market's over the trailing 120 months, gross. Above zero = the sleeve beat the market over the previous decade. Momentum rarely dips below; value spends whole decades below; the blends sit between.

The last 21 months carry the modern-era numbers. Excess CAGR over the market (French rows) or SPY (ETFs), with the recent windows stopped at 2024-12 and then run through 2026-09, and the rally months alone. Stopped at 2024-12, the big-cap 50/50 had earned nothing over the market since 2010 and the live MTUM+VLUE blend had lagged SPY by 2%/yr; the 2025-26 value rally put both back ahead.

C. Drawdowns: the sleeve falls with the market

Drawdown from the prior peak, monthly, gross. A long-only sleeve has a beta near 1; it does not protect in a crash, it is a different way to hold stocks.
