The sleeve's weight: is its weak long record the data or the rule?

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 60). Follows addendum 59, which found the sleeve made about 10% a year with a 47% fall on rebuilt 1994-2020 history and did not hedge the other two engines. Questions and decision rules written before any number was computed; corrected after independent review. Simulated, in sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • Unresolved, and the answer differs by half. By the rule set in advance the answer is "the data": 2015-2020, where every fund the sleeve trades is real, looks like the real years (+0.44 [+0.21, +0.75] log-points a year against +0.39 [+0.23, +0.56]; 0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year). But that rests on three months. Without February to April 2020 the same rule gives "in between" (+0.21 [+0.09, +0.32]). So I report it as unresolved.
  • The shock ladder (half the sleeve) made money wherever volatility funds are real: +36% a year in 2011-2015, +139% a year in 2015-2020, +57% a year in 2020-2023, +42% a year in 2023-2026. It buys leveraged volatility or leveraged semiconductors after a large one-day bond or credit move or an overbought reading, and a few days carry each stretch.
  • But its rarest tests look fitted to March 2020. "TLT up more than 6% in a day" has been true on 2 days in 32 years, both in March 2020; "TLT down more than 3.5%" on 6 days, 4 of them in March 2020. And the single best day since 2023 (2024-08-05, +58% for that half) fired because a short-term Treasury fund rose 0.56%, inside a band 0.1 wide; without that day the sleeve made +30% a year since 2023-02, not +39%.
  • The inverse-fund half (short biotech, short long bonds) lost for nine years on real or near-real funds (-24% a year in 2011-2015, -4% in 2015-2020; LABD is rebuilt before 2015-05 from a real biotech index) and made money only since 2020-12 (+59% then +30% a year): the rule, or the period. About half of the sleeve's return to 2023-01, and two-fifths since, came from it.
  • Not a hedge of Holy Grail and KMLM together in any stretch. The inverse-fund half moved opposite to Holy Grail on its worst days in all six stretches; the reviewer showed that is standing short exposure, not timing.
  • As a third leg the sleeve beats cash, at any weight. At 31.8% against T-bills it adds +0.14 log-points a year to the book on 2015-2020 and +0.11 since 2021-06, with a slightly deeper fall (+0.8 and +1.8 points). The same holds at 10% and 50%: the test says "better than cash", not "31.8% is right". By half: the ladder alone beats cash on all three recent stretches; the inverse-fund half only since 2021-06.
  • What a third leg buys is a smaller fall, not growth. Since 2021-06 the book with no third leg grows +172% a year with a deepest fall of 20%; with the sleeve at 31.8%, +128% and 16%. That is a risk-budget choice; the data cannot pick the number.

5. Candidate edges checked on the way

  • The shock ladder alone as the third leg. Against the live sleeve at the same 31.8% the book grew +0.094 [+0.047, +0.148] more a year in 2011-2015, +0.146 [+0.073, +0.244] in 2015-2020 and -0.005 [-0.056, +0.046] since 2021-06. Sample: three stretches, the ladder out of cash on about 8%, 11% and 6% of days. Mirror: on 2002-2011, where the bond funds are real but the volatility funds are models, the ladder's one-day tests fired on 172 days and made almost nothing (reviewer), so the only history that could contradict it is modelled. Against it: its rarest tests look fitted to March 2020. What it would take: paper-tracking every ladder trigger from here, and a rebuild of UVXY and SVXY for 2006-2011 from the published futures index.
  • The ladder's overbought and oversold tests (reviewer's finding, not recomputed here, no placebo run). Since 2011-11 they fired on 222 days for a summed return of +4.48 (t 5.3; the top three days are 0.88 of it); the one-day tests fired on 95 days for +4.68 (top three 1.52). On 1994-2011 the overbought and oversold tests were positive before costs (+1.26 and +1.01) where the one-day tests were not (-0.75, -0.09). The less crisis-dependent part of the ladder; it would take a placebo and a cost-inclusive run to say more.
  • Dropping the inverse-fund half. The same numbers read the other way: it cost the book growth for nine years on real funds and has paid since 2020-12 (short bonds in 2022, short biotech in 2021-22 and since). One regime in its favour; the owner's call, not a finding.

6. What this does not settle

  • Every real year is in sample for the sleeve, and so is March 2020. It was assembled in 2026-07 from two community strategies; the ladder's thresholds match that month's moves.
  • Period and data move together. Where funds are rebuilt is also the earlier market. Volatility funds are models before 2011-10, leveraged funds before 2008-2010, bond funds before 2002-07 (the high-yield fund, the ladder's first test, before 2007-04).
  • A few days decide each stretch, and one band 0.1 wide decided the best of them. Bootstrap intervals understate how uncertain a return of that shape is.
  • Fills. UVXY at the close after a shock is simulated at the closing price with 5.9 basis points of cost; the live rule decides about nine minutes before the close.
  • One seam day (2023-02-01) is missing from the sleeve's path and every book here.
