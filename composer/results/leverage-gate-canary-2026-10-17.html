Canary signals as a leverage gate

EMAs, CAPE levels, bond yields, the curve's re-steepening window, retail leverage (FINRA margin debt) and OFR stress, each tested as the switch that holds HG's five trend baskets at 1x instead of 3x. HG on the 1990 to 2026 proxy panel, out of sample for its rules; the dip-buys stay levered (addendum 42). Every signal is read with its publication lag.

None of them beats the plain 200-day gate. Used alone, the best (EMAs, CAPE below 35, OFR stress) tie it at best; bond yields, the curve window and margin growth do no better than staying levered. On top of the 200-day, CAPE and margin excess add a little, and all of it comes from one episode: they stepped down before the 2000 top. One episode out of about ninety tests is what chance alone produces, so no EV+ gate beyond the 200-day is established.

Basis and limits

  • Each signal is used three ways: alone, together with the 200-day (leveraged only when both agree), and as a condition on when the 200-day applies (and its mirror). The conditional use never beat the plain 200-day, the same verdict as addendum 40.
  • Publication lags: Treasury yields and the New York Fed term premium one session; OFR stress two sessions; FINRA margin month m from the start of month m+2; CAPE two months. Before a series exists the gate stays on.
  • Margin growth reproduces the canary's own numbers (June 2026: +49.0% a year, +28.2 points over the S&P). It has cooled since: +31.4% and +17.0 points in September.
  • About 90 variants were run (22 signals, four uses each) and all are shown. The best result, CAPE below 35 with the 200-day, has a 97% chance of beating the 200-day alone on its own bootstrap; across 90 correlated tries that is expected by chance.
  • Not tested: the canary's seven-indicator leading stack. Its data comes from FRED, which did not respond from this session; it is retried in the background and will be added when it does.
  • Research only. Nothing here changes a live symphony.
