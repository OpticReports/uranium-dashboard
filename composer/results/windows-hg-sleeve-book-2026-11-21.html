Windows and moving averages in Holy Grail, the sleeve and the book

Study of 2026-11-20 (addendum 59). 134 variants declared before any was run: every window in Holy Grail and the crash sleeve, simple and exponential averages, then the three-engine book. Long history from the start. In sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • No window or moving-average change is supported in either symphony. 0 of 89 Holy Grail variants and 0 of 45 sleeve variants pass the five tests set in advance.
  • Holy Grail's windows sit on flat ground. Crossover pairs near 10/20, the 20-day cash test at 10 to 50 days, the SPY gate at 100 to 300 days and the pick ranking at 8 to 14 days are all within noise of the live rule. Moving the RSI tests off 10 days leans worse; allowing for all the tries it is distinguishably worse only for the RSI tests at 20 days with the live thresholds, the RSI tests at 17 days with the live thresholds, the RSI tests at 5 days with matched thresholds, every RSI window at 5 days.
  • Exponential averages are no better than simple ones. Same windows, EMA minus SMA: crossovers -0.013 rebuilt and +0.014 real, cash test -0.007 rebuilt and +0.009 real, SPY gate -0.011 rebuilt and -0.016 real. Where both trend tests are slowed two to five times the EMA loses less than the SMA (+0.000 rebuilt, +0.042 real, t +2.3), but every such variant is still behind the live rule.
  • The sleeve's windows are level too. Its IEF-against-PSQ test is level from 8 to 12 days. Stretching the ladder's one-day shock tests to 2, 3 or 5 days is behind on every stretch (t -1.5 since 2020-12, -2.7 on the 26 rebuilt years before), though not distinguishably on the short verdict sample.
  • At book level nothing moves. The best single swap in each engine changes the book by +0.014 to +0.027 log-points a year (0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year). The plain intervals for Holy Grail's and the sleeve's best sit just above zero, but each is the best of its engine's tries picked after the fact; allowing for that pick none is distinguishable.
  • By-product: every engine looks rougher on rebuilt history than on its real years (all simulated). Holy Grail fell 50% in 2011-12 and 60% in 2001 (real years: 36%). The sleeve made +10% a year with a 47% fall on 1994-2020 (real: +39%, 15%). The book's deepest falls: 35% (2000-03 to 2000-05, back by 2000-11), 32% (2003-09 to 2005-04, back by 2006-02), 29% (2010-11 to 2011-08, back by 2011-11) (real years: 11%).
  • By-product: the sleeve reads as uncorrelated with the other two, not as a hedge of them. On the worst 5% of days for Holy Grail and KMLM together it made +0.27% against +0.15% on other days (t 0.4), and +0.55% on their best days. On 1994-2020: -0.09% on the worst days. Against Holy Grail alone since 2021 it did pay: +0.78% on Holy Grail's worst days (t 2.4); on the long history +0.11% (t 0.6).

3. The crash sleeve

Live, in two halves. Half one: hold the inverse funds (LABD, TMV) when the 8-day RSI of IEF is below the 45-day RSI of PSQ, each only while its own 20-day RSI is below KMLM's 10-day. Half two: a ladder of one-day bond and credit shocks with RSI tests at 10 to 16 days. It has no moving average, so only windows were varied.

All 45 sleeve variants

t-statistic against the live sleeve on the verdict years (2020-12 on).

Best by t: kmlm5, KMLM window (live 10) at 1.84, where about 2.84 was needed. (The bar computed as registered reads 4.19; it is inflated by variants that differ from the live sleeve on only a handful of days, so the plain correction for 45 tries is shown.) 15 of 45 are ahead on the verdict years (2020-12 on, 5.9 years), 3 (ief10, kmlm5, x0.7) on all three stretches. The ladder is active on few days in that sample (the simulation reviewer counted 91 of 1,498 sessions), so ladder variants have little power there and the long rebuilt history is the better guide to shape. The "matched" ladder-return variants are in effect a different ladder: their thresholds were set on a handful of events in proxy funds, and five of the thirteen rungs never fire.

Is it a hedge?

Not of the other two together. It is close to uncorrelated with them: it earns about the same on their worst days as on any other day, and more on their best days. Against Holy Grail alone it has paid on the bad days since 2021, much less so on the long history.

5. Candidate edges checked on the way

None clears the bar. Listed because the standing rule is to report them.

  • Holy Grail on a slower crossover (15/30-day simple). Rebuilt years +0.112 log-points a year (t +2.4), max drawdown 36% against the live 50%; real years -0.016 (t -0.3), drawdown 42% against 36%; modelled years -0.013. Both together t +1.22, family-wise p 0.97. 80% of the rebuilt-years gain is its best 10 days and the smaller drawdown is one episode (2011). Its exponential twin shows +0.007 on the rebuilt years, so the gain does not survive a change of form. Would need to hold on the real years; it does not.
  • Holy Grail with a 100-day SPY gate. Rebuilt years +0.045 log-points a year (t +0.8), max drawdown 47% against the live 50%; real years +0.023 (t +0.7), drawdown 35% against 36%; modelled years -0.049. Both together t +1.06, family-wise p 0.99. Ahead on both verdict stretches, behind on the modelled one, where its deepest fall is 74% against the live 60%. The 10/30 crossover has the same profile (t +1.04).
  • Holy Grail's best by t (picks11). Rebuilt years -0.008 log-points a year (t -1.3), max drawdown 50% against the live 50%; real years +0.027 (t +2.0), drawdown 36% against 36%; modelled years -0.005. Both together t +1.42, family-wise p 0.93. A hundredth of a log-point; 109% of its real-years gain is its best 10 days.
  • The 20-day cash test looks fitted to the real years. Its 13 other windows and forms are ahead of the live rule in 12 of 13 on the modelled years and 10 of 13 on the rebuilt years, and in 0 of 13 on the real years. Each gap is small (under 0.05); it says the 20 is not special, not that another number is better.
  • The sleeve's best by t (kmlm5: the KMLM window in its two inverse-fund tests at 5 days instead of 10). Rebuilt years +0.097 log-points a year (t +1.4), max drawdown 13% against the live 13%; real years +0.030 (t +1.2), drawdown 13% against 15%; modelled years +0.001. Both together t +1.84, family-wise p 0.74. Ahead since 2020-12 on both stretches, flat on the 26 rebuilt years before. Six years, one of 45 tries: inside the noise. Would need the long history to agree, and it does not.
  • The sleeve's IEF window at 14 or 20 days on the long history. 1994-2020 (rebuilt, shape only): +0.028 and +0.037 log-points a year (t +2.6 and +2.8), deepest fall 42% against 47%. On the real years it is -0.042 and -0.042. Fading over time and reversed where the funds are real: not usable.
  • The sleeve against Holy Grail alone. On Holy Grail's worst 5% of days since 2021-06 (69 days) the sleeve made +0.78% against +0.13% on other days (t +2.4); mirror: -0.39% on Holy Grail's best days. On 1994-2020: +0.11% (t +0.6). A real but recent offset; it would take the long history agreeing, or more years, to size on it.
  • Risk, not edge. On rebuilt history each engine has fallen much further than in its real years, and the book has fallen 35% (section 4).

6. What this does not settle

  • Small gains are invisible. The typical variant needed 0.10 log-points a year in Holy Grail and about 0.07 in the sleeve to stand out half the time. "No evidence for a change", not "proof the live windows are best".
  • Measurement basis: daily close to close, trades filled at the same close, 5.9 basis points per unit of turnover (about 15% above what Composer's own backtest implies; the same for every variant). Each engine is corrected for its own number of tries, not for all 134.
  • The real years are in sample for the live rules. Their authors chose the windows on roughly those years.
  • Rebuilt funds are index times leverage: financing, fees and tracking are approximations. On Holy Grail's rebuilt 2006-2015 about a fifth of sessions hold a fund before its launch. Before 1998 several leveraged funds are the same rebuilt series. The sleeve's verdict sample is short (5.9 years) because its KMLM signal before 2020-12 is the rebuilt fund.
  • The sleeve's ladder thresholds were tuned by its authors; matched thresholds for rare events (a 6% day in TLT) are noisy.
  • Not modelled: intraday paths, taxes, capacity, near-ties between ranked picks; decisions are simulated at the close.
  • The hedge reading is on 69 bad days since 2021 and on rebuilt history; neither is a clean test.
  • HARV was not studied (being closed).

8. Method notes for next time

  • Run the long history first. Both engines' real years were their best years; the rebuilt years are the fairer test of a rule that was chosen on the real ones.
  • Gate the simulator on Composer for variants in each family before reading anything, including one exponential form.
  • When a window change alters how often a test fires, test it both ways (matched and raw) and set the matched level on years the verdict does not use.
  • For a hedge, score the variant on the bad days of what it hedges, not only on its own growth.

Independent verification

Two reviewers that did not write the code checked this study in parallel; the full log is research/windows_2026-11/VERIFICATION.md.

  • Simulation: no blocking finding. Two independent re-implementations reproduce all 136 daily paths (the only differences are exact ties between identical rebuilt funds in 1991-93). Four more variants were run on Composer itself, on forms the gates had not covered: 99.9% to 100% of sessions agree. One labelling fault: the sleeve's "matched" ladder-return variants are in effect a different ladder. Corrected in the text.
  • Statistics: every stored number reproduces; the headline stands. Four claims were corrected before this version: the hedge paragraph (the sleeve is uncorrelated with the other two, not a hedge of them), the sleeve's significance bar (inflated by variants that rarely differ from the live rule), the exponential-average bullet (one family was missing), and the book's fall in 2011 (28%, not 17 points). Candidate edges the first draft left out were added to section 5.
  • No alternative, defensible test passes any variant in either engine.
