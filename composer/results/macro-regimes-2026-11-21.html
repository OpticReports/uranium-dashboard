Macro states as a risk-on / risk-off layer across the symphonies

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 61). Thirty macro states in nine families (rates and curve, inflation, valuation, gold, credit, dollar, volume, liquidity, oil), five cross-engine signals and three re-entry signals, all defined before any was joined to an engine path. Simulated, in sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • No macro state gives a usable risk-off switch for the book. Cutting the book to half while a state is on fails the rule set in advance for 9 of the 10 family composites that could be scored (volume shocks are too rare since 2015). The one that cleared it by the letter, liquidity (p 0.002), is in effect one state and one episode, disappears if trades fill one close later, and is not confirmed.
  • Why: on these simulated paths the book earned more, not less, in the states an overlay would call risk-off. With the S&P 500 below its 200-day average it grew at a rate of +1.48 log-points a year against +0.61 above it since 2015-06 (higher in 9 of 9 years; p about 0.006 to 0.012), and +0.53 against +0.22 on the rebuilt years. 0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year. This is the mechanism the record already names (settled questions, 2026-08-05: each engine earns most in its own stress state); the conditions below are new. De-risking there costs 0.072 a year against holding the same cash evenly.
  • Two conditions on that. 50% of those days and 68% of their growth are 2020 and 2022; without them it is +0.96 against +0.56 (p 0.089 to 0.14). And it assumes fills at the close the signal is read from: with every trade one close later, Holy Grail and the sleeve earn less below the average than above it, the rebuilt-years gap is gone (-0.04), and what remains is KMLM since 2021-06 (+1.57, mostly 2022).
  • Holy Grail's gate being off is the same state (the same reading on 98% of days), so "the other engines earn more while Holy Grail sits out" is the same finding, not a second one: +1.02 a year more since 2015-06 (p about 0.014 to 0.031), with 80% of it in 2020 and 2022 (without them +0.08 a year (p 0.83)); KMLM carries it since 2021-06, the sleeve in 2015-2020, neither in both. Moving Holy Grail's weight to them adds +0.020 a year (p 0.096) with a deeper fall.
  • Gold: a lead, with the opposite sign to the one I registered. Since 2015-06 the book grew +1.12 a year with gold strong against +0.47 without (p about 0.001). But it is +1.05 on 2015-06 to 2020-12, where a third of the book is the rebuilt KMLM fund, and +0.24 (p 0.39) since 2021-06; the rebuilt years have the other sign (-0.05), which fails the rule set in advance; one close late it is +0.23 (p 0.089 to 0.18). Not a rule, and not yet a candidate.
  • Nothing in yields and the curve, inflation, CAPE, the dollar or oil. None is distinguishable on both the recent and the rebuilt years. Credit stress and volume shocks read risk-on, which is the S&P result again.
  • Macro as a re-entry signal for Holy Grail's gate: no. None of three re-entry states picks the gate-off days worth taking.
  • By-product: the simulated returns depend on holding the new position in the session right after a trade. With every trade filled one close late, the book's growth since 2015-06 drops from +0.78 to +0.32 log-points a year and its deepest fall goes from 21% to 47% (Holy Grail +0.61 to +0.06, KMLM +1.16 to +0.65, Sleeve +0.40 to +0.07). That is a bound, and the record says it is not what live trading does: measured fills run about 15:53 and land within 3 basis points a side of the close (addendum 14b, 253 fills), on decisions read around 15:54 (addendum 50). What it does say is that a missed or late rebalance is the expensive failure, on either broker, and most of all in weak markets.

Positive growth difference means timing the cash beat spreading it evenly; p against every time-shifted copy of the state. The rule set in advance: beat the even mix on growth (or cut the deepest fall by 3 points without less growth), stand above the shifted copies after correcting for 11 states (on the better of the growth p and the fall p), agree in sign on the rebuilt years, beat the mirror, and have at least 8 episodes.

Liquidity clears it by the letter (+0.060 a year, p 0.002; 0.025 after correction). It should not count: since 2015 the composite is one state, the Fed's balance sheet smaller than six months before (1,770 of its 1,788 on-days), while before 2003 it is M2 growth alone, so "same sign on the rebuilt years" compares two different indicators; its 21 switches are two regimes, most of them flicker around a flat balance sheet in 2015-2017; 61% of its off-days and 76% of the growth earned on them sit in one stretch, 2019-10 to 2022-08; its timed book still falls 3.1 points deeper than the evenly-held one and grows +0.60 against the full book's +0.78; without 2020-2021 its gain is +0.027 a year, and the on/off difference is -0.32 (p 0.10); and one close late the on/off gap is -0.01 (p 0.93 to 0.95). I report it as one episode, which is a downgrade against the letter of the rule made after seeing the result. In its favour: tight days earned less than the other days within each of the 6 years that have both.

The dollar composite cut the deepest fall on the recent years (by 3.2 points, p 0.008; 0.083 after correction) and deepened it on the rebuilt ones. That cut is one episode: the book's deepest fall, 2018-10-30 to 2018-12-24, with the dollar state on for 100% of it.

7. Leads and candidate edges

None is actionable. Each gives its sample, its mirror or placebo, and what it would take.

  • Weak markets (S&P 500 below its 200-day average) as the book's best state. Sample: 549 days since 2015-06 in 10 regimes (8 above the off-days' rate). Sections 2 and 3. By engine on each one's own recent sample: Holy Grail +0.29 (p 0.22), KMLM +1.80 (p 0.001), Sleeve +0.74 (p 0.011). Mirror: de-risking while the S&P is ABOVE its average beats evenly-held cash by +0.067 a year. Those days also hold 38% of the book's deepest-fall days. Tested after seeing this (book at half size normally, full size below the average, 60% invested on average): since 2015 it grew +0.55 a year against +0.49 for the same cash held evenly (p <0.001) and +0.78 for the full book; deepest fall 18.8% against 13.2% and 21.4%; on the rebuilt years +0.24 a year against +0.21 for the same cash held evenly (p 0.010) and +0.30 for the full book; deepest fall 27.8% against 23.2% and 34.9%. The timing shows on the same-close paths, but the reserve costs more growth than it saves in falls: a plain book 87% invested has the same deepest fall and grows +0.69. What it would take: live decision-versus-fill prices on stressed days, since one close late most of it is gone.
  • Gold strong as a risk-on state (a lead). Since 2015-06: book +1.12 against +0.47 a year (shift p 0.001, second test 0.001; 7 regimes). One state carries it, gold beating the S&P 500 (+0.58, p 0.002; one close late +0.11); the other four are weaker. One gold regime, 2018-12 to 2020-11, holds 44% of the growth earned with gold strong; without it the gap is +0.42 (p 0.017 to 0.036). By engine on each one's own recent sample: Holy Grail +0.24 (p 0.18), KMLM +0.02 (p 0.99), Sleeve +0.36 (p 0.050); KMLM's +0.95 on 2015 on is its rebuilt years. Mirror: cutting the book to half while gold is NOT strong beat evenly-held cash by +0.073 a year (p 0.002), with a deepest fall +1.8 pts deeper; that book grew +0.66 with a deepest fall of 17.9%; a plain book 82.5% invested has the same fall and grows +0.65, so at equal deepest fall it is a wash. Against it: the sign is the opposite of the one registered, the test was chosen after seeing the map, 1994-2015 shows nothing (+0.27 vs +0.32, p 0.68), since 2021-06 alone the gap is +0.94 vs +0.70 (p 0.39), and one close late it is +0.23 (p 0.089 to 0.18). What it would take: the next two or three gold regimes, tracked from today with the definition frozen.
  • Tight liquidity as risk-off. Section 4: +0.58 against +1.14 a year (shift p 0.005; second test 0.018 to 0.021). Two regimes; one close late the gap is -0.01 (p 0.93 to 0.95). Needs another cycle.
  • Credit stress and heavy volume read risk-on: the S&P result again. Three single states have the same sign on the recent and the rebuilt years: Baa spread up more than 0.30 in a quarter: +0.78 (shift p 0.007, second test 0.082 to 0.099), rebuilt +0.48 (p 0.010), 74% of its days are below-average days, one close late +0.51 and +0.07; volume 25% above its yearly average: +1.85 (shift p <0.001, second test 0.020 to 0.056), rebuilt +0.46 (p 0.032), 78% of its days are below-average days, one close late -0.31 and +0.20; more than 60% of volume on down days: +0.70 (shift p 0.044, second test 0.15 to 0.21), rebuilt +0.78 (p 0.001), 53% of its days are below-average days, one close late +0.14 and +0.14. Volume fades one close late on both stretches and all three on the rebuilt years; the Baa state keeps its recent-years gap one close late (second test p 0.17).
  • While the sleeve's shock ladder is out of cash. The other two engines earned more on 2015-2020 (+2.03, p 0.001) and on the rebuilt years (+0.92, p <0.001), and less since 2021-06 (-0.60, p 0.40). Those are shift p only, and this state is tied to volatility. Inconsistent.
  • KMLM's switch risk-off, and two of three engine states on. The sleeve earned more the next day (+0.59 and +0.88 a year, shift p <0.001 and <0.001) on the recent years and nothing on the rebuilt ones. The sleeve's own tree reads KMLM, so this is probably its own state seen twice.
  • Volume shocks. 83 days since 2006-07 in 10 regimes; Holy Grail earned at a rate of +3.06 against +0.47 log-points a year on them (shift p 0.035 after 11; second test 0.009 to 0.012, uncorrected).   • Re-entry on a narrowing Baa spread. The ungated tree earned less, not more, on gate-off days after the spread narrowed, on both stretches (-1.21 a year, p 0.24, since 2006-07; -1.71, p 0.062, before); not distinguishable after correction.

8. What this does not settle

  • Eleven recent years hold about one of each macro regime. The study can rule states out and rank leads; it cannot confirm a macro rule.
  • Same-close fills. Engines decide on and trade at the same close in simulation, and the market states use that close too. Section 3 shows how much rests on it.
  • The recent years are in sample for all three engines. KMLM supplies 55% of the book's simulated growth since 2015, and 2020 and 2022 are the two biggest years (36% of the total); any state that lines up with those years looks good.
  • The book has no data from 2020-12-02 to 2021-06-01 (KMLM uncovered); the two sides are joined. Engine paths also skip about 25 bond-holiday sessions before 2007 and three splice days (2015-09-01, 2021-06-01, 2023-02-01).
  • Revised data. CPI, M2 and the financial conditions index are today's vintages, not what was published then. The review fetched the conditions index as first published (from 2011-05): "above zero" is on in 8 weekly readings since 2015-06 on today's file and in 2 as published. The three states built on it (two credit, one re-entry) could not have been traded as tested; none of them carries a result here. CPI is used from the 22nd of the next month; months with a late release can still be a few days early.
  • Composites change make-up through time. "At least half the states with data" means fewer states before 2003 (real yields, breakevens, the Fed's balance sheet) and before 2001 (daily gold and copper). CAPE above its 90th percentile is on nearly every day since 2015.
  • Multiplicity. Thirty single states, eleven composites, four targets, three stretches, five cross signals, three re-entry states, plus the follow-ups marked as added afterwards. The correction is within each family of composites only. Leads picked across targets (volume on Holy Grail) are corrected within one.
  • Thresholds were fixed once, not tuned, so a better-placed threshold could exist; searching for it on this sample would prove nothing.
  • Measurement basis: daily close to close, mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points per unit of turnover.
