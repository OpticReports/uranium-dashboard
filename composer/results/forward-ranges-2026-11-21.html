What the next 1, 5 and 10 years could look like, by sleeve weight

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 62). Monte Carlo of the three-symphony book at six sleeve weights under ten scenarios, plus three other methods quants use. Every scenario resamples the engines' own simulated history, which the rules were built on; none is a forecast and none is a floor.

  • Plan on a range, not on the backtest. For today's book (31.8% sleeve), median return a year over ten years: +120% if the backtest since 2015 simply repeats (a ceiling), +46% if the engines keep half their simulated edge, +21% if they keep a quarter, -2.4% if they keep none. The same haircuts on the longer, rougher 1994-on history give +24% and +11%. Half and a quarter are borrowed from studies of other people's strategies; nothing yet measures the decay of these three.
  • No scenario is a floor. If none of the edge survives, the median is a loss of about 2% a year, about 4.5 points a year behind T-bills: a 58% / 57% / 58% chance of being below the start after 1 / 5 / 10 years, and a median deepest fall of 73%.
  • Less edge means deeper falls, not only lower returns. The scenarios keep the engines' swings and cut only their edge. Median deepest fall within ten years: 21% (1-in-20: 28%) on the backtest, 32% (1-in-20: 46%) at half the edge, 45% (1-in-20: 66%) at a quarter. At a quarter of the edge a fall deeper than 30% at some point is 97% likely and deeper than 50% is 34%.
  • One year out, luck dominates. One-year return, 1-in-20 bad / median / 1-in-20 good: -12% / +40% / +185% at half the edge and -26% / +16% / +130% at a quarter. Chance of ending a year below the start: 11% to 15% at half and 29% to 34% at a quarter; after five years at a quarter of the edge it is still 11%.
  • Sleeve weight: more sleeve means less return and a smaller fall wherever an edge survives; how much it costs is not decided by the data. Median ten-year return at a 0 / 31.8 / 50% sleeve: +153% / +120% / +101% on the backtest, +54% / +46% / +40% at half the edge, +23% / +21% / +19% at a quarter; a 1-in-20 deepest fall: 40% / 28% / 22%, 62% / 46% / 39%, 80% / 66% / 59%. In points of return the sleeve costs less the lower the return (51, 14, 4 points for a 19 to 23 point cut in the 1-in-20 fall), but that follows from giving all three engines the same haircut. Where each engine's decay is measured (1994-on history, one close late) going from no sleeve to 50% gives up 39% and 37% of the return against 34% on the backtest, because the sleeve's own return shrinks most. With no edge at all the order flips: -6.0% / -2.4% / -1.4% a year, median deepest fall 83% / 73% / 67%.
  • If every trade always filled a session late the median is +38% a year with a median deepest fall of 42%; a one-session delay keeps 19% / 60% / 20% of Holy Grail's / KMLM's / the sleeve's edge. Live fills have not been there; it prices execution failure, not a plan.
  • Lowering the S&P 500 to 2% a year takes little off the 2015-on backtest (+111%), because by this simulation the engines' return is timing, not market exposure. That is a statement about the backtest, not a low-return-decade estimate: in the rebuilt 2000 to 2009 history (modelled), with the S&P at -1.7% a year, the same book made about +30% a year.
  • Two checks that rule out less than they seem to. Picking the best of 1,000 unskilled strategies would not produce this Sharpe (0.98 against 2.72); that rules out one explanation only, not rules fitted to this history or same-close fills. And the live record neither confirms nor contradicts the simulation: each symphony is nominally ahead on its own dates, but Holy Grail since it was funded is behind (+78.1% against +92.5%), and the gaps are a handful of sessions where live and simulated held different positions.

Each scenario draws 10,000 ten-year paths by picking stretches of real sessions from its source (about a month at a time, so quiet and violent spells stay together) and replaying all three engines on the same sessions, so they move together as they did. "Shifted" scenarios take the same sessions and lower each engine's average daily return by a fixed amount, leaving its swings as they were; the book's Sharpe over T-bills goes from 2.64 to 1.32 at half the edge and 0.71 at a quarter. Weights drift, and all three are reset to target at once when any engine passes its own target plus 10 points (for today's book that is POLICY's 44% cap in effect; POLICY stages the reset over two sessions). The S&P 500 compounded at +12.4% a year on the 2015-06 source. Monte Carlo error is about 0.3 points on a median and 0.5 on a probability.

The rule fixed in advance: the answer to "which sleeve weight" is whichever ordering holds in every scenario. Two orderings hold wherever an edge survives: more sleeve, lower median return; more sleeve, smaller deepest fall. One does not hold everywhere: the 1-in-20 bad five years favours less sleeve on the ceilings and at half the edge, and more sleeve once the edge is cut to a quarter or the haircut is taken off the 1994-on history; the chance of ending below the start, where it is not zero, favours more sleeve. And the price of the sleeve as a share of return depends on whose decay you assume: 25% and 16% under one haircut for all three engines, 39% on the 1994-on history, 37% one close late. Before 2015 Holy Grail / KMLM / the sleeve kept 73% / 35% / 1% of their 2015-on edge (rebuilt and modelled data, addendum 60), so a book whose sleeve decays like that pays more for it. 6. What this does not settle

  • Everything here is in sample. The rules were built on this history; the ranges are scenarios, not forecasts, and the spread inside each scenario is luck around an assumed average. How wrong the average is shows up only as the choice of scenario.
  • No scenario is a floor. "None of the edge" still assumes the engines earn T-bills on average. A negative edge is possible and is worse.
  • The haircuts are borrowed and equal across engines. The published figures come from other people's strategies; the sleeve, KMLM and Holy Grail need not decay alike (before 2015 they kept 73% / 35% / 1% of their recent edge).
  • The return is concentrated. The best 50 of 2,761 sessions carry 79% / 49% / 90% of Holy Grail's / KMLM's / the sleeve's return over T-bills since 2015-06. Without the book's own best 50 sessions the backtest is +54% a year, not +118%; 2020 alone was +540%, and without it the backtest is +95%.
  • Every draw is made of the engines' own simulated past. A regime outside it is in no path, and no draw has a day worse than the worst in its source. Leveraged and inverse funds can lose most of their value in one session.
  • Shifting the average and keeping the swings also takes the haircut off days an engine sits in cash (the sleeve is in cash on about two sessions in three). The reviewer checked that putting it on invested days only barely moves the results, and that an edge fading on its best days first made the falls smaller than shown, not larger, in the one variant tested.
  • Sources. The 2015-06 source has no sessions from 2020-12-02 to 2021-06-01 (KMLM uncovered). Real funds: Holy Grail from 2015-09, the sleeve from 2020-12, KMLM from 2021-06. Before that: rebuilt funds (Holy Grail 2006-07 to 2015-08; the KMLM fund 1991-04 to 2020-12), and shape only for Holy Grail before 2006-07 and the sleeve before 2015-06; VIX funds are modelled before 2008. The rebuilt and modelled years are weaker evidence than the real ones, not equal.
  • Not modelled: taxes, deposits and withdrawals, capacity at a larger size, a staged reset, any future change to the symphonies.
  • Same-close fills in every scenario but "one close late". Measurement basis: daily mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points on cap resets.
