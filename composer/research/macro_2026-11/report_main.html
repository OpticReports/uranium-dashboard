Macro states as a risk-on / risk-off layer across the symphonies

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 61). Thirty macro states in nine families (rates and curve, inflation, valuation, gold, credit, dollar, volume, liquidity, oil), five cross-engine signals and three re-entry signals, all defined before any was joined to an engine path. Simulated, in sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • No macro state gives a usable risk-off switch for the book. Cutting the book to half while a state is on fails the rule set in advance for {{n_fail}} of the {{n_scored}} family composites that could be scored (volume shocks are too rare since 2015). The one that cleared it by the letter, liquidity (p {{l_p1p}}), is in effect one state and one episode, disappears if trades fill one close later, and is not confirmed.
  • Why: on these simulated paths the book earned more, not less, in the states an overlay would call risk-off. With the S&P 500 below its 200-day average it grew at a rate of {{t1_on}} log-points a year against {{t1_off}} above it since 2015-06 (higher in {{t1_yrs}} years; p about {{t1_nw}}), and {{t1_on_s}} against {{t1_off_s}} on the rebuilt years. 0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year. This is the mechanism the record already names (settled questions, 2026-08-05: each engine earns most in its own stress state); the conditions below are new. De-risking there costs {{t1_p1abs}} a year against holding the same cash evenly.
  • Two conditions on that. {{t1_2y_days}} of those days and {{t1_2y_log}} of their growth are 2020 and 2022; without them it is {{t1_wo}}. And it assumes fills at the close the signal is read from: with every trade one close later, Holy Grail and the sleeve earn less below the average than above it, the rebuilt-years gap is gone ({{t1_late_s}}), and what remains is KMLM since 2021-06 ({{t1_late_k}}, mostly 2022).
  • Holy Grail's gate being off is the same state (the same reading on {{x1_same}} of days), so "the other engines earn more while Holy Grail sits out" is the same finding, not a second one: {{x1_v}} a year more since 2015-06 (p about {{x1_nw}}), with {{x1_top_share}} of it in {{x1_top}} (without them {{x1_wo}}); KMLM carries it since 2021-06, the sleeve in 2015-2020, neither in both. Moving Holy Grail's weight to them adds {{rd_dg}} a year (p {{rd_p}}) with a deeper fall.
  • Gold: a lead, with the opposite sign to the one I registered. Since 2015-06 the book grew {{g_on}} a year with gold strong against {{g_off}} without (p about {{g_nw}}). But it is {{g_e4}} on 2015-06 to 2020-12, where a third of the book is the rebuilt KMLM fund, and {{g_real_d}} (p {{g_real_p}}) since 2021-06; the rebuilt years have the other sign ({{g_shape_d}}), which fails the rule set in advance; one close late it is {{g_late}}. Not a rule, and not yet a candidate.
  • Nothing in yields and the curve, inflation, CAPE, the dollar or oil. None is distinguishable on both the recent and the rebuilt years. Credit stress and volume shocks read risk-on, which is the S&P result again.
  • Macro as a re-entry signal for Holy Grail's gate: no. None of three re-entry states picks the gate-off days worth taking.
  • By-product: the simulated returns depend on holding the new position in the session right after a trade. With every trade filled one close late, the book's growth since 2015-06 drops from {{bl_same}} to {{bl_late}} log-points a year and its deepest fall goes from {{bl_dd_same}} to {{bl_dd_late}} ({{bl_eng}}). That is a bound, and the record says it is not what live trading does: measured fills run about 15:53 and land within 3 basis points a side of the close (addendum 14b, 253 fills), on decisions read around 15:54 (addendum 50). What it does say is that a missed or late rebalance is the expensive failure, on either broker, and most of all in weak markets.

1. What was tested, and on what

Each state is yes/no every session and uses only data published before that session. A family's composite is on when at least half its states are on. Engines: the live rules' simulated daily returns from addenda 58 to 60; the book at 34.1 / 34.1 / 31.8.

{{t_design}}

Daily yields and spreads are used two sessions back (they are published after the close); market prices and volume at the prior close, like the engines' own signals. "Recent" is 2015-06 on for the book and the sleeve, 2006-07 on for Holy Grail, 2021-06 on for KMLM (its real fund); "rebuilt" is everything before, where funds or signals are rebuilt and results are shape only. From 2015-06 to 2020-12 a third of the book (KMLM) is still a rebuilt fund; only 2021-06 on is all real. The book's recent sample is eleven years with about one of each macro regime: a macro rule cannot be confirmed on it. Earlier studies rejected de-risking overlays ten times (addenda 19b to 53); this one covers indicators and book-level questions those did not.

Two p-values. The registered test compares each result with every time-shifted copy of the same state (it keeps the state's persistence). The review showed that test is too generous for states tied to volatility (the S&P below its average, Holy Grail's gate, the shock ladder, credit, volume): with equal average returns it rejects at the 5% level 11 to 22% of the time for the S&P state (5% on Holy Grail alone, whose volatility is not tied to it), 13 to 31% for Holy Grail's gate, 15 to 21% for the credit states, 13 to 38% for the volume states and 5 to 16% for the shock ladder; 6 to 9% for gold, the dollar and liquidity, where it is close to right (the reviewer's computation). So the tables also give a second p (Newey-West, 63 sessions) and the headline rows quote that one. The S&P state is a reference in the pre-registration, outside every multiple-test correction.

3. How much of the weak-market result survives

Added after the review. Each row is the growth rate with the S&P 500 below its 200-day average less the rate above it, on the study's paths and with every trade filled one close later.

{{t_robust}} {{ch_late}} {{ch_curve}}

  • Two years. 2020 and 2022 hold {{t1_2y_days}} of the below-average days and {{t1_2y_log}} of their growth; without them {{t1_wo}}. In its favour: the rate below the average beat the rate above it in {{t1_yrs}} years.
  • Concentrated, but not only a handful of days at book level: the 20 best of {{t1_n}} days are {{t1_top20}} of the growth on them; with the 10 best and 10 worst days removed from each side the gap is still +0.69, and the median day is +28 against +17 basis points (reviewer's figures). Not just volatility: growth per unit of volatility is {{t1_mv}} below against above.
  • The whole book, one close late: {{bl_same}} becomes {{bl_late}} a year and the deepest fall {{bl_dd_same}} becomes {{bl_dd_late}}; by engine {{bl_eng}}.

One close late is a stress, not a forecast of live results: it holds yesterday's positions for a whole extra session. Live, Composer reads prices around 15:54 (addendum 50) and its fills, about 15:53, have averaged within 3 basis points a side of the official close (addendum 14b, 253 fills, not split by market state). The simulated paths read and trade at the closing price; on 23 of 272 engine-sessions checked in addendum 50 the close would have decided differently from the 15:54 reading.

4. De-risking on a state against holding the same cash all the time

The book at half size while the state is on, the rest in T-bills, compared with a book that always holds the same average amount of T-bills.

{{t_p1}}

Positive growth difference means timing the cash beat spreading it evenly; p against every time-shifted copy of the state. The rule set in advance: beat the even mix on growth (or cut the deepest fall by 3 points without less growth), stand above the shifted copies after correcting for 11 states (on the better of the growth p and the fall p), agree in sign on the rebuilt years, beat the mirror, and have at least 8 episodes.

Liquidity clears it by the letter ({{l_dg}} a year, p {{l_p1p}}; {{l_p1h}} after correction). It should not count: since 2015 the composite is one state, the Fed's balance sheet smaller than six months before ({{lq_l2}} of its {{lq_on}} on-days), while before 2003 it is M2 growth alone, so "same sign on the rebuilt years" compares two different indicators; its {{lq_sw}} switches are two regimes, most of them flicker around a flat balance sheet in 2015-2017; {{lq_blk_share}} of its off-days and {{lq_blk_log}} of the growth earned on them sit in one stretch, {{lq_blk}}; its timed book still falls {{l_ddp}} points deeper than the evenly-held one and grows {{l_pol}} against the full book's {{l_full}}; without 2020-2021 its gain is {{l_wo}}; and one close late the on/off gap is {{l_late}}. I report it as one episode, which is a downgrade against the letter of the rule made after seeing the result. In its favour: tight days earned less than the other days within {{lq_within}} years that have both.

The dollar composite cut the deepest fall on the recent years ({{d_txt}}) and deepened it on the rebuilt ones. That cut is one episode: the book's deepest fall, {{d_fall}}, with the dollar state on for {{d_share}} of it.

Defined after seeing the table above. Holy Grail on gate-off days is not idle: its dip-buying rules still trade, and on the study's paths it earned {{own_on}} log-points a year on those days since 2015. So the other engines are not better places for its weight.

A tilt between engines by macro state

{{t_p2}}

While a state is on, 15 points move from the engine that did worst to the one that did best in that state's earlier days (estimated month by month on earlier data only). The gains are not specific to the state: the mirror (tilting when the state is off) gains as much, because the rule simply leans toward KMLM, the engine with the highest simulated return. With the state always on the same tilt adds {{p2_always}} a year; each state's gain is about that times its share of days. On the recent sample tilts can only begin in {{p2_first}} (five years of estimation first), so the deepest fall, in 2018, is untouched by construction.

{{t_re}}

Holy Grail's tree without its SPY gate, on days the gate is off, less T-bills (the registered comparison). On those days the ungated tree earned {{re_v}} over T-bills since 2006-07 and {{re_s}} before; the live gated tree, which still dip-buys, earned {{gt_v}} and {{gt_s}} on the same days. None is actionable. Each gives its sample, its mirror or placebo, and what it would take.

  • Weak markets (S&P 500 below its 200-day average) as the book's best state. Sample: {{t1_n}} days since 2015-06 in {{t1_reg}} regimes ({{t1_reg_up}} above the off-days' rate). Sections 2 and 3. By engine on each one's own recent sample: {{t1_eng}}. Mirror: de-risking while the S&P is ABOVE its average beats evenly-held cash by {{t1_mirror}} a year. Those days also hold {{t1_falls}} of the book's deepest-fall days. Tested after seeing this (book at half size normally, full size below the average, {{lean_exp}} invested on average): since 2015 it grew {{lean_v}}; on the rebuilt years {{lean_s}}. The timing shows on the same-close paths, but the reserve costs more growth than it saves in falls: {{lean_same}}. What it would take: live decision-versus-fill prices on stressed days, since one close late most of it is gone.
  • Gold strong as a risk-on state (a lead). Since 2015-06: book {{g_on}} against {{g_off}} a year (shift p {{g_p}}, second test {{g_nw}}; {{g_reg}} regimes). One state carries it, gold beating the S&P 500 ({{g2_txt}}); the other four are weaker. One gold regime, {{gr_span}}, holds {{gr_share}} of the growth earned with gold strong; without it the gap is {{gr_wo}}.   • Tight liquidity as risk-off. Section 4: {{l_on}} against {{l_off}} a year (shift p {{l_p}}; second test {{l_nw}}). Two regimes; one close late the gap is {{l_late}}. Needs another cycle.
  • Credit stress and heavy volume read risk-on: the S&P result again. Three single states have the same sign on the recent and the rebuilt years: {{same_txt}}. Volume fades one close late on both stretches and all three on the rebuilt years; the Baa state keeps its recent-years gap one close late (second test p 0.17).
  • While the sleeve's shock ladder is out of cash. The other two engines earned more on 2015-2020 ({{x3_e4}}) and on the rebuilt years ({{x3_s}}), and less since 2021-06 ({{x3_r}}). Those are shift p only, and this state is tied to volatility. Inconsistent.
  • KMLM's switch risk-off, and two of three engine states on. The sleeve earned more the next day ({{x25_txt}}) on the recent years and nothing on the rebuilt ones. The sleeve's own tree reads KMLM, so this is probably its own state seen twice.
  • Volume shocks. {{vol_n}} days since 2006-07 in {{vol_reg}} regimes; Holy Grail earned at a rate of {{vol_hg}} log-points a year on them (shift p {{vol_p}} after 11; second test {{vol_nw}}, uncorrected). 8. What this does not settle

  • Eleven recent years hold about one of each macro regime. The study can rule states out and rank leads; it cannot confirm a macro rule.
  • Same-close fills. Engines decide on and trade at the same close in simulation, and the market states use that close too. Section 3 shows how much rests on it.
  • The recent years are in sample for all three engines. KMLM supplies {{kmlm_share}} of the book's simulated growth since 2015, and {{top2}} are the two biggest years ({{top2_share}} of the total); any state that lines up with those years looks good.
  • The book has no data from 2020-12-02 to 2021-06-01 (KMLM uncovered); the two sides are joined. Engine paths also skip about 25 bond-holiday sessions before 2007 and three splice days (2015-09-01, 2021-06-01, 2023-02-01).
  • Revised data. CPI, M2 and the financial conditions index are today's vintages, not what was published then. The review fetched the conditions index as first published (from 2011-05): "above zero" is on in 8 weekly readings since 2015-06 on today's file and in 2 as published. The three states built on it (two credit, one re-entry) could not have been traded as tested; none of them carries a result here. CPI is used from the 22nd of the next month; months with a late release can still be a few days early.
  • Composites change make-up through time. "At least half the states with data" means fewer states before 2003 (real yields, breakevens, the Fed's balance sheet) and before 2001 (daily gold and copper). CAPE above its 90th percentile is on nearly every day since 2015.
  • Multiplicity. Thirty single states, eleven composites, four targets, three stretches, five cross signals, three re-entry states, plus the follow-ups marked as added afterwards. The correction is within each family of composites only. Leads picked across targets (volume on Holy Grail) are corrected within one.
  • Thresholds were fixed once, not tuned, so a better-placed threshold could exist; searching for it on this sample would prove nothing.
  • Measurement basis: daily close to close, mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points per unit of turnover.
