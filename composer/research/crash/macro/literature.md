# Evidence review: graded bear gates and macro conditioning for a 3x-ETF trend strategy

Scope: six questions on (a) MA / time-series-momentum (TSMOM) filters, (b) graded vs binary exposure, (c) CAPE timing, (d) yield curve / credit / oil / Sahm as conditioners, (e) volatility-managed portfolios and their critiques, (f) out-of-sample decay and multiple testing. Numbers marked **[P]** were read from the primary paper/PDF; **[S]** are from a secondary summary (CXO Advisory, Alpha Architect, blog) because the primary was paywalled or unreachable; **[Pr]** = practitioner (non-refereed) source. Nothing below is extrapolated; where a source gives no number, none is given.

## 2. Short notes by question

**Q1 (MA/TSMOM on equities).** Every serious test agrees on shape: the filter buys a large drawdown reduction (≈−85% → ≈−50% on the S&P over a century; Faber, Gayed) at the price of lagging in most years. The CAGR edge is small and not statistically significant once costs and look-ahead are handled (Zakamulin 2014/2015/2018); the Sharpe edge is real but concentrated in four or five long bears. Whipsaw cost is quantified only by practitioners (≈6% per whipsaw, 3–4 whipsaws per captured downturn; GTT) and by Zakamulin's "≈80% of exit-to-reentry intervals lose". Equities are the *weakest* trend sector (Lempérière Table 2). Applied to a 3x daily-reset ETF, the 200-day rotation still carries a −92% max DD in Gayed's simulation; it does not solve 1929–32-type sequences, and the Sharpe falls from 0.60 (unlevered) to 0.47 (3x).

**Q2 (graded vs binary).** The only refereed result showing a graded rule beating binary *after costs* is state-conditional, not distance-proportional: Goulding–Harvey–Mazzoleni blend slow and fast signals only when they disagree, with weights estimated from past post-correction/post-rebound returns and shrunk to 0.5. That exactly targets the owner's failure mode (first weeks of V-shaped rebounds = "rebound" state). A fixed average of lookbacks does worse than the single slow rule; momentum-of-specification does not help (Newfound); drawdown-depth stops only help where returns are autocorrelated (Kaminski–Lo) and the depth needed for slow models to pay is ≥20% over ≥6 months (Newfound). Continuous signals mainly lower turnover (Baltas–Kosowski). No peer-reviewed paper on sigmoid/ramped exposure vs binary was found.

**Q3 (CAPE).** Asness–Ilmanen–Maloney: CAPE-only timing has the same Sharpe as buy-and-hold over 1900–2015 and over 1958–2015, with a −32% relative drawdown and chronic under-investment as CAPE drifted up; it fights 12-month momentum (correlation −0.2). The 10-year R² (30–40%, Campbell–Shiller) collapses under Boudoukh–Israel–Richardson's effective-sample arithmetic. This is consistent with the owner's in-house finding that CAPE tilts cannot beat their inverse at ≤1-year horizons. "CAPE high for longer" has no primary evidence; the US record has ~3 episodes above 30, so duration and level cannot be separated.

**Q4 (macro conditioners).** The curve is the best *recession* predictor (4–6 quarter lead, essentially no false positives on 10y–3m inversion) but the worst *equity-exit* trigger (Fama–French: 24/24 US losses), because the lead is long and variable and equities rally after inversion. Credit spreads (GZ/EBP) forecast activity at 3–12 months but have not been tested as a trend conditioner; HY OAS is itself a trending series, so gating a price trend on it double-counts. Oil: 10/11 recessions preceded, but one 2003 false positive, a 1–15-month lead, and the 1990 spike arrived after the peak. Sahm: lags recessions by months and false-positived in 2024. The strongest refereed evidence that macro *complements* trend is Neely et al.: technical signals catch peaks, macro signals catch troughs, and the combined OOS gain is entirely inside recessions (R²_OS +11.24% in recessions, −2.80% in expansions). That maps to using macro to decide when to **loosen/re-enter** (as GTT and AQR's economic trend do), not when to tighten. AQR's macro momentum earns +13.7% in the ten worst equity quarters, but it is long–short, gross of costs and hindsight-prone by its authors' own disclaimer.

**Q5 (vol-managed).** Moreira–Muir's market result (+25% Sharpe, less risk in recessions) is exactly the direction the owner's RSI-dip buying runs against, and the critiques cut both ways: Cederburg et al. show real-time vol management loses for 72/103 strategies, Barroso–Detzel show the *market* version survives costs only in high-sentiment regimes. The part that is not contestable is arithmetic: a daily-reset 3x ETF's return is a function of realized variance (Avellaneda–Zhang; Gayed Tables 2–3 show six days at 75% vol turning +0.51% into negative 2x/3x returns). So the "earns in high-vol states" property of HG is a property of the *index* path; the 3x wrapper pays variance drag precisely then.

**Q6 (OOS decay / multiple testing).** The reference points: best-of-7,846 technical rules fell from 17.2%/yr to 2.8%/yr (p=0.32) in a 10-year holdout; published predictors lose 26% OOS and 58% post-publication; t>3 is the discovery hurdle; the long bears that give any gate its value number four or five in a century. Any gate variant tuned on 2000–02 and 2008 is fit to two events.

## 3. Implications for the owner's gate (derived strictly from the cited evidence)

1. **Binary 200-day gating works as insurance against ≥20%, ≥6-month declines and nothing else.** Its documented value sits in 4–5 episodes per century (Zakamulin; GTT; Newfound), it lost money in ≈80% of exit-to-reentry intervals and throughout 1975–1999, and on a 3x wrapper it still leaves a −92% simulated max DD (Gayed). The owner's observed 9–10%/yr cost in 2015–2026 is the normal premium, not a bug; a graded gate can change the premium, not the fact that it is paid in every V-shaped year.

2. **If graded, grade by state, not by distance or depth.** The only after-cost evidence that a graded rule beats binary is Goulding–Harvey–Mazzoleni's blend at slow/fast *disagreement* (Sharpe 0.64→0.80, worst DD −18.8%→−13.2%, break-even cost 29 bp; post-GFC 0.03→0.34). The owner already has the two legs (SPY-vs-200-day = slow; TQQQ 10>20-day = fast). The evidence says: when slow is bearish and fast turns bullish ("rebound"), re-enter with a *partial* weight estimated from past post-rebound returns and shrunk toward 0.5; when slow is bullish and fast turns bearish ("correction"), cut only partially. It does *not* support a ramp that tightens with bear duration/depth (Kaminski–Lo: depth stops only pay under autocorrelation), nor a plain average of lookbacks (worse than the single slow rule in GHM's Table E.1).

3. **The one graded rule with mechanical rather than statistical support is realized-variance scaling of the 3x sleeve.** LETF variance drag (Avellaneda–Zhang; Gayed) is arithmetic, and vol targeting is the one overlay that helps equities in every study (Harvey et al.; Moreira–Muir) while the critiques (Cederburg; Barroso–Detzel) attack its *alpha*, not its drawdown reduction. Scaling 3x exposure by target/realized vol (with a leverage cap, as Moreira–Muir's capped versions keep the Sharpe) is better supported than any CAPE/curve/oil conditioner. Note this directly conflicts with the RSI(10)<30 dip buy at full 3x size; the evidence says size the dip buy by realized vol.

4. **Macro conditioning: do not use it to tighten; at most use growth data to decide when to loosen.** CAPE adds nothing at any horizon the gate operates on (AIM Table 2/5; Boudoukh et al.; the owner's own inverse test). Curve inversion is a 24/24 loser as an equity exit (Fama–French) despite being the best recession predictor; Sahm lags; oil has a 1-in-12 false positive and a 1–15-month lead. The only refereed complementarity (Neely et al.) is that macro signals identify *troughs*; practitioner implementations (GTT's retail-sales/IP gate on MA sells; AQR economic trend added to price trend) exploit that by suppressing trend exits when growth is positive and by re-entering earlier when fundamentals turn. Credit spreads have no trend-conditioning evidence at all and double-count a price signal.

5. **Budget for degrees of freedom against five events.** Every added conditioner (CAPE level, CAPE duration, curve, OAS, oil, Sahm) is a parameter fitted to ≤5 deep bears; Sullivan et al. and McLean–Pontiff give the expected haircut (best-rule OOS collapse; −26% OOS), Harvey–Liu–Zhu and Bailey–López de Prado give the hurdle (t>3; deflated Sharpe over the number of variants tried). Pre-register one graded specification (the GHM structure with shrinkage to 0.5 and a vol cap), evaluate 2000–02 and 2008 reconstructions and 2015–2026 separately, count whipsaws and trades/yr (Gayed: 5/yr for 200-day vs 38/yr for 10-day), and report the deflated Sharpe with the trial count. Expect the in-sample improvement to shrink by a quarter to a half out of sample.
