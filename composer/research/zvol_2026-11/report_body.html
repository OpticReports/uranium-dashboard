Is the ZVOL harvester worth its capital?

Study run 2026-11-18 on data to the 11-17 close. Pre-registered, then checked by two independent reviewers. Report-only: nothing was traded or changed.

Short answer: it is not a cost centre, but it is a much more modest earner than its 2023–2025 backtest suggested, and moving its money into KMLM buys more return only by taking clearly more drawdown.
  • Live, it has made money, not lost it. Composer shows HARV at {{harv_disp}} since July. That leaves out {{harv_dist}} of fund distributions that were paid to account cash, outside the symphony. Counting them, HARV made about {{harv_true}}, against {{harv_bills}} for T-bills on the same dollars.
  • Your "dog" read is right for 2026 and wrong for the rule. Over the last 252 sessions the rule earned {{roll_last}} net, which sits at the {{roll_pct}}th percentile of its one-year results back to 2006. The 2023–2025 pace ({{real_cagr}} a year net) was the unusual stretch. Its long-run pace before ZVOL existed is about {{pre_cagr}} a year with a {{pre_dd}} worst drawdown.
  • Its timing is real. In every era back to 2006 the rule beat every placebo version of itself, and ZVOL earned 35–61 bps a day on the days the rule held it against 2–7 bps on all days. Holding the same funds without the timing earns about cash.
  • Moving HARV's 15% into the KMLM symphony is a risk trade. Live since July it would have made {{live_a}} against {{live_keep}}, with a {{live_a_dd}} drawdown against {{live_keep_dd}}. With the switcher held to its live pace, the extra return is {{a_dcagr}} a year (the range spans zero) while the worst drawdown deepens by {{a_ddd}}. By the rule fixed in advance the thesis is inconclusive, leaning against.
  • No refinement earned a recommendation. Term-structure, variance-premium, VVIX and credit variables did not predict the short-volatility return well enough to trade; only SPY's 2-day RSI, the rule's own trigger, did.

2. Why it looks worse than it is

ZVOL since launch: price against total return

Indexed to 100 on 2023-04-19. The fund has paid $0.485 a share every month since January 2024.

  • A price chart of ZVOL is wrong by design. Price is down 57% since launch; total return is up 56%. Any chart, moving average or app figure that uses price reads each monthly payout as a loss.
  • Composer's symphony figures have the same hole. The payout lands in account cash, so the symphony's own curve never gets it back. HARV's displayed return is understated by about 3 points so far and by about 4.5 more after today.
  • ZVOL held every day has been poor since 2024: +8.5%, −11.4% and +5.0% by year, after +52% in 2023. That is the fund, not the rule: the rule holds ZVOL on about one day in five.

3. Where the return comes from

{{t_legs}}
  • Timing is the whole strategy. The same three funds at their average weights, with no timing, earned {{static}} a year, below cash. The rule earned {{real_gross}} before costs. Run backwards (long volatility where it is short and the reverse) it loses {{mirror}} a year.
  • It is a rebound trade, not a carry trade. On ZVOL days, about 6 points of the 64-point total came from the futures curve rolling down; the rest came from volatility falling back after a sell-off.
  • Trading costs are large and already counted: about {{cost_drag}} a year at Composer's 5 bps, on roughly 137 times the capital traded per year. Recorded live ZVOL fills average +2.5 bps (22 fills), so the assumption is not flattering.

6. Beyond price: what predicts the short-volatility return

Eight variables named in advance, each known at the close, tested against the next 1, 5 and 21 sessions of the short mid-term position, 2008–2026. A variable "works" (✔) only if it survives a correction for running 24 tests and also forecasts out of sample.

{{t_pred}}
  • Only the rule's own trigger passes. The carry and risk-premium variables have the signs the literature predicts (steeper curve and a larger variance premium are better for the short), with t-statistics of 2 to 2.7 at one month, but none forecasts out of sample and none separates the rule's good entries from its bad ones.
  • Reading: these variables describe a slow, monthly premium. The harvester is a two-day rebound trade, so they have little to say about it. This agrees with the earlier no-builds (addenda 25, 31, 39).

Refinements named in advance

{{t_ref}}
  • SVXY in place of ZVOL wins in 2023–2026 and loses before; not robust.
  • Dropping the long-volatility leg (VXZ) trims drawdown by 4 to 8 points in all three windows for a little less return, but no range excludes zero. It is the one candidate worth keeping on the backlog.
  • A carry gate was not run: no carry variable passed the bar that would have defined it.

7. Costs and risks the backtest does not carry

  • ZVOL itself. A $20.6M fund paying out about 82% of its value a year, almost all return of capital. A payout cut, reverse split or closure within a year or two is likely. The fund also became "actively managed" in October 2025, though it still tracks −1× mid-term futures (beta 0.93–1.00 by half-year). Its prospectus warns of a total loss in a single day.
  • Tail risk is larger than 2023–2026 shows. The rule's worst day in the long path is −10.3% (February 2018) against −5.2% in the real era, and its worst drawdown 32% against 10%. The real ZVOL record has no crash deeper than −37% for the fund.
  • Distributions leak. About $1,675 (HARV) and $1,144 (KMLM) so far, plus about {{ex_harv}} and {{ex_kmlm}} today, sit in account cash unless they are swept back in.
  • The same rule is held twice, so the true harvester weight is about 19% of the book, not 15%.
  • HARV has no HYG guard, despite its name. Earlier "keep" studies (addenda 13, 16, 34) used a guarded history for it.
  • The IBKR mirror cannot buy ZVOL yet (orders held on 11-16 and 11-17), so the two brokers currently differ on exactly this fund.
  • Size. One entry is about $36k of ZVOL across both symphonies against roughly $0.7M traded a day: about 5% of volume.

My read: the data does not support moving it for "better returns" unless you are choosing to run a riskier book, and that choice is better made directly (how much switcher do you want?) than by retiring the one sleeve that is uncorrelated with it. The plumbing fixes are worth doing either way. Any change would go to both brokers in the same change set.

9. What this taught us about testing

  1. Measure total return, in dollars, at the account. Price charts and the symphony curve both misread a fund that pays out 7% a month. Every live scorecard now needs "distributions paid outside" as a line.
  2. Benchmark against the dumb version. A strategy's return means little until it is set against the same funds without timing, against a permanent position, and against cash.
  3. Test timing with placebos and a mirror. Shift the positions in time; run the rule backwards. Skill survives both; luck does not.
  4. Go outside the adoption window. The rule was chosen on 2023+ results. Twenty years of futures data showed the same edge at a third of the pace. The earlier figure was never a forecast.
  5. Decompose the return. Carry against price change told us this is a rebound trade, which is why carry variables do not help it.
  6. Name predictors in advance and correct for the count. Eight variables at three horizons give 24 chances to be fooled; three looked good until corrected and tested out of sample.
  7. Judge a sleeve by what it does to the book. Its own return is the wrong yardstick; change in book return and drawdown, with ranges, at equal risk, is the right one.
  8. Haircut any backtest that is too good. Decisions that only work at the switcher's backtest pace are bets on that backtest.
  9. Write the decision rule first. It kept this from becoming a search for the answer either of us expected.
  10. Count what the backtest leaves out: fund structure, payouts, broker friction, duplication, size.

10. Edges and open questions

  • Candidate edge (the rule's own, now measured on 20 years): after SPY's 2-day RSI closes in its lowest fifth, the short mid-term position earns +37 bps the next session against about −5 on other days (t −4.9, survives correction, positive out of sample; n 4,749). Using it elsewhere would need a cheaper or more liquid short-volatility vehicle than ZVOL.
  • Candidate, not robust: SVXY instead of ZVOL, +7.2 pts a year in 2023–2026 [+2.1, +12.4], negative before. Needs more live years.
  • Candidate, not significant: dropping the VXZ leg lowers drawdown in all windows. Revisit after another year.
  • Open: how much of the edge depends on trading at the close the signal is read from (the live record tracks the model so far; 83 sessions); what happens to the rule if ZVOL closes; whether IBKR will clear ZVOL for the mirror's orders.
