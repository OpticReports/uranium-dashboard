1. The scenarios

{{t_scen}}

Each scenario draws {{paths}} ten-year paths by picking stretches of real sessions from its source (about a month at a time, so quiet and violent spells stay together) and replaying all three engines on the same sessions, so they move together as they did. "Shifted" scenarios take the same sessions and lower each engine's average daily return by a fixed amount, leaving its swings as they were. Weights drift and are reset to target when any engine passes the largest target plus 10 points (44% today), as POLICY does. The S&P 500 grew {{spx_b}} a year on the 2015-06 source.

4. Drifting weights against a daily reset

{{t_drift}}

The earlier studies today reset the book to its target weights every session. The account does not: weights drift and POLICY resets them only at the cap. Same draws, 31.8% sleeve. This is the gap you pointed to: in the one live fall the three-symphony book has had (2026-10-28 to 11-05) it fell 14.2% against 10.7% simulated with a daily reset, with KMLM at 40% of the book.

6. What this does not settle

  • Every draw is made of the engines' own simulated past. A regime outside it is in no path: the 1970s, a change in how volatility products trade, a leveraged fund closing or being restructured.
  • No draw has a day worse than the worst in its source. Leveraged and inverse funds can lose most of their value in one session.
  • The haircuts are borrowed. The 50% and 73% figures come from studies of other people's strategies; nothing measures the decay of these three. The live record is too short to say.
  • Shifting the average and keeping the swings is a simplification: an edge that fades may fade on its best days first, which would change the shape of the falls too.
  • Not modelled: taxes, deposits and withdrawals, capacity at a larger size, any future change to the symphonies.
  • Same-close fills in every scenario but "one close late". Measurement basis: daily mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points on cap resets.
