What the next 1, 5 and 10 years could look like, by sleeve weight

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 62). Monte Carlo of the three-symphony book at six sleeve weights under seven scenarios, plus three other methods quants use. Every scenario resamples the engines' own simulated history; none is a forecast.

  • Plan on a range, not on the backtest. For today's book (31.8% sleeve), median return a year over ten years: {{ceil_10}} if the backtest since 2015 simply repeats (a ceiling), {{d_10}} if the engines keep half their simulated edge, {{e_10}} if they keep a quarter. Half and a quarter are what published studies of other strategies found between backtest and live; nothing yet measures the decay of these three.
  • The risk grows faster than the return shrinks. The scenarios keep the engines' swings and cut only their edge. Median deepest fall within ten years: {{b_dd}} on the backtest, {{d_dd}} at half the edge, {{e_dd}} at a quarter. At a quarter of the edge a fall deeper than 30% at some point is {{e_p30}} likely and deeper than 50% is {{e_p50}}.
  • One year out, luck dominates. One-year return, 1-in-20 bad / median / 1-in-20 good: {{d_1}} at half the edge and {{e_1}} at a quarter. Chance of ending a year below the start: {{d_loss1}} and {{e_loss1}}; after five years at a quarter of the edge it is still {{e_loss5}}.
  • Sleeve weight: the same trade in every scenario, but the sleeve gets cheaper as the edge shrinks. Median ten-year return at a 0 / 31.8 / 50% sleeve: {{w_b}} on the backtest, {{w_d}} at half the edge, {{w_e}} at a quarter. A 1-in-20 deepest fall at the same weights: {{w_b_dd}}, {{w_d_dd}}, {{w_e_dd}}. The less you trust the backtest, the less return the sleeve costs and the more fall it saves.

  • If every trade always filled a session late the median is {{f_10}} a year with a median deepest fall of {{f_dd}}. Live fills have not been there; it prices execution failure, not a plan.
  • Luck alone does not explain the backtest (the best of 1,000 unskilled tries would show a Sharpe near {{def_best}}, against {{def_sr}}), and the live record is ahead of the simulation so far ({{live_txt}}). Neither says how much of the edge persists.

1. The scenarios

{{t_scen}}

Each scenario draws {{paths}} ten-year paths by picking stretches of real sessions from its source (about a month at a time, so quiet and violent spells stay together) and replaying all three engines on the same sessions, so they move together as they did. "Shifted" scenarios take the same sessions and lower each engine's average daily return by a fixed amount, leaving its swings as they were. Weights drift, and all three are reset to target when any engine passes its own target plus 10 points (for today's book that is POLICY's 44% cap in effect). The S&P 500 grew {{spx_b}} a year on the 2015-06 source.

4. Drifting weights against a daily reset

{{t_drift}}

The earlier studies today reset the book to its target weights every session. The account does not: weights drift and POLICY resets them only at the cap. Same draws, 31.8% sleeve. Drift inside the cap turns out to change the deepest fall little on average, so it is not the main reason live falls ran deeper than the tables. In the one live fall the three-symphony book has had (2026-10-28 to 11-05) it fell 14.2% against 10.7% simulated: about 1.5 points of the gap were KMLM sitting at 40% of the book, and about 2 points were the engines themselves doing worse live than simulated over those six sessions (KMLM -36.3% against -32.9%).

The cap rule matters at a large sleeve

{{t_cap}}

I first registered one cap for all three engines (the largest target plus 10 points). At a 40 to 50% sleeve that lets Holy Grail or KMLM run from a 25 to 30% target up to 50 to 60% before anything resets, so the book stops being a large-sleeve book. The runs in this report give each engine its own cap; the registered rule is shown here for comparison. If the sleeve target is ever raised, POLICY's cap needs the same change.

5. Other methods quants use, and what each says here

Could the backtest be luck? (deflated Sharpe ratio)

{{t_def}}

If someone tried N strategies with no skill at all and kept the best, this is the Sharpe that best one would be expected to show on a sample this long. The simulated Sharpe is far above it at every count, so trying many variants and keeping the best does not explain these backtests. The test assumes independent, bell-shaped daily returns and says nothing about rules shaped, knowingly or not, to fit this history, which is the more likely source of decay here.

How much is just the stock market? (beta and what is left)

{{t_beta}}

Market beta explains a small part of Holy Grail's and KMLM's simulated return and none of the sleeve's (its beta is negative). So a decade of weak stock returns takes about 10 points a year off Holy Grail, 7 off KMLM and adds 4 to the sleeve: small beside the haircut scenarios. The beta is an average; these engines switch between leveraged long, inverse and cash, so their exposure on any one day is far from it.

The live record

{{t_live}}

All three are ahead of the simulation on their own dates. The sample is 94 to 238 sessions, and the rules were still being changed inside it (Holy Grail's gate went live 2026-10-18; the sleeve was assembled 2026-07), so this is not yet an out-of-sample record. It is the series that will eventually replace the borrowed haircuts.

6. What this does not settle

  • Every draw is made of the engines' own simulated past. A regime outside it is in no path: the 1970s, a change in how volatility products trade, a leveraged fund closing or being restructured.
  • No draw has a day worse than the worst in its source. Leveraged and inverse funds can lose most of their value in one session.
  • The haircuts are borrowed. The 50% and 73% figures come from studies of other people's strategies; nothing measures the decay of these three. The live record is too short to say.
  • Shifting the average and keeping the swings is a simplification: an edge that fades may fade on its best days first, which would change the shape of the falls too.
  • Not modelled: taxes, deposits and withdrawals, capacity at a larger size, any future change to the symphonies.
  • Same-close fills in every scenario but "one close late". Measurement basis: daily mark-to-market; 5.9 basis points on cap resets.
