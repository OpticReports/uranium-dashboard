The sleeve's weight: is its weak long record the data or the rule?

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 60). Follows addendum 59, which found the sleeve made about 10% a year with a 47% fall on rebuilt 1994-2020 history and did not hedge the other two engines. Questions and decision rules written before any number was computed; corrected after independent review. Simulated, in sample; nothing here is a forecast.

1. Data or rule?

The same simulated path as addendum 59, cut by how real its inputs are.

2. A few days

Checked after the fact. The ladder's best days are UVXY at 100% of its half. Which test fired differs: 2020-03-16 on a 3% rise in the high-yield bond fund; 2018-02-02 and both 2021 days on an overbought reading in a leisure-stocks fund; 2024-08-05 on the short-bond band (reviewer's trace). UVXY was a 2x fund and SVXY a -1x fund before 2018-02-28 (1.5x and -0.5x now); at today's leverage 2015-2020 is about +50% a year (reviewer).

3. Is it a hedge?

{{t_hedge}}

The sleeve's (or one half's) average daily return on the other side's worst 5% of days, against its other days; t compares the two. Against the two engines together it is never clearly positive. The inverse-fund half against Holy Grail is positive in all six stretches (t above 2 in four); through 2015 it held its inverse funds on Holy Grail's worst days about as often as on ordinary days (reviewer), so this is short exposure that happens to pay on tech-led down days, not timing.

4. The weight

Book = a third leg at weight w, the rest split equally between Holy Grail and KMLM, rebalanced daily. Control: T-bills as the third leg.

{{ch_cagr}} {{ch_dd}} {{t_weight}} {{t_test}} {{t_third}}

The test set in advance: against T-bills at the same weight, growth higher with the 90% interval above zero, or the fall smaller on at least 90% of resampled paths with growth not lower. The sleeve passes on 2015-2020 and since 2021-06 and not before; it passes just as well at 10% and at 50%, so it does not single out 31.8%. At 10% and 50%: {{vt_10_50}}. The pass survives dropping the sleeve's ten best days (reviewer: +0.06 and +0.05). Intervals differ in the third decimal between tables (separate bootstrap draws). Before 2020-12 the KMLM engine's own path is on the rebuilt KMLM fund (shape only, addendum 58).

5. Candidate edges checked on the way

  • The shock ladder alone as the third leg. Against the live sleeve at the same 31.8% the book grew {{la_e3}} more a year in 2011-2015, {{la_e4}} in 2015-2020 and {{la_real}} since 2021-06. Sample: three stretches, the ladder out of cash on about 8%, 11% and 6% of days. Mirror: on 2002-2011, where the bond funds are real but the volatility funds are models, the ladder's one-day tests fired on 172 days and made almost nothing (reviewer), so the only history that could contradict it is modelled. Against it: its rarest tests look fitted to March 2020. What it would take: paper-tracking every ladder trigger from here, and a rebuild of UVXY and SVXY for 2006-2011 from the published futures index.
  • The ladder's overbought and oversold tests (reviewer's finding, not recomputed here, no placebo run). Since 2011-11 they fired on 222 days for a summed return of +4.48 (t 5.3; the top three days are 0.88 of it); the one-day tests fired on 95 days for +4.68 (top three 1.52). On 1994-2011 the overbought and oversold tests were positive before costs (+1.26 and +1.01) where the one-day tests were not (-0.75, -0.09). The less crisis-dependent part of the ladder; it would take a placebo and a cost-inclusive run to say more.
  • Dropping the inverse-fund half. The same numbers read the other way: it cost the book growth for nine years on real funds and has paid since 2020-12 (short bonds in 2022, short biotech in 2021-22 and since). One regime in its favour; the owner's call, not a finding.

6. What this does not settle

  • Every real year is in sample for the sleeve, and so is March 2020. It was assembled in 2026-07 from two community strategies; the ladder's thresholds match that month's moves.
  • Period and data move together. Where funds are rebuilt is also the earlier market. Volatility funds are models before 2011-10, leveraged funds before 2008-2010, bond funds before 2002-07 (the high-yield fund, the ladder's first test, before 2007-04).
  • A few days decide each stretch, and one band 0.1 wide decided the best of them. Bootstrap intervals understate how uncertain a return of that shape is.
  • Fills. UVXY at the close after a shock is simulated at the closing price with 5.9 basis points of cost; the live rule decides about nine minutes before the close.
  • One seam day (2023-02-01) is missing from the sleeve's path and every book here.
