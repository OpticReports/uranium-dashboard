The sleeve's weight: is its weak long record the data or the rule?

Study of 2026-11-21 (addendum 60). Follows addendum 59, which found the sleeve made about 10% a year with a 47% fall on rebuilt 1994-2020 history and did not hedge the other two engines. Questions and decision rules written before any number was computed. Simulated, in sample; nothing here is a forecast.

  • Mostly the data. Once every fund the sleeve trades is real (2015-06 on) it made {{e4_cagr}} a year to 2020, {{e5_cagr}} to 2023-01 and {{e6_cagr}} since, with falls of {{dd_rng}}. The one input still rebuilt before 2020-12, the KMLM signal, barely matters: four versions of it give {{e4_var}} a year. The weak record sits before 2015, where the funds it trades are rebuilt.
  • But the return is a handful of days. Half of the sleeve buys UVXY (leveraged volatility) the day after a bond or credit shock. Its ten best days carry each stretch: {{top_txt}}. Without 2020-02 to 04 the sleeve's 2015-2020 return is {{e4_wo}} a year, not {{e4_cagr}}.
  • The two halves have different records. The shock ladder made money in every stretch where its inputs are real ({{fb_txt}}). The inverse-fund half (short biotech, short bonds) made money only since 2020-12 ({{ih_real}}); before that it lost or was flat, including with real funds ({{ih_before}}).
  • It is not a day-to-day hedge of Holy Grail and KMLM together in any stretch. It pays on rare shock days, when those two are often already in cash or short. The inverse-fund half has offset Holy Grail's worst days in all six stretches, at a running cost.
  • The 31.8% weight passes the test set in advance. Against T-bills at the same weight the sleeve adds {{vt_e4}} log-points a year to the book on 2015-2020 and {{vt_real}} since 2021-06 (0.10 is roughly 10% more growth a year), with a deepest fall within two points of the T-bill book's. On the rebuilt years before 2015 it adds nothing.
  • What the weight buys is a smaller fall, not growth. Any third leg, sleeve or cash, lowers the book's growth and its deepest fall. At 31.8% against none: since 2021-06 the book grows {{w0_real}} a year instead of {{w318_real}}, and its deepest fall is {{dd318_real}} instead of {{dd0_real}}. That is a risk-budget choice; the data cannot pick the number.

2. A handful of days

{{t_conc}} {{t_days}}

Checked after the fact. The ladder's best days are UVXY held at 100% of its half on the day after a shock: March 2020, February 2018, August 2024. That is the "crash convexity" the sleeve was built for; it also means a stretch without such days earns little, and that the next shock has to be caught for the record to repeat.

3. Is it a hedge?

{{t_hedge}}

The sleeve's (or one half's) average daily return on the other side's worst 5% of days, against its other days; t compares the two. A hedge would be clearly positive in the first column of each pair. Against the two engines together it never is. The inverse-fund half against Holy Grail alone is positive in all six stretches.

5. Candidate edges checked on the way

  • The shock ladder alone. {{edge_fb}} Mirror: the same half on rebuilt 1994-2011 made nothing, where its shock tests almost never fire on proxy funds, so that is no test of it. What it would take to use more of it: evidence that the ladder catches shocks it was not fitted on. It was assembled in 2026 from strategies back-tested on 2022-23; 2015-2020 is the nearest thing to that evidence, and it is one crisis (March 2020) plus February 2018.
  • The inverse-fund half as an offset to Holy Grail. On Holy Grail's worst 5% of days it made {{ih_hg}} across the six stretches (t {{ih_hg_t}}), and lost on Holy Grail's best days. Its own return was negative in four of the six. An insurance leg with a premium, and the premium has been negative only since 2020-12.

6. What this does not settle

  • Every real year is in sample for the sleeve. It was assembled in 2026-07 from two community strategies whose own backtests start in 2022-23. 2015-2020 was not used to build it, as far as the record shows, but its authors could see those years.
  • Period and data move together. The stretches where funds are rebuilt are also the earlier years; the test cannot fully separate "rebuilt data" from "an earlier market". The 2011-2015 stretch, with most funds real, was flat.
  • Ten days decide each stretch. Intervals from a block bootstrap understate how uncertain a return of that shape is.
  • UVXY at the close after a shock is simulated at the closing price with 5.9 basis points of cost; real fills on such days can be much worse.
  • The book before 2020-12 uses the rebuilt KMLM fund for both the KMLM engine and the sleeve's signal (shape only).
