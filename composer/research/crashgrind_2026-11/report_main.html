1. The rebuild

The sleeve's crash trades are in UVXY and SVXY, which launched in 2011-10. They track a published index of the first two VIX futures. I rebuilt that index from the exchange's daily settlement file for every monthly contract, added T-bills and fees, and applied each fund's leverage (2 x and -1 x before 2018-02-28, the convention of the existing history; today's 1.5 x and -0.5 x shown beside it).

{{ch_fid}} {{t_fid}} {{t_bars}}

Three daily-accuracy bars are missed, two bars (yearly growth; the sleeve's own behaviour on the rebuilt funds) are met. The miss is timing, not level: futures settle at 16:15 and the funds close at 16:00, so a late-day move lands on different days in the two series (2018-02-05 is the extreme: the index rose 96% at settlement, the fund 34% at its close). The two-day and five-day columns were added after seeing this. On the rebuilt funds the sleeve's 2020 is {{a5_2020}} log-points on the real ones, so the rebuild understates that crash payoff. VIXM and VXZ (the mid-term index) are not rebuilt.

5. What this does not settle

  • {{n_fast}} fast falls. That is enough to see the pattern is not there in most of them; it could not have confirmed a rule either way.
  • 2006 to 2011 is shape only by the bars set in advance: the rebuilt funds match the real ones' growth but not their daily timing. A fund rebuilt from its index also ignores what a real one faces in a crash (the 2018-02 event that cut these funds' leverage).
  • The readings were written knowing what 2020 looked like. That is why 2020 is left out of the test; it also means these four are not the only possible readings.
  • Real, rebuilt, modelled. Real funds: Holy Grail from 2015-09, the sleeve from 2020-12, KMLM from 2021-06. UVXY, SVXY and VIXY rebuilt from futures 2006-01 to launch; VIXM and VXZ on the old model; before 2006 nothing changes and VIX funds are a model of a market that did not exist before 2004. No sessions 2020-12-02 to 2021-06-01.
  • Same-close fills in the engine paths; the one-close-late columns are the bound. Measurement basis: daily mark-to-market.
