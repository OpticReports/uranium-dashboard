KMLM switcher: is there a better time frame?

Study of 2026-11-20. {{n_cells}} variants declared before any was run, on {{n_sess}} sessions with the real KMLM fund ({{span_b}} to {{end}}). In sample; none of this is a forecast.

  • No better time frame is supported. None of the {{n_cells}} variants passes the five tests set in advance. Only {{n_ahead}} of {{n_chall}} are ahead of the live rule at all, each by an amount well inside the noise.
  • Nearby windows cannot be told apart from 10. Windows of 9 to 12 days are within a hair of it. Shorter windows (3 to 8) are lower in both periods, but only the 3-day version is clearly worse once 74 tries are allowed for. Longer windows (14 to 60) are level with it since 2023 and lower before.
  • Every moving-average and momentum version is behind. All 20 moving-average forms (simple or exponential, 10 to 200 days) and all 6 relative-momentum forms are below the live rule in both periods. The 10 and 20-day price-against-average forms are clearly worse; the rest are worse on the numbers but not provably so.
  • No macro input could be shown to help. Six inputs named in advance (credit, financial stress, stock-bond correlation, the dollar, the yield curve, real rates): none predicts the returns of the switcher's risk-on side on 16 years of data at the bar set.
  • Recommendation: leave the switcher as it is. Five and a half years of data cannot see small improvements; section 8 says how small.

4. Before the KMLM fund existed

The KMLM fund launched in December 2020. On {{c_span}} the switch has to read an older managed-futures fund that agrees with it about 84% of the time, and SVIX and ZVOL are modelled. There the 10-day rule returned {{c_cagr}} a year with a {{c_dd}} drawdown, and the longer windows ran ahead of it on the numbers.

The longer windows average {{c_long}} log-points a year ahead there (t {{c_long_t}}); the best single one has a family-wise p of {{c_best_p}}, and ten sessions carry {{c_top10}} of the gap. The {{c_ahead}} of {{c_n}} variants ahead are one pattern counted many times, not that many confirmations. It does not repeat with the real fund. So: no sign that 10 is special on the stand-in years, and no case for another window either.

5. Macro

Each input was first asked a simple question on {{mac_span}}: when its state is favourable, does the risk-on basket (TECL and SOXL) do better over the next 1, 5 and 21 sessions? Only an input that passed would have been built into the tree. Inputs that pass: {{mac_works}}.

{{ch_macro}} {{t_mac}}

None is detectable. The bar was high: with 36 tests an input needed to move the basket by roughly 45 to 75 basis points a day to pass, against an average day of 23. So this says no large macro effect exists, not that none does. At 21 sessions {{mac_neg}} of the six lean against a confirmation filter (the basket did better after the unfavourable state), none significantly.

Point in time: each state uses data from the session before (two for the stress index, six for the weekly dollar index). The stress and FRED series are today's vintage; the stress index is revised after publication. Not re-run because earlier studies answered them: VIX term structure (addenda 2, 39), rate-hike vetoes (33), CAPE (26, 40), volatility gates on the long leg (42, 54.1). Because no input passed, none was built into the tree.

8. What this does not settle

  • Small improvements are invisible. To stand out from 74 tries half the time, a neighbouring window would have had to add {{mde_near}} log-points a year (the 12-day cell: {{mde_best}}, roughly 80 points of yearly return on the live rule's {{live_cagr}}); the typical variant {{mde_med}}. A real gain a third that size could be there unseen. The reading is "no evidence for a change", not "proof that 10 is best".
  • The earlier period flatters the live rule. Its author fitted it on 2022, and single sessions there carry much of the gap (2022-11-10: the live rule +20% in a day).
  • Decisions are simulated at the close. Composer decides a few minutes earlier; 8 of 83 live KMLM sessions were knife-edges.
  • What would change the answer: another two or three years of live data, or a managed-futures signal with a longer real history than the KMLM fund.

9. Method notes for next time

  • Declaring the 74 variants first mattered: the scouting pass had shown longer windows ahead since 2023, and the period nobody had seen did not agree.
  • Checking the simulator against Composer's own engine on three variants, not only the live rule, is what makes the moving-average results trustworthy.
  • State the smallest effect a test can see next to its verdict. "Nothing passed" means little without it.
  • A macro input has to predict returns on its own, on long history, before it goes near the tree; and the bar for that should be set with the sample size in view.
