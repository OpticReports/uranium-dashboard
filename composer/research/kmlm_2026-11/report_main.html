KMLM switcher: is there a better time frame?

Study of 2026-11-20. {{n_cells}} variants declared before any was run, on {{n_sess}} sessions with the real KMLM fund ({{span_b}} to {{end}}). In sample; none of this is a forecast.

  • No better time frame was found. None of the {{n_cells}} variants passes the five tests set in advance. Only {{n_ahead}} of {{n_chall}} are even ahead of the live rule, all by amounts well inside the noise.
  • The 10-day window is not a fluke setting. Windows of 9 to 12 days behave the same. Shorter windows (3 to 8) are clearly worse. Longer ones (14 to 60) look better in one half of the data and worse in the other.
  • Every moving-average version is worse. Simple or exponential, price-above-average or crossover, 10 to 200 days: all 20 lag the live rule, as do all 6 relative-momentum versions.
  • No macro input earns a place. Six inputs named in advance (credit, financial stress, stock-bond correlation, the dollar, the yield curve, real rates): none predicts the returns of the switcher's risk-on side on 16 years of data.
  • Recommendation: leave the switcher as it is. What this test cannot rule out, and what would change the answer, is at the end.

1. The window

The switch compares the 10-day RSI of XLK with the 10-day RSI of the KMLM fund. This is the same comparison at fifteen windows, everything else unchanged. The two periods are drawn separately because the earlier one (2021-06 to 2023-04) was not looked at before the test.

{{ch_surface}}

From 9 to 12 days the three lines sit on zero. Below 9 the rule flips more often (up to two and a half times as often at 3 days) and loses in both periods. Above 12 the periods disagree: ahead since 2023, behind before it. A setting that only works in one period is not a better time frame.

{{t_win}}

Return a year is the simulated symphony on both periods pooled (the live rule: {{live_cagr}} a year, {{live_dd}} max drawdown; {{live_b}} in the first period, which contains 2022, the year its author fitted it on, and {{live_a}} since). "Family-wise p" is the chance of a t that large among {{n_cells}} tries if none were better. "Knife-edge days": days the flip would differ with XLK 0.3% higher or lower.

4. Before the KMLM fund existed

The KMLM fund launched in December 2020. On {{c_span}} the switch has to read an older managed-futures fund that agrees with it about 84% of the time, and SVIX and ZVOL are modelled. There the 10-day rule returned {{c_cagr}} a year with a {{c_dd}} drawdown, and {{c_ahead}} of {{c_n}} variants were ahead of it, the longer windows most.

{{ch_surface_c}}

This cannot pick a window: it is a different signal. What it does say is that nothing about "10" is special outside the fund's own five years. Put with section 1: the window barely matters; the fund the switch reads matters a great deal.

5. Macro

Each input was first asked a simple question on {{mac_span}}: when its state is favourable, does the risk-on basket (TECL and SOXL) do better over the next 1, 5 and 21 sessions? Only an input that passed would have been built into the tree. Inputs that work: {{mac_works}}.

{{ch_macro}} {{t_mac}}

Five of the six point the wrong way for a confirmation filter: the risk-on side did better after the unfavourable state. The largest is financial stress: {{stress_off}} over the next 21 sessions when stress was elevated against {{stress_on}} otherwise (t {{stress_t}}, placebo p {{stress_mp}}). That is the dip-buying the symphony already does with price, and it is not significant. A macro veto would have removed the entries that paid best.

Not re-run because earlier studies answered them: VIX term structure (addenda 2, 39), rate-hike vetoes (33), CAPE (26, 40), volatility gates on the long leg (42, 54.1).

7. Edges looked for on the way

  • Elevated financial stress, then leveraged tech. Risk-on basket {{stress_off}} over 21 sessions after stress vs {{stress_on}} otherwise, 2010-2026, t {{stress_t}}, placebo p {{stress_mp}}. Not an edge by the bar set (Holm p 1.0); the symphony's own dip-buys already trade it. To use it would need more independent episodes than 16 years hold.
  • Longer windows on the stand-in years. 25 to 30-day windows ran about 0.10 to 0.14 log-points a year ahead on 2007-2020, but they are behind on the first real period and the signal there is a different fund. Not usable.
  • Nothing else cleared a placebo.

8. What this does not settle

  • Small improvements are invisible. With five and a half years of data, a variant would need to add roughly 0.5 log-points a year to stand out from 74 tries. A real improvement a third that size could be there and this test would not see it. The honest reading is "no evidence for a change", not "proof the 10-day rule is best".
  • The first period flatters the live rule. Its author fitted it on 2022. A challenger had to win on the incumbent's home ground.
  • Decisions are simulated at the close. Composer decides a few minutes earlier; 8 of 83 live KMLM sessions were knife-edges.
  • What would change the answer: another two or three years of live data, or a managed-futures signal with a longer real history than the KMLM fund.

9. Method notes for next time

  • Declaring the 74 variants first is what kept "longer is better since 2023" from becoming a finding: the unseen period reversed it.
  • Checking the simulator against Composer's own engine on three variants, not only the live rule, is what makes the moving-average results trustworthy.
  • A macro input has to predict returns on its own, on long history, before it goes near the tree.
